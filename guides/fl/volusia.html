How to buy tax-sale property in Volusia County, FL

What the Volusia County offices publish about buying tax-sale property: who runs each sale or list, when and where, and what a buyer must do. Every answer below was read on an official page and links to it. Questions not yet confirmed are listed as such.

Auctions

A clerk's tax deed sale: the property itself is sold to the highest bidder at a scheduled public auction.

Which office handles it and how do I contact them?

The Clerk of the Circuit Court's tax deed contact at the Volusia County Courthouse in DeLand is (386) 736-5919.

“Contact Information Volusia County Courthouse - DeLand (386) 736-5919”

Source: Tax Deeds - Clerk of the Circuit Court, Volusia County Florida · verified 2026-11-02

How does a tax sale work here? Statewide

Property on which a tax deed application has been filed is sold at public auction to the highest bidder by the clerk of the circuit court of the county where the property is located.

“Real property advertised for sale to the highest bidder as a result of an application filed under s. 197.502 shall be sold at public auction by the clerk of the circuit court, or his or her deputy, of the county where the property is located”

Source: The 2026 Florida Statutes, section 197.542 (Online Sunshine) · F.S. 197.542(1) · verified 2026-11-02

When are sales held?

Sales are held online at 9:00 AM ET; sale dates are on the Clerk's Tax Deed Inquiry listing. Items are auctioned in order of certificate number.

“Tax Deed sales are held online at www.volusia.realtaxdeed.com at 9:00 AM ET. Check the Tax Deed Inquiry listing for sale dates. At the date and time specified for the sale, each item is auctioned in order of certificate number and sold to the highest bidder.”

Source: Tax Deeds - Clerk of the Circuit Court, Volusia County Florida · verified 2026-11-02

Where do I bid - online or in person?

Sales are held online at www.volusia.realtaxdeed.com.

“Tax Deed sales are held online at www.volusia.realtaxdeed.com at 9:00 AM ET.”

Source: Tax Deeds - Clerk of the Circuit Court, Volusia County Florida · verified 2026-11-02

How do I register to bid?

Bidders must register at www.volusia.realtaxdeed.com to get a username and password, and make a deposit for each property they want to bid on.

“In order to participate in the sale, bidders must register at www.volusia.realtaxdeed.com to obtain a Username and Password. A valid deposit of $200.00 or 5% of the winning bid, whichever is greater must be made for each property they would like to bid on.”

Source: Tax Deeds - Clerk of the Circuit Court, Volusia County Florida · verified 2026-11-02

Is a deposit required and how much?

A non-refundable deposit of 5% of the final bid or $200.00, whichever is greater, is required for each property. ACH deposits are due 4 full working days before the sale; wire transfers by 2:00 PM ET on the business day before the sale.

“Bidders must remit their ACH deposit no later than 4 full working days prior to the commencement of a sale or by 2:00 PM ET on the business day before the sale when using wire transfer. … For each piece of property purchased, the successful high bidder is required to pay a non-refundable deposit equal to 5 percent of the final bid or $200.00, whichever is greater, at the time of the sale.”

Source: Tax Deeds - Clerk of the Circuit Court, Volusia County Florida · verified 2026-11-02

What makes up the opening or minimum bid? Statewide

On county-held certificates on nonhomestead property, the opening bid is the value of all outstanding certificates plus omitted years' taxes, delinquent taxes, current taxes if due, interest, and all costs and fees paid by the county. On property assessed as homestead on the latest roll it also includes one-half of the latest assessed value.

“On county-held certificates on nonhomestead property shall be the sum of the value of all outstanding certificates against the property, plus omitted years' taxes, delinquent taxes, current taxes, if due, interest, and all costs and fees paid by the county. ... an amount equal to one-half of the latest assessed value of the homestead.”

Source: The 2026 Florida Statutes, section 197.502 (Online Sunshine) · F.S. 197.502(6)(a), (6)(c) · verified 2026-11-02

When and how must the winning bidder pay?

The balance of the high bid, with the recording fee, documentary stamps and a deposit registry fee, must be received within 24 hours of the sale; a wire transfer no later than 12:00 PM ET on the next business day. Cash is not accepted.

“payment of the balance of the high bid, along with the recording fee, state documentary stamps of $.70 per $100.00 of the transfer amount, and a deposit registry fee must be received by the Clerk's office within 24 hours of the sale. … Wire transfer must be received no later than 12:00 PM ET on the next business day after the sale. … the Clerk's Office will no longer accept cash as a means of bidder account deposit, bids or final winning bid payment.”

Source: Tax Deeds - Clerk of the Circuit Court, Volusia County Florida · verified 2026-11-02

What costs are added on top of the winning bid?

The recording fee, state documentary stamps of $.70 per $100.00 of the transfer amount, and a deposit registry fee are paid with the balance of the bid.

“payment of the balance of the high bid, along with the recording fee, state documentary stamps of $.70 per $100.00 of the transfer amount, and a deposit registry fee must be received by the Clerk's office within 24 hours of the sale.”

Source: Tax Deeds - Clerk of the Circuit Court, Volusia County Florida · verified 2026-11-02

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive? Statewide

The buyer receives a tax deed issued in the name of the county and signed by the clerk. The deed is prima facie evidence that all proceedings were regular, and the grantee is entitled to immediate possession.

“All tax deeds shall be issued in the name of a county and shall be signed by the clerk of the county. ... All deeds issued pursuant to this section shall be prima facie evidence of the regularity of all proceedings from the valuation of the lands to the issuance of the deed, inclusive.”

Source: The 2026 Florida Statutes, section 197.552 (Online Sunshine) · F.S. 197.552; F.S. 197.562 · verified 2026-11-02

Can the former owner redeem after the sale? Statewide

A certificate can be redeemed at any time before a tax deed is issued, unless full payment for the tax deed, including documentary stamps and recording fees, has been made to the clerk.

“A person may redeem a tax certificate at any time after the certificate is issued and before a tax deed is issued unless full payment for a tax deed is made to the clerk of the court, including documentary stamps and recording fees.”

Source: The 2026 Florida Statutes, section 197.472 (Online Sunshine) · F.S. 197.472(1) · verified 2026-11-02

Which liens or interests survive the sale? Statewide

Except as the chapter provides, no right, interest, restriction or covenant survives the tax deed, but a lien of record held by a municipal or county governmental unit, special district or community development district that is not satisfied from the sale proceeds survives.

“Except as specifically provided in this chapter, no right, interest, restriction, or other covenant shall survive the issuance of a tax deed, except that a lien of record held by a municipal or county governmental unit, special district, or community development district, when such lien is not satisfied as of the disbursement of proceeds of sale under the provisions of s. 197.582 ... shall survive the issuance of a tax deed.”

Source: The 2026 Florida Statutes, section 197.552 (Online Sunshine) · F.S. 197.552 · verified 2026-11-02

Is the title insurable or does it need a quiet-title action? Statewide

Under s. 197.552 a tax deed is prima facie evidence of the regularity of all proceedings from the valuation of the lands to the issuance of the deed.

“All deeds issued pursuant to this section shall be prima facie evidence of the regularity of all proceedings from the valuation of the lands to the issuance of the deed, inclusive.”

Source: The 2026 Florida Statutes, section 197.552 (Online Sunshine) · F.S. 197.552 · verified 2026-11-02

What if the property is occupied? Statewide

The grantee of a tax deed is entitled to immediate possession of the land. If a demand for possession is refused, the purchaser may apply to the circuit court for a writ of assistance upon 5 days' notice, and if the court finds for the applicant it orders the sheriff to put the grantee in possession.

“Any person, firm, corporation, or county that is the grantee of any tax deed under this law shall be entitled to the immediate possession of the lands described in the deed. If a demand for possession is refused, the purchaser may apply to the circuit court for a writ of assistance upon 5 days’ notice directed to the person refusing to deliver possession. ... If the court finds for the applicant, an order shall be issued by the court directing the sheriff to put the grantee in possession of the lands.”

Source: Florida Statutes 197.562 - Grantee of tax deed entitled to immediate possession · F.S. 197.562 · verified 2026-11-02

What happens to property that does not sell? Statewide

If there are no higher bids the property is struck off to the certificateholder. If there are no bidders and the certificateholder does not pay resale costs or the amounts due for the deed within 30 days after the sale, the clerk puts the land on the list of lands available for taxes.

“On county-held or individually held certificates for which there are no bidders at the public sale and for which the certificateholder fails to timely pay costs of resale or fails to pay the amounts due for issuance of a tax deed within 30 days after the sale, the clerk shall enter the land on a list entitled "lands available for taxes"”

Source: The 2026 Florida Statutes, section 197.502 (Online Sunshine) · F.S. 197.502(7); F.S. 197.542(1) · verified 2026-11-02

What happens to money paid above what was owed (surplus)? Statewide

If the property sells for more than the certificateholder's statutory bid, the clerk pays the surplus first to governmental units for liens of record they hold, then keeps any balance for the benefit of persons described in s. 197.522(1)(a). A claimant has 120 days from the date of the notice to file a written claim with the clerk for the surplus proceeds.

“If the property is purchased for an amount in excess of the statutory bid of the certificateholder, the surplus must be paid over and disbursed by the clerk ... The clerk shall distribute the surplus to the governmental units for the payment of any lien of record held by a governmental unit against the property ... If there remains a balance of undistributed funds, the balance must be retained by the clerk for the benefit of persons described in s. 197.522 (1)(a) ... has 120 days from the date of the notice to file a written claim with the clerk for the surplus proceeds.”

Source: Florida Statutes 197.582 - Disbursement of proceeds of sale · F.S. 197.582(2)(a), (3) · Clerk of the circuit court · verified 2026-11-02

Available

Property that failed to sell at a tax deed sale, purchasable from the clerk without an auction (Lands Available for Taxes).

Which office handles it and how do I contact them?

For the current purchase price, call the Volusia County Clerk's Tax Deed Department at (386) 736-5919.

“For the current purchase price, call the Tax Deed Department at (386) 736-5919.”

Source: List of Lands Available for Taxes (Volusia County Clerk list document) · verified 2026-11-02

What is this list and why is the property available? Statewide

These are properties offered at a tax deed sale that drew no bidders, where the certificateholder did not pay resale costs or the amounts due for the deed within 30 days. The clerk enters them on the list of lands available for taxes and notifies the county commission.

“On county-held or individually held certificates for which there are no bidders at the public sale and for which the certificateholder fails to timely pay costs of resale or fails to pay the amounts due for issuance of a tax deed within 30 days after the sale, the clerk shall enter the land on a list entitled "lands available for taxes" and shall immediately notify the county commission that the property is available.”

Source: The 2026 Florida Statutes, section 197.502 (Online Sunshine) · F.S. 197.502(7) · verified 2026-11-02

How do I buy a property from this list? Statewide

For the first 90 days only the county may buy the land (or waive its right). After that, any person may purchase the property from the clerk, without further notice or advertising, for the opening bid.

“During the first 90 days after the property is placed on the list, the county may purchase the land for the opening bid or may waive its rights to purchase the property. Thereafter, any person, the county, or any other governmental unit may purchase the property from the clerk, without further notice or advertising, for the opening bid”

Source: The 2026 Florida Statutes, section 197.502 (Online Sunshine) · F.S. 197.502(7) · verified 2026-11-02

How is the price set?

The amount on the list is the original opening bid and does not include accrued taxes, interest and other costs; call the Tax Deed Department for the current purchase price.

“*The dollar amount shown is the original opening bid, it does not include accrued taxes, interest, and other costs. For the current purchase price, call the Tax Deed Department at (386) 736-5919.”

Source: List of Lands Available for Taxes (Volusia County Clerk list document) · verified 2026-11-02

What costs are added to the price? Statewide

The statute adds accrued interest on the opening bid through the month of sale and the omitted years' taxes to the required minimum bid. County clerks list their own additional charges in the county rows.

“Interest on the opening bid continues to accrue through the month of sale as prescribed by s. 197.542 . (8) Taxes may not be extended against parcels listed as lands available for taxes, but in each year the taxes that would have been due shall be treated as omitted years and added to the required minimum bid.”

Source: The 2026 Florida Statutes, section 197.502 (Online Sunshine) · F.S. 197.502(7), (8) · verified 2026-11-02

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive? Statewide

All tax deeds are issued in the name of the county and signed by the clerk, and tax deeds issued to a purchaser of land for delinquent taxes are in the form prescribed by the Department of Revenue.

“All tax deeds shall be issued in the name of a county and shall be signed by the clerk of the county. ... Tax deeds issued to a purchaser of land for delinquent taxes shall be in the form prescribed by the department.”

Source: The 2026 Florida Statutes, section 197.552 (Online Sunshine) · F.S. 197.552 · verified 2026-11-02

How long does a property stay available? Statewide

Three years after the day the land was offered for public sale, unsold land escheats to the county free and clear, and the clerk executes an escheatment tax deed to the board of county commissioners.

“Three years after the day the land was offered for public sale, the land shall escheat to the county in which it is located, free and clear. All tax certificates, accrued taxes, and liens of any nature against the property shall be deemed canceled as a matter of law”

Source: The 2026 Florida Statutes, section 197.502 (Online Sunshine) · F.S. 197.502(8) · verified 2026-11-02

Which liens or interests stay with the property? Statewide

Except as the chapter provides, no right, interest, restriction or covenant survives the issuance of a tax deed. A lien of record held by a municipal or county governmental unit, special district, or community development district that is not satisfied when the sale proceeds are disbursed survives the tax deed.

“Except as specifically provided in this chapter, no right, interest, restriction, or other covenant shall survive the issuance of a tax deed, except that a lien of record held by a municipal or county governmental unit, special district, or community development district, when such lien is not satisfied as of the disbursement of proceeds of sale under the provisions of s. 197.582 , shall survive the issuance of a tax deed.”

Source: The 2026 Florida Statutes, section 197.552 (Online Sunshine) · F.S. 197.552 · verified 2026-11-02

Liens & Certificates

A tax certificate: a lien on the property for unpaid taxes, bought by assignment from the county. It is not ownership of the land.

What is a tax lien or certificate here? Statewide

A tax certificate is a paper or electronic legal document representing unpaid delinquent real property taxes, non-ad valorem assessments, interest and related costs and charges, issued against a specific parcel of real property and becoming a first lien on it, superior to all other liens except as provided by s. 197.573(2).

““Tax certificate” means a paper or electronic legal document, representing unpaid delinquent real property taxes, non-ad valorem assessments, including special assessments, interest, and related costs and charges, issued in accordance with this chapter against a specific parcel of real property and becoming a first lien thereon, superior to all other liens, except as provided by s. 197.573 (2).”

Source: The 2026 Florida Statutes, section 197.102 (Online Sunshine) · F.S. 197.102(1)(f); F.S. 197.573(2) · verified 2026-11-02

How do I buy one (auction / over the counter / assignment)?

Volusia County certificate sales are held over the internet with the tax sale vendor LienHub; the cost of each certificate is listed beside its parcel in the delinquent advertisement.

“In Volusia County, tax certificate sales are currently held over the internet with our tax sale vendor - LienHub. ... The cost to purchase a certificate is listed beside each parcel in the delinquent advertisement.”

Source: Tax Collector - Volusia | Taxes | Tax Certificates · Volusia County Tax Collector · verified 2026-11-02

What interest or penalty does the holder earn? Statewide

The maximum rate of interest on a tax certificate is 18 percent per year, and interest is calculated from the first day of each month.

“The maximum rate of interest on a tax certificate is 18 percent per year. ... Interest shall be calculated from the first day of each month.”

Source: The 2026 Florida Statutes, section 197.172 (Online Sunshine) · F.S. 197.172(2), (4) · verified 2026-11-02

How long does the owner have to redeem? Statewide

A certificate may be redeemed at any time after it is issued and before a tax deed is issued, unless full payment for the tax deed, including documentary stamps and recording fees, has been made to the clerk. The redeemer pays the tax collector the face amount plus all interest, costs and charges.

“A person may redeem a tax certificate at any time after the certificate is issued and before a tax deed is issued unless full payment for a tax deed is made to the clerk of the court, including documentary stamps and recording fees. The person redeeming a tax certificate shall pay the tax collector the face amount plus all interest, costs, and charges.”

Source: The 2026 Florida Statutes, section 197.472 (Online Sunshine) · F.S. 197.472(1) · verified 2026-11-02

How does a holder get the property if it is not redeemed? Statewide

After 2 years have passed since April 1 of the year the certificate was issued, and before it is canceled, the holder may file the certificate and a tax deed application with the county tax collector. The property is then sold at public auction by the clerk.

“The holder of a tax certificate at any time after 2 years have elapsed since April 1 of the year of issuance of the tax certificate and before the cancellation of the certificate, may file the certificate and an application for a tax deed with the tax collector of the county where the property described in the certificate is located.”

Source: The 2026 Florida Statutes, section 197.502 (Online Sunshine) · F.S. 197.502(1) · verified 2026-11-02

When does a certificate or lien expire? Statewide

Seven years after the certificate's issuance date (the first day of the advertised certificate sale), if no tax deed has been applied for and no other administrative or legal proceeding, including a bankruptcy, has existed of record, the certificate is null and void and is canceled.

“Seven years after the date of issuance of a tax certificate, which is the date of the first day of the tax certificate sale as advertised under s. 197.432 , if a tax deed has not been applied for, and no other administrative or legal proceeding, including a bankruptcy, has existed of record, the tax certificate is null and void and shall be canceled.”

Source: The 2026 Florida Statutes, section 197.482 (Online Sunshine) · F.S. 197.482(1) · verified 2026-11-02

What fees does a buyer pay?

The certificate cost includes the gross tax, a 3% fee, advertising cost, and the cost of the certificate sale. Recording an assignment carries a $2.25 transfer fee.

“This cost includes gross tax, 3% fee, advertising cost, and the cost of the tax certificate sale. ... There is a $2.25 transfer fee for recording each assignment in the official records.”

Source: Tax Collector - Volusia | Taxes | Tax Certificates · Volusia County Tax Collector · verified 2026-11-02

What risks does the law or the county point out (worthless parcels / bankruptcy / expiry)?

A certificate is an investment and not a deed, and it does not convey title. Holders may not contact owners to demand payment until two years after April 1 of the year of issuance and can be barred from bidding for doing so; void or corrected certificates earn 8% or the bid rate, whichever is less.

“It does not convey title to the land. Tax certificates are investments and not a deed to the property. ... certificate holders are prohibited from contacting property owners to encourage or demand payment until two years after April 1 of the year of issuance of the tax certificate. A certificate holder can be barred from bidding at our tax certificate sale for initiating such contact. ... Refunds on void or corrected tax certificates will earn interest at the rate of 8% or the rate of interest bid at the tax certificate sale, whichever is less.”

Source: Tax Collector - Volusia | Taxes | Tax Certificates · Volusia County Tax Collector · verified 2026-11-02

Can certificates be bought from the county or another holder (assignment)?

County-held certificates can be bought in the DeLand office or on LienHub. Certificates may be sold or assigned to another party in the DeLand office, with a $2.25 transfer fee, and the new owner must register with the certificate vendor.

“Currently, county-held tax certificates are available for purchase in our DeLand office or on LienHub . ... Tax certificates may be sold and/or assigned to another party. Currently these may be transferred in our DeLand office. There is a $2.25 transfer fee for recording each assignment in the official records. In addition, the new owner will need to be registered with our tax certificate vendor.”

Source: Tax Collector - Volusia | Taxes | Tax Certificates · Volusia County Tax Collector · verified 2026-11-02
