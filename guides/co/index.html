Auctions

Shown here: Saguache's and (for the dated sale cycle) Douglas's tax lien sale lists - sales of liens, not the land - and Morgan's auctions of an option for a Treasurer's deed.

How does a tax sale work here? Statewide

After a tax lien has been held long enough, the lawful holder of the certificate of purchase may apply for a treasurer's deed; the county treasurer then holds a public auction, and the winning bidder receives a certificate of option for a treasurer's deed.

“AFTER AUGUST 1 OF THE THIRD CALENDAR YEAR FOLLOWING THE DATE OF A TAX LIEN SALE, A LAWFUL HOLDER MAY APPLY FOR A TREASURER'S DEED FOR THE PROPERTY DESCRIBED IN THE CERTIFICATE OF PURCHASE ... "CERTIFICATE OF OPTION FOR A TREASURER'S DEED" MEANS A CERTIFICATE ISSUED BY A TREASURER TO THE SUCCESSFUL PURCHASER AT THE PUBLIC AUCTION”

Source: Session Laws of Colorado 2026, Ch. 135 (Senate Bill 26-144) · C.R.S. 39-11.5-102(1); 39-11.5-101(3) · verified 2026-11-02

When are sales held? Statewide

The first public auction must be scheduled no less than thirty and no more than sixty calendar days after the treasurer mails the notice of public auction.

“THE INITIAL PUBLIC AUCTION HELD PURSUANT TO THIS ARTICLE 11.5 MUST BE SCHEDULED FOR NO LESS THAN THIRTY CALENDAR DAYS NOR MORE THAN SIXTY CALENDAR DAYS AFTER THE DATE OF MAILING THE NOTICE OF PUBLIC AUCTION.”

Source: Session Laws of Colorado 2026, Ch. 135 (Senate Bill 26-144) · C.R.S. 39-11.5-109(1) · verified 2026-11-02

Where do I bid - online or in person? Statewide

The treasurer holds the public auction at a courthouse or at a building housing the county clerk and recorder or the treasurer, as named in the notice; it may also be held on the internet or another electronic medium.

“THE TREASURER SHALL CONDUCT THE PUBLIC AUCTION AT ANY DOOR OR ENTRANCE TO, OR IN ANY ROOM IN ANY BUILDING TEMPORARILY OR PERMANENTLY USED AS, A COURTHOUSE OR AT OR WITHIN ANY BUILDING WHERE THE OFFICE OF THE COUNTY CLERK AND RECORDER OR THE OFFICE OF THE TREASURER IS LOCATED, WHICH PLACE MUST BE SPECIFICALLY DESIGNATED IN THE NOTICE OF PUBLIC AUCTION; EXCEPT THAT A PUBLIC AUCTION MAY ALSO BE CONDUCTED BY MEANS OF THE INTERNET OR OTHER ELECTRONIC MEDIUM.”

Source: Session Laws of Colorado 2026, Ch. 135 (Senate Bill 26-144) · C.R.S. 39-11.5-110(1)(a) · verified 2026-11-02

How do I register to bid? Statewide

The statutory notice of public auction tells interested bidders that they must register and gives the treasurer's website or office phone number for more information.

“INTERESTED BIDDERS MUST REGISTER. MORE INFORMATION AT (WEBSITE OR TREASURER'S OFFICE PHONE NUMBER).”

Source: Session Laws of Colorado 2026, Ch. 135 (Senate Bill 26-144) · C.R.S. 39-11.5-105(4)(d) · verified 2026-11-02

Is a deposit required and how much? Statewide

The treasurer may require the lawful holder who applies for a treasurer's deed to deposit up to one thousand dollars plus the permitted fees when the application is accepted, applied against the treasurer's fees and costs. Bidding rules for the public auction are posted on the treasurer's website or the auction medium at least fourteen calendar days before the auction.

“THE TREASURER MAY REQUIRE THE LAWFUL HOLDER TO MAKE A DEPOSIT OF UP TO ONE THOUSAND DOLLARS PLUS THE AMOUNT OF FEES PERMITTED PURSUANT TO SECTION 30-1-102 (1.7), AT THE TIME THE APPLICATION FOR TREASURER'S DEED IS ACCEPTED, TO BE APPLIED AGAINST THE FEES AND COSTS OF THE TREASURER IN PROCEEDING PURSUANT TO THIS ARTICLE 11.5. ... BIDDING RULES FOR THE PUBLIC AUCTION WILL BE POSTED ON THE TREASURER'S WEBSITE OR THE ELECTRONIC MEDIUM USED TO CONDUCT THE AUCTION AT LEAST FOURTEEN CALENDAR DAYS BEFORE THE DATE OF THE PUBLIC AUCTION.”

Source: Session Laws of Colorado 2026, Ch. 135 (Senate Bill 26-144) · C.R.S. 39-11.5-102(5)(a); 39-11.5-105(3)(d) · verified 2026-11-02

What makes up the opening or minimum bid? Statewide

The lawful holder must bid no less than the total debt as determined by the treasurer; the bid form sets out all money due to the lawful holder, including the treasurer's fees and costs.

“MUST SET FORTH ALL MONEY DUE AND OWING TO THE LAWFUL HOLDER, INCLUDING ALL TREASURER FEES AND COSTS ... A LAWFUL HOLDER MUST BID NO LESS THAN THE AMOUNT OF THE TOTAL DEBT, ... AS DETERMINED BY THE TREASURER, FOR THE CERTIFICATE OF PURCHASE BEING SOLD.”

Source: Session Laws of Colorado 2026, Ch. 135 (Senate Bill 26-144) · C.R.S. 39-11.5-107(1)(a), (6) · verified 2026-11-02

When and how must the winning bidder pay? Statewide

Payment of the amount bid must reach the treasurer by the date and time of the auction, or at another time on the auction day that the treasurer sets in writing, in the form the treasurer specifies. If the highest bidder does not pay in full, the next highest bidder who pays in time is the successful bidder.

“THE PAYMENT OF ANY AMOUNT BID AT AN AUCTION MUST BE RECEIVED BY A TREASURER NO LATER THAN THE DATE AND TIME OF THE AUCTION, OR AT AN ALTERNATIVE TIME AFTER THE AUCTION AND ON THE DAY OF THE AUCTION, AS SPECIFIED IN WRITING BY THE TREASURER. A BID PAYMENT MUST BE MADE IN THE FORM SPECIFIED BY THE TREASURER.”

Source: Session Laws of Colorado 2026, Ch. 135 (Senate Bill 26-144) · C.R.S. 39-11.5-107(7) · verified 2026-11-02

What costs are added on top of the winning bid? Statewide

If the property is sold to a third-party bidder, or to the lawful holder bidding competitively, the treasurer may collect up to three hundred dollars from the successful bidder for processing the public auction.

“IF A PROPERTY IS SOLD TO A THIRD-PARTY BIDDER OR A LAWFUL HOLDER AS A COMPETITIVE BIDDER, A TREASURER MAY COLLECT UP TO THREE HUNDRED DOLLARS FROM THE SUCCESSFUL BIDDER FOR PERFORMING ACTIONS RELATED TO PROCESSING THE PUBLIC AUCTION”

Source: Session Laws of Colorado 2026, Ch. 135 (Senate Bill 26-144) · C.R.S. 39-11.5-107(9) · verified 2026-11-02

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive? Statewide

When the repurchase period ends, title vests in the holder of the certificate of option, and the treasurer executes and records a treasurer's deed confirming the transfer.

“UPON THE EXPIRATION OF THE REPURCHASE PERIOD ... TITLE TO THE PROPERTY VESTS IN THE HOLDER OF THE CERTIFICATE OF OPTION FOR A TREASURER'S DEED ... THE TREASURER SHALL EXECUTE AND RECORD A TREASURER'S DEED PURSUANT TO SECTION 39-11.5-502 TO THE HOLDER OF THE CERTIFICATE OF OPTION FOR A TREASURER'S DEED ... CONFIRMING THE TRANSFER OF TITLE TO THE PROPERTY.”

Source: Session Laws of Colorado 2026, Ch. 135 (Senate Bill 26-144) · C.R.S. 39-11.5-501(1) · verified 2026-11-02

Can the former owner redeem after the sale? Statewide

The property owner may redeem before the auction by filing an intent to redeem no later than seven calendar days before the scheduled auction. After the auction, a lawful holder who did not win may repurchase the certificate of option by noon on the eighth business day after the auction.

“A PROPERTY OWNER IS ENTITLED TO REDEEM A PROPERTY IF, NO LATER THAN SEVEN CALENDAR DAYS PRIOR TO THE CURRENTLY SCHEDULED PUBLIC AUCTION DATE, THE PROPERTY OWNER FILES AN INTENT TO REDEEM ... NO LATER THAN NOON ON THE EIGHTH BUSINESS DAY FOLLOWING THE PUBLIC AUCTION, THE LAWFUL HOLDER MAY REPURCHASE THE CERTIFICATE OF OPTION FOR A TREASURER'S DEED”

Source: Session Laws of Colorado 2026, Ch. 135 (Senate Bill 26-144) · C.R.S. 39-11.5-106(1)(a); 39-11.5-301(5) · verified 2026-11-02

Which liens or interests survive the sale? Statewide

Subject to junior lienors' overbid claims, title is free and clear of liens and encumbrances junior to the certificate of purchase. The treasurer's deed does not affect existing roads, rights-of-way, conservation easements, other easements or equitable servitudes.

“THE TITLE IS FREE AND CLEAR OF ALL LIENS AND ENCUMBRANCES JUNIOR TO THE CERTIFICATE OF PURCHASE ... EXECUTION OF A TREASURER'S DEED PURSUANT TO THIS SECTION DOES NOT AFFECT THE EXISTENCE OF ANY PUBLIC OR PRIVATE ROADS, RIGHTS-OF-WAY, CONSERVATION EASEMENTS, OTHER EASEMENTS, OR EQUITABLE SERVITUDES”

Source: Session Laws of Colorado 2026, Ch. 135 (Senate Bill 26-144) · C.R.S. 39-11.5-501(1), (3) · verified 2026-11-02

What happens to property that does not sell? Statewide

If no valid bids are received at the public auction, the lawful holder who does not withdraw the application becomes the purchaser.

“"PURCHASER" MEANS A PERSON TO WHOM THE TREASURER AWARDS THE CERTIFICATE OF OPTION FOR A TREASURER'S DEED AS THE WINNING BIDDER AT THE PUBLIC AUCTION WHO TIMELY SUBMITS PROPER FUNDS, OR, IF NO VALID BIDS ARE RECEIVED AT THE PUBLIC AUCTION, THE LAWFUL HOLDER WHO DOES NOT FILE A WITHDRAWAL OF THE APPLICATION”

Source: Session Laws of Colorado 2026, Ch. 135 (Senate Bill 26-144) · C.R.S. 39-11.5-101(22) · verified 2026-11-02

What happens to money paid above what was owed (surplus)? Statewide

Once the claim period ends, remaining overbid is paid in order of recording priority to junior lienors who filed a claim, up to their unpaid lien plus fees and costs. Any overbid money left after that is paid to the property owner.

“ANY REMAINING OVERBID MUST BE PAID IN ORDER OF RECORDING PRIORITY TO JUNIOR LIENORS ... AFTER PAYMENT TO ALL JUNIOR LIENORS ENTITLED TO RECEIVE A PORTION OF THE OVERBID PURSUANT TO THIS SECTION ... ANY REMAINING OVERBID MONEY MUST BE PAID TO THE PROPERTY OWNER”

Source: Session Laws of Colorado 2026, Ch. 135 (Senate Bill 26-144) · C.R.S. 39-11.5-112(1) · County treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

Liens & Certificates

County-held tax lien certificates offered for assignment by the treasurer; the lien, not the land.

What is a tax lien or certificate here? Statewide

The treasurer prepares a certificate of purchase for the purchaser of a tax lien on real property sold for delinquent taxes; it describes the property and states the rate of interest and the total taxes, delinquent interest and fees.

“The treasurer shall prepare, sign, and retain for safekeeping or deliver to the purchaser of a tax lien on any real property sold for the payment of delinquent taxes a certificate of purchase describing the property on which the taxes and fees were paid by the purchaser, as the same was described in the record of sales, and also stating the rate of interest and the total amount of all taxes, delinquent interest, and fees”

Source: Colorado Revised Statutes 2024, Title 39 (Office of Legislative Legal Services) · C.R.S. 39-11-117 · verified 2026-11-02

How do I buy one (auction / over the counter / assignment)? Statewide

Tax liens are sold to the bidders who pay the taxes, delinquent interest and fees, or who pay the largest amount above them (a premium); the excess goes to the county general fund.

“sold to the persons who pay therefor the taxes, delinquent interest, and fees then due thereon or who further pay the largest amount in excess of said taxes, delinquent interest, and fees. The excess amount shall be credited to the county general fund.”

Source: Colorado Revised Statutes 2024, Title 39 (Office of Legislative Legal Services) · C.R.S. 39-11-115(1) · verified 2026-11-02

What interest or penalty does the holder earn? Statewide

Redemption interest is nine percentage points above the discount rate a commercial bank pays the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City, rounded to the nearest full percent.

“The annual rate of redemption interest shall be nine percentage points above the discount rate, which discount rate shall be the rate of interest a commercial bank pays to the federal reserve bank of Kansas City using a government bond or other eligible paper as security, and shall be rounded to the nearest full percent.”

Source: Colorado Revised Statutes 2024, Title 39 (Office of Legislative Legal Services) · C.R.S. 39-12-103(3) · verified 2026-11-02

How long does the owner have to redeem? Statewide

Under the treasurer's deed auction process, the owner must file an intent to redeem no later than seven calendar days before the scheduled public auction; the treasurer's redemption statement is effective for ten business days or until noon the day before the auction, whichever is first, and the owner may redeem at any time before it expires.

“A PROPERTY OWNER IS ENTITLED TO REDEEM A PROPERTY IF, NO LATER THAN SEVEN CALENDAR DAYS PRIOR TO THE CURRENTLY SCHEDULED PUBLIC AUCTION DATE, THE PROPERTY OWNER FILES AN INTENT TO REDEEM THE PROPERTY WITH THE TREASURER. ... A REDEMPTION STATEMENT IS EFFECTIVE FOR TEN BUSINESS DAYS OR UNTIL NOON THE DAY BEFORE THE CURRENTLY SCHEDULED PUBLIC AUCTION DATE, WHICHEVER OCCURS FIRST. ... A PROPERTY OWNER MAY REDEEM A PROPERTY AT ANY TIME BEFORE THE EXPIRATION OF A REDEMPTION STATEMENT BY PAYING THE TREASURER”

Source: Session Laws of Colorado 2026, Ch. 135 (Senate Bill 26-144) · C.R.S. 39-11.5-106(1)(a), (3)(a), (3)(b) · verified 2026-11-02

How does a holder get the property if it is not redeemed? Statewide

After August 1 of the third calendar year after the tax lien sale, the lawful holder may apply for a treasurer's deed. The property then goes to a public auction; if the holder is not the winning bidder, the holder may repurchase the certificate of option within the repurchase period.

“AFTER AUGUST 1 OF THE THIRD CALENDAR YEAR FOLLOWING THE DATE OF A TAX LIEN SALE, A LAWFUL HOLDER MAY APPLY FOR A TREASURER'S DEED ... A LAWFUL HOLDER IS ENTITLED TO REPURCHASE A CERTIFICATE OF OPTION FOR A TREASURER'S DEED IF THE FOLLOWING REQUIREMENTS ARE MET TO THE SATISFACTION OF THE TREASURER: (a) THE LAWFUL HOLDER WAS NOT THE SUCCESSFUL PURCHASER AT THE PUBLIC AUCTION”

Source: Session Laws of Colorado 2026, Ch. 135 (Senate Bill 26-144) · C.R.S. 39-11.5-102(1); 39-11.5-301(1) · verified 2026-11-02

When does a certificate or lien expire? Statewide

As amended, a lien created by a certificate of purchase may not remain a lien longer than seven years after its original issuance. A certificate issued before January 1, 2026 may remain a lien for fifteen years after issuance.

“No lien upon real property created by a tax certificate or a certificate of purchase issued by a treasurer on account of any delinquent property taxes or any special assessment of any kind or nature shall MAY remain a lien thereon for a period longer than fifteen SEVEN years after the original issuance thereof ... NOTWITHSTANDING ANY OTHER PROVISION OF THIS SECTION, A LIEN ON PROPERTY CREATED BY A TAX LIEN SALE CERTIFICATE OF PURCHASE ISSUED PRIOR TO JANUARY 1, 2026, MAY REMAIN A LIEN FOR A PERIOD OF FIFTEEN YEARS AFTER THE DATE OF ISSUANCE.”

Source: Session Laws of Colorado 2026, Ch. 135 (Senate Bill 26-144) · C.R.S. 39-11-148(1) · verified 2026-11-02

What fees does a buyer pay? Statewide

Under the amended fee schedule the county treasurer charges FIVE dollars for each certificate of purchase delivered from a tax lien sale, and FIVE dollars for assigning a county-held certificate to a person who wants to buy it.

“(i) For each certificate of purchase delivered, four FROM A TAX LIEN SALE, FIVE ... (l) For the assignment of a certificate of purchase, made to the county, city, town, or city and county at any tax sale, to a person desiring to purchase land PROPERTY covered by such certificate, four FIVE dollars”

Source: Session Laws of Colorado 2026, Ch. 135 (Senate Bill 26-144) · C.R.S. 30-1-102(1)(i), (l) · County treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

What risks does the law or the county point out (worthless parcels / bankruptcy / expiry)? Statewide

No treasurer's deed may issue after a public auction on a certificate of purchase that has ceased to be a lien under the limitation section. An injunction or bankruptcy petition automatically stays the treasurer from conducting the public auction; the treasurer announces that fact and takes no action on the scheduled date.

“No treasurer's deed shall MAY issue on AFTER PUBLIC AUCTION IN CONNECTION WITH any tax LIEN sale evidenced by tax certificate or certificate of purchase where such tax certificate or THE certificate of purchase has ceased to be a lien pursuant to the provisions of this section ... BEFORE ANY INJUNCTION OR BANKRUPTCY PETITION IS FILED WHICH AUTOMATICALLY STAYS THE TREASURER FROM CONDUCTING THE PUBLIC AUCTION, THE TREASURER SHALL ANNOUNCE, POST, OR PROVIDE NOTICE OF THAT FACT ON THE THEN-SCHEDULED PUBLIC AUCTION DATE, TAKE NO ACTION AT THE THEN-SCHEDULED PUBLIC AUCTION”

Source: Session Laws of Colorado 2026, Ch. 135 (Senate Bill 26-144) · C.R.S. 39-11-148(2); 39-11.5-111(2)(a) · verified 2026-11-02

Can certificates be bought from the county or another holder (assignment)? Statewide

A county holding certificates of purchase from tax lien sales may assign, sell or transfer them in the manner, at the times and on the terms its board of county commissioners sets by resolution.

“Any county in this state having in its possession or under its control certificates of purchase resulting from the sale of a tax lien on land PROPERTY for the nonpayment of general taxes may assign, sell, or transfer such THOSE certificates OF PURCHASE in such manner, at such times, and on such terms as may be determined by resolution of the board of county commissioners”

Source: Session Laws of Colorado 2026, Ch. 135 (Senate Bill 26-144) · C.R.S. 39-11-122 · verified 2026-11-02
