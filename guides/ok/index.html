Available 10 of 14 answered

County-owned property acquired at the resale, offered by the county under its own process.

Step 1 · Find Find the sale or list

What is this list and why is the property available?

Property the county acquired at the tax resale may be sold by the county treasurer after notice by publication, at a price approved by the board of county commissioners.

Official wording
“Any property acquired by the county under the provisions of the resale tax laws may be sold by the county treasurer, after notice by publication, at a price as may be approved by the board of county commissioners, the notice to be given after receipt o f bid on the property.”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3135(A) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

How long does a property stay available?

The county holds property it acquired at resale until it is sold; a sale follows a notice the treasurer publishes once during each of the three consecutive weeks preceding the sale.

Official wording
“Any property acquired by the county under the provisions of the resale tax laws may be sold by the county treasurer, after notice by publication ... The notice shall be published by the county treasurer once during each of the three (3) consecutive weeks preceding the sale”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3135(A) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

How is the price set?

At a county auction, a bid below all the ad valorem taxes owed at the original resale is accepted only with the approval of the county commissioners and the county excise board.

Official wording
“Any bid which is less than all of the real estate ad valorem taxes owed at the time of the original resale shall be accepted only upon approval of the county commissioners and the coun ty excise board.”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3135(B) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 3 · Buy How to buy

How do I buy a property from this list?

On the date in the published notice, the property is sold to the highest competitive bidder for cash in hand or certified funds, or to the original bidder if no higher price is offered, subject to the board of county commissioners' approval.

Official wording
“On the date stated in the notice, the property shall be sold by the county treasurer to the hig hest competitive bidder, for cash in hand or certified funds , or to the original bidder if there be no higher price offered. The sale in any event shall be subject to the approval of the board of county commissioners in its discretion.”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3135(A) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What costs are added to the price?

Advertising and other sale expenses are added to the sale price as a separate charge paid by the purchaser in addition to the bid; a bidder must deposit enough to cover the advertisement and costs before the property is advertised, and the recording cost is part of the cost of sale paid by the purchaser.

Official wording
“shall be apportioned to the respective tracts listed in the sale and shall be added to the sale price of the real estate as a separate and additional charge and shall be paid by the purc haser, in addition to the amount bid upon the real estate. A deposit shall be required of any bidder before advertisement of the property to cover the advertisement and costs. ... The cost of recording shall be considered a part of the cost of sale, and shall be paid by the purchaser.”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3135(A); 3136 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What forms or documents are required?

The State Auditor and Inspector prescribes the form of bid the county treasurers use.

Official wording
“The State Auditor and Inspector shall p rescribe, for the use of the several county treasurers, a form of bid and form of report of sale.”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3136 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

How and when do I pay?

Bidders must pay a deposit before the property is advertised to cover advertising and costs.

Official wording
“A deposit shall be required of any bidder before advertisement of the property to cover the advertisement and costs.”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3135(A) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 4 · After After you buy

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive?

After the commissioners approve the sale, their chair executes a deed conveying title on a form prescribed by the State Auditor and Inspector.

Official wording
“Upon approval of the sale as hereinbefore provided, the chair of the board of county commissioners shall execute a deed conveying title to the purchaser of the property in as full and ample manner as by law provide d on a form prescribed by the State Auditor and Inspector.”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3135(A) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Are sales final or can a purchase be refunded?

The county's resale property fund reimburses a purchaser at resale or at commissioners' sale where no tax was due or the sale was illegal and the purchaser has no adequate recourse against the property. A refund claim not filed within three (3) years from the sale is not paid.

Official wording
“For the reimbursement of the purchaser at resale or at commissioners' sale of any lot, tract, or parcel of real estate, sold at resale, against which no tax was due ... or where the sale thereof to such purchaser was illegal for any other reason; and such purchaser has no adequate recourse against the property thus sold ... Provided, however, that no claim for refund not filed, as herein provided, within a period of three (3) years from the date of such sale shall be allowed or paid from said fund”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3137(B)(3) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 5 · Risks Risks to weigh

Which liens or interests stay with the property?

The resale deed vests an absolute fee simple title subject to claims the state may have had on the real estate for taxes or other liens or encumbrances; where the property was bid off in the name of the county, those claims are canceled and extinguished. Recorded restrictions and covenants running with the land survive a resale tax deed.

Official wording
“shall vest in the grantee an absolute and perfect title in fee simple to the real estate, subject to all claims which the state may have had on the real estate for taxes or other liens or encumbrances ... shall be canceled and extinguished with respect to any deed conveying title to the board of county commissioners where such property was bid off in the name of the county ... said restrictions and covenants shall survive and be enforceable after the issuance of a resale or certificate tax deed”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3131(A); 3145 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Auctions 11 of 17 answered

Step 1 · Find Find the sale or list

How does a tax sale work here?

When real estate taxes have been unpaid for three years or more, the county treasurer advertises and sells the real estate at the annual tax resale for those taxes and all other delinquent taxes, special assessments and costs.

Official wording
“where taxes are a lien upon real property and have been unpaid for a period of three (3) years or more as of the date such taxes first became due and payable, advertise and sell such real estate for such taxes and all other delinquent taxes, special assessments and costs at the tax resale provided for in”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3105(A) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

When are sales held?

The resale is held on the second Monday of June each year; an online resale starts during normal business hours within the full week of the second Monday of June.

Official wording
“the county treasurer shall proceed to sell such real estate at resale, which shall be held on the second Monday of June each year in each county or, if co nducted through an online auction, shall be started on a date and time set by the county treasurer during normal business hours within the full week of the second Monday of June”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3125 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Where do I bid - online or in person?

The county treasurer may hold the June resale as an online auction instead of in person.

Official wording
“The county treasurer may choose to hold the June resale through an online auction instead of the in-person process as prescribed in subsection A of this section.”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3129(B) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What makes up the opening or minimum bid?

Property must sell for at least two-thirds of its current assessed value or the total taxes, penalties, interest and costs due, whichever is less.

Official wording
“All property must be sold for a sum not less than two-thirds (2/3) of the assessed value of such real estate as fixed for the current fiscal year, or for the total amount of taxes, penalties, interest and costs due on such property, whichever is the l esser.”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3129(C) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What does the law or the county tell buyers to check before buying?

The Oklahoma Health Care Authority produces a list for each county of resale properties on which it has liens, and county treasurers must make that list available to potential buyers at the resale.

Official wording
“Using the information provided, the OHCA shall produce a list for each county of properties on which the OHCA has liens. The coun ty treasurers shall make the list of properties with the OHCA liens available to potential buyers at the tax resales.”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3129(D) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 3 · Buy How to buy

When and how must the winning bidder pay?

Property is sold at public auction to the highest bidder for cash or certified funds; online auctions may accept online payments.

Official wording
“The real estate shall be sold at public auction to the highest bidder for cash or certified funds. Online a uctions may accept online payments.”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3129(A) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What costs are added on top of the winning bid?

In addition to the bid, the treasurer collects enough to pay the county's expenses of preparing, listing and advertising the property.

Official wording
“The county treasurer shall also charge ... an amount in addition to the bid placed on such real estate, sufficient to pay all expenses incurred by the county in preparing, listing and advertising the lot or tract purchased by such bidder”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3131(B) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive?

The resale deed vests absolute and perfect title in fee simple, subject to all claims the state may have for taxes or other liens or encumbrances.

Official wording

Can the former owner redeem after the sale?

The owner or anyone with a legal or equitable interest may redeem only before the resale auction starts, by paying the original delinquency with interest and costs.

Official wording
“may redeem the same at any time before the start of the resale auction by paying to the county treasurer the sum which was originally delinquent including interest at the lawful rate”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3113 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What happens to property that does not sell?

If no bid reaches the required minimum, the county treasurer bids the property off in the name of the county.

Official wording
“if there is no bid equal to or greater than the sum so required, the county treasurer shall bid off the same in the name of the county.”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3129(C) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 5 · Risks Risks to weigh

Which liens or interests survive the sale?

The resale tax deed does not extinguish the mortgage of a mortgagee to whom no notice was sent.

Official wording
“the resale tax deed shall be ineffective to extinguish any mortgage on said real estate of a mortgagee to whom no notice was sent.”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3127 · Checked Nov 2, 2026
