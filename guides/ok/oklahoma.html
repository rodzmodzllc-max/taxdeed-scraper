How to buy tax-sale property in Oklahoma County, OK

What the Oklahoma County offices publish about buying tax-sale property: who runs each sale or list, when and where, and what a buyer must do. Every answer below was read on an official page and links to it. Questions not yet confirmed are listed as such.

Auctions

How does a tax sale work here? Statewide

When real estate taxes have been unpaid for three years or more, the county treasurer advertises and sells the real estate at the annual tax resale for those taxes and all other delinquent taxes, special assessments and costs.

“where taxes are a lien upon real property and have been unpaid for a period of three (3) years or more as of the date such taxes first became due and payable, advertise and sell such real estate for such taxes and all other delinquent taxes, special assessments and costs at the tax resale provided for in”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3105(A) · verified 2026-11-02

When are sales held?

The 2026 resale began Monday, June 8, 2026, between 8:00 a.m. and 5:00 p.m., continuing day to day until completed.

“Beginning Monday, June 08, 2026, between the hours of 8:00 a.m. and 5:00 p.m. on Monday (Exact time to be determined by County Treasurer) and continuing from day to day thereafter between the hours of 8:00 a. m. and 5:00 p. m., until said sale has been completed according to law.”

Source: Oklahoma County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

Where do I bid - online or in person?

Bidders must follow the sale on the Treasurer's Resale Active List web page.

“IT WILL BE THE BIDDERS RESPONSIBILITY TO FOLLOW ALONG WITH THE SALE FROM THE RESALE ACTIVE LIST ON OUR WEBSITE.”

Source: Oklahoma County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

How do I register to bid?

Resale bidders must be 18 or older; for 2026 they could pre-register at the County Treasurer's Office from May 14 to June 5.

“ALL RESALE BIDDERS MUST BE 18 YEARS OF AGE OR OLDER, IN ORDER TO BID ON PROPERTY. ... Bidders can pre-register in the County Treasurer's Office May 14 thru June 5, 2026.”

Source: Oklahoma County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

What makes up the opening or minimum bid? Statewide

Property must sell for at least two-thirds of its current assessed value or the total taxes, penalties, interest and costs due, whichever is less.

“All property must be sold for a sum not less than two-thirds (2/3) of the assessed value of such real estate as fixed for the current fiscal year, or for the total amount of taxes, penalties, interest and costs due on such property, whichever is the l esser.”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3129(C) · verified 2026-11-02

When and how must the winning bidder pay?

You must have cash at the time of the sale and cannot leave to get it; money can be placed on trust before 8:00 a.m. on sale day.

“YOU MUST HAVE CASH AT THE TIME OF THE SALE. YOU CAN NOT LEAVE TO GO GET CASH. YOU CAN PUT MONEY ON TRUST BEFORE 8:00 A.M. ON DAY OF SALE.”

Source: Oklahoma County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

What costs are added on top of the winning bid?

Successful bids include the cost, abstract fee and resale advertising; the Treasurer also collects $10.00 per deed plus a $20.00 filing fee.

“All Successful Bids include the amount bid and any cost, abstract fee, and Resale advertising. ... The Treasurer collect $10.00 for each deed issued and an additional filing fee of $20.00.”

Source: Oklahoma County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive? Statewide

The resale deed vests absolute and perfect title in fee simple, subject to all claims the state may have for taxes or other liens or encumbrances.

“shall vest in the grantee an absolute and perfect title in fee simple to the real estate, subject to all claims which the state may have had on the real estate for taxes or other liens or encumbrances”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3131(A) · verified 2026-11-02

Can the former owner redeem after the sale? Statewide

The owner or anyone with a legal or equitable interest may redeem only before the resale auction starts, by paying the original delinquency with interest and costs.

“may redeem the same at any time before the start of the resale auction by paying to the county treasurer the sum which was originally delinquent including interest at the lawful rate”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3113 · verified 2026-11-02

Which liens or interests survive the sale?

Some Federal, State and City liens may still exist and be valid against property sold at the resale; an OHCA lien list is available on request.

“Each buyer needs to beware that some Federal, State and City liens may still exist and be valid against the property. ... A copy of OHCA Lien list is available upon request.”

Source: Oklahoma County Treasurer - June Resale Instructions/Guidelines · Oklahoma County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

Is the title insurable or does it need a quiet-title action?

At the resale, a tax deed is not a warranty deed and the County does not warrant title. Each purchaser should consider filing a quiet title action in District Court for a more marketable title.

“A tax deed is not a warranty deed and the County does not warrant title to any property sold. The buyer assumes all responsibility and liability upon the purchase of these properties. While it is the decision of each buyer, each purchaser should give consideration to the filing of a quiet title action in District Court in order to ensure a more marketable title.”

Source: Oklahoma County Treasurer - June Resale Instructions/Guidelines · Oklahoma County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

What happens to property that does not sell? Statewide

If no bid reaches the required minimum, the county treasurer bids the property off in the name of the county.

“if there is no bid equal to or greater than the sum so required, the county treasurer shall bid off the same in the name of the county.”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3129(C) · verified 2026-11-02

What does the law or the county tell buyers to check before buying?

The resale is a buyer beware sale; each bidder should research the records and inspect the property before bidding.

“THE RESALE IS A BUYER BEWARE SALE! ... Each bidder should research the records and inspect the property which they are considering prior to entering a bid.”

Source: Oklahoma County Treasurer - June Resale Instructions/Guidelines · Oklahoma County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

Available

County-owned property acquired at the resale, offered by the county under its own process.

Which office handles it and how do I contact them?

Questions about buying county property go to the Treasurer's delinquent tax department at (405) 713-1300.

“If you have any questions concerning this process, please call the delinquent tax department at (405) 713-1300.”

Source: Oklahoma County Treasurer - Guidelines for Purchasing County Owned Properties · verified 2026-11-02

What is this list and why is the property available? Statewide

Property the county acquired at the tax resale may be sold by the county treasurer after notice by publication, at a price approved by the board of county commissioners.

“Any property acquired by the county under the provisions of the resale tax laws may be sold by the county treasurer, after notice by publication, at a price as may be approved by the board of county commissioners, the notice to be given after receipt o f bid on the property.”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3135(A) · verified 2026-11-02

How do I buy a property from this list?

Complete the County Property Bid Form, print it and present it to the Oklahoma County Treasurer's Office.

“An interested bidder must initiate a bid by completing a bid form by clicking this link, County Property Bid Form. You will need to print the form and present it to the Oklahoma County Treasurer's Office.”

Source: Oklahoma County Treasurer - Guidelines for Purchasing County Owned Properties · verified 2026-11-02

How is the price set?

Once the County owns these properties, the public can buy them through a bidding process.

“Once the County owns these properties, they can be purchased by the public through a bidding process.”

Source: Oklahoma County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

What costs are added to the price?

The Treasurer collects an additional $330.00 in cash for estimated publication cost, treasurer's cost and recording fees.

“We will collect an additional $330.00 in cash for estimated publication cost, treasurer's cost and recording fees.”

Source: Oklahoma County Treasurer - Guidelines for Purchasing County Owned Properties · verified 2026-11-02

What forms or documents are required?

A completed County Property Bid Form.

“An interested bidder must initiate a bid by completing a bid form”

Source: Oklahoma County Treasurer - Guidelines for Purchasing County Owned Properties · verified 2026-11-02

How and when do I pay?

Pay the full bid in cash, or 10% in cash and the rest by personal check (proof it cleared is needed before the deed issues); cashier's checks and money orders are also accepted.

“Bidder has the option of paying full amount of bid in cash or paying 10% in cash with remaining balance to be paid by personal check (proof the check has cleared must be presented before deed will be issued). We also accept cashiers check or money orders.”

Source: Oklahoma County Treasurer - Guidelines for Purchasing County Owned Properties · verified 2026-11-02

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive? Statewide

After the commissioners approve the sale, their chair executes a deed conveying title on a form prescribed by the State Auditor and Inspector.

“Upon approval of the sale as hereinbefore provided, the chair of the board of county commissioners shall execute a deed conveying title to the purchaser of the property in as full and ample manner as by law provide d on a form prescribed by the State Auditor and Inspector.”

Source: Oklahoma Statutes - Title 68 (Oklahoma Legislature, complete title) · 68 O.S. 3135(A) · verified 2026-11-02

How long does a property stay available?

The bid is published for three consecutive weeks before the sale date, which is scheduled about (30) days from receipt of the bid.

“Bid will be published for three consecutive weeks prior to the sale date which will be scheduled approximately (30) days from the receipt of the bid.”

Source: Oklahoma County Treasurer - Guidelines for Purchasing County Owned Properties · Oklahoma County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

Which liens or interests stay with the property?

Some Federal, State and City liens may still exist and be valid against county-owned property.

“Some Federal, State and City liens may still exist and be valid against the property.”

Source: Oklahoma County Treasurer - County Owned Properties for Sale · Oklahoma County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

The buyer assumes all responsibility and liability upon the purchase of these properties. While it is the decision of each buyer, each purchaser should give consideration to the filing of a quiet title action in District Court in order to insure a more marketable title.”

Can I inspect the property before buying?

Each bidder should research the records and inspect the property before entering a bid.

“Each bidder should research the records and inspect the property which they are considering prior to entering a bid.”

Source: Oklahoma County Treasurer - County Owned Properties for Sale · Oklahoma County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

Are sales final or can a purchase be refunded?

Any unused part of the money collected for publication is refunded. If the initial bidder is not the highest bidder, the money on deposit is refunded within thirty (30) days.

“The cost may be less than the amount collected and any balance will be refunded. ... If the initial bidder is not the highest bidder at the conclusion of the sale, the monies placed on deposit will be refunded within thirty (30) days.”

Source: Oklahoma County Treasurer - Guidelines for Purchasing County Owned Properties · Oklahoma County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02
