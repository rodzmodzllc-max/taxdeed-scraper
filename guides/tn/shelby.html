How to buy tax-sale property in Shelby County, TN

Land bank inventory: county-owned parcels taken through the delinquent tax process, offered by the Land Bank on an offer basis.

Step 1 · Find Find the sale or list

Which office handles it and how do I contact them?

The Shelby County Land Bank's property site lists phone 901-222-1150 and e-mail landbank@shelbycountytn.gov.

Official wording
“"tenantName":"Shelby County Land Bank" ... "supportEmail":"landbank@shelbycountytn.gov","supportPhone":"901-222-1150"”

Source: Shelby County Land Bank - ePropertyPlus public site (FAQ and disclaimer) · Shelby County Land Bank · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What is this list and why is the property available? Statewide

The county mayor takes charge of all parcels the county bought at a delinquent tax sale.

Official wording
“It is the duty of the county mayor of each county to take charge of all parcels purchased by the county at a delin- quent tax sale pursuant to T.C.A..§ 67-5-2501.”

Source: CTAS - Collection of Delinquent Real Property Taxes (University of Tennessee County Technical Assistance Service) · T.C.A. 67-5-2507 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

How long does a property stay available?

Once advertised in the Tri-State Defender or The Daily News, listings are open for the 10 days after the advertisement, as required by T.C.A. 67-5-2507; others may apply to take part in a bid-off.

Official wording
“Please monitor our website for listings of properties for the 10 days after they are advertised in either the Tri-State Defender or The Daily News as required by T.C.A. 67-5-2507. ... You may apply for these properties online to participate in a bid-off for the property.”

Source: Shelby County Land Bank - ePropertyPlus public site settings (disclaimer and help text) · Shelby County Land Bank · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What does the law or the program tell buyers to check before buying?

Prospective purchasers must perform a due diligence verification of the property, including its value and condition, compliance with building and local regulations, and the terms of all encumbrances, rights and interests registered against the title.

Official wording
“Prospective Purchasers must perform a due diligence verification of the property, including but not limited to, the value and condition of the property including compliance with building and local authority regulations, and the terms of all encumbrances, rights and interests registered against or in respect of the title.”

Source: Shelby County Land Bank - ePropertyPlus public site settings (disclaimer and help text) · Shelby County Land Bank · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 1 · Find Find the sale or list

How does a tax sale work here? Statewide

After a delinquent tax suit ends, the county sells the property at a tax sale ordered by the court for cash, subject to the taxpayer's right of redemption.

Official wording
“After the conclusion of the delinquent tax suit, the county holds a tax sale to sell property in order to collect delinquent taxes. ... The court shall order the sale of the property for cash, subject to the equity of redemption”

Source: CTAS - Collection of Delinquent Real Property Taxes (University of Tennessee County Technical Assistance Service) · T.C.A. 67-5-2501 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Where do I bid - online or in person? Statewide

The sale is held at the place and time given in the notice and must be public and open to all.

Official wording
“The sale must be conducted at the place and time given in the notice or as advertised. The sale should be public and open to all.”

Source: CTAS - Collection of Delinquent Real Property Taxes (University of Tennessee County Technical Assistance Service) · T.C.A. 67-5-2502 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What if the property is occupied? Statewide

On application of the purchaser, the court that made the tax sale orders a writ of possession in a proper case. A purchaser who made no advance demand has no right to rents or profits from a taxpayer who stayed in possession during the redemption period.

Official wording
“A writ of possession shall, upon application of the purchaser, in a proper case, be ordered by the court in which the tax sale has been made. A purchaser not making an advance demand for rents or profits shall have no rights to rents or profits from a taxpayer who has remained in possession during the redemption period.”

Source: CTAS - Collection of Delinquent Real Property Taxes (University of Tennessee County Technical Assistance Service) · T.C.A. 67-5-2503(b) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 4 · After After you buy

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive? Statewide

A tax deed or an order confirming the sale is an assurance of perfect title; it can be invalidated only on grounds set by statute, such as proof that the land was not liable to sale for taxes or that the taxes were paid before the sale.

Official wording
“A tax deed of conveyance or an order confirming the sale is an assurance of perfect title to the purchaser of such land, and no such conveyance will be invalidated in any court, except by proof that the land was not liable to sale for taxes, or that the taxes for which the land was sold have been paid before the sale”

Source: CTAS - Collection of Delinquent Real Property Taxes (University of Tennessee County Technical Assistance Service) · T.C.A. 67-5-2504(b) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Can the former owner redeem after the sale? Statewide

Redemption runs from the order confirming the sale: one year if taxes were delinquent five years or less, one hundred eighty days if more than five but less than eight years, and ninety days if eight years or more.

Official wording
“If the period of delinquency is five years or less, the redemption period shall be one year from the entry of the order confirming the sale; If the period of delinquency is more than five years but less than eight years, the redemption period shall be one hundred eighty days from the entry of the order confirming the sale; or If the period of delinquency is eight years or more, the redemption period shall be ninety days”

Source: CTAS - Collection of Delinquent Real Property Taxes (University of Tennessee County Technical Assistance Service) · T.C.A. 67-5-2701(a)(1) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What happens to property that does not sell? Statewide

The clerk bids the amount due for taxes, penalties, interest and costs if no other bidder offers the same or more.

Official wording
“The clerk should bid in the amount due for taxes, penalties, interest and costs at the sale if no other bidder offers the same or larger bid.”

Source: CTAS - Collection of Delinquent Real Property Taxes (University of Tennessee County Technical Assistance Service) · T.C.A. 67-5-2501; 67-5-2506 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 5 · Risks Risks to weigh

Is the title insurable or does it need a quiet-title action? Statewide

A tax deed or an order confirming the sale is an assurance of perfect title, invalidated only on proof that the land was not liable to sale, the taxes were paid before the sale, or there was substantial noncompliance with mandatory statutory provisions. After the order confirming the sale, the purchaser may file suit to quiet title.

Official wording
“A tax deed of conveyance or an order confirming the sale is an assurance of perfect title to the purchaser of such land, and no such conveyance will be invalidated in any court, except by proof that the land was not liable to sale for taxes, or that the taxes for which the land was sold have been paid before the sale or that there was substantial noncompliance with mandatory statutory provisions relating to the proceedings in which the parcel was sold ... After entry of an order confirming the sale of a parcel, the purchaser may file suit to quiet title”

Source: CTAS - Collection of Delinquent Real Property Taxes (University of Tennessee County Technical Assistance Service) · T.C.A. 67-5-2504(b), (d)(4) · Checked Nov 2, 2026
