How to buy tax-sale property in Wright County, MN

What the Wright County offices publish about buying tax-sale property: who runs each sale or list, when and where, and what a buyer must do. Every answer below was read on an official page and links to it. Questions not yet confirmed are listed as such.

Auctions

An online auction of tax-forfeited land the county holds (Wright County).

Which office handles it and how do I contact them?

Wright County Taxpayer Services, Wright County Government Center, 3650 Braddock Avenue NE, Buffalo; county phone 763-682-3900 or 800-362-3667.

“If you have any questions, please reach out to Taxpayer Services at Wright County. ... 3650 Braddock Avenue NE Buffalo, MN 55313 Ph: 763-682-3900 or 800-362-3667”

Source: Online Tax Forfeit Parcel Auction is Live - Wright County, MN · verified 2026-11-02

How does a tax sale work here? Statewide

Before managing tax-forfeited land any other way, the county must first offer forfeited parcels at a public auction run by the county auditor and sell to the highest bidder.

“Prior to managing tax-forfeited lands as otherwise provided in this chapter, a county must first offer tax-forfeited parcels for sale pursuant to this section ... The county auditor must sell the property at a public auction to the highest bidder in a manner reasonably calculated to facilitate public participation, including by online auction.”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.005, subd. 1, 4 · verified 2026-11-02

When are sales held?

The county's most recent announced online tax forfeiture auction ended at 9 p.m. CDT on Sunday, Sept. 27.

“The auction will end at 9 p.m. CDT Sunday, Sept. 27.”

Source: Online Tax Forfeit Parcel Auction is Live - Wright County, MN · verified 2026-11-02

Where do I bid - online or in person?

Online, through the county's auction host Public Surplus. All parcel information is in the item listing.

“Parcels are available to be bid on via our online auction host, Public Surplus. The auction will end at 9 p.m. CDT Sunday, Sept. 27. All relevant parcel information is located within the item listing.”

Source: Online Tax Forfeit Parcel Auction is Live - Wright County, MN · verified 2026-11-02

What makes up the opening or minimum bid? Statewide

The starting price is the estimated market value from the most recent assessment, and the property may not sell below it for 30 days. If no buyer pays it, the price drops to the minimum bid: the delinquent taxes, special assessments, penalties, interest and costs.

“"minimum bid" means the sum of delinquent taxes, special assessments, penalties, interests, and costs assigned to the parcel. ... the initial price of the property, which is equal to the estimated market value, as determined by the most recent assessment. The property must not be sold for less than the initial price for 30 days after it is initially made available at auction. If no buyer is willing to pay the initial price, the price for the property must be reduced to the minimum bid.”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.005, subd. 2(3), 4(b) · verified 2026-11-02

When and how must the winning bidder pay? Statewide

Parcels are sold for cash only unless the county board has adopted a resolution for sale on terms; on terms, at least ten percent of the purchase price is paid at the time of purchase and the balance in no more than ten equal annual installments, or per county board policy.

“The parcels must be sold for cash only, unless the county board of the county has adopted a resolution providing for their sale on terms, in which event the resolution controls with respect to the sale. When the sale is made on terms other than for cash only (1) a payment of at least ten percent of the purchase price must be made at the time of purchase, and the balance must be paid in no more than ten equal annual installments, or (2) the payments must be made in accordance with county board policy”

Source: Sec. 282.01 MN Statutes · Minn. Stat. § 282.01, subd. 4 · county auditor · verified 2026-11-02

What costs are added on top of the winning bid? Statewide

A $25 fee is collected in addition to the sale price when the land is fully paid for.

“and upon full payment for the land, plus a $25 fee in addition to the sale price, the sale shall be complete and a conveyance of the land shall be issued to the purchaser”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.014 · verified 2026-11-02

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive? Statewide

After full payment, a conveyance is issued to the purchaser according to the land's status on forfeiture. Any conveyance of forfeited land reserves all minerals and mineral rights to the state.

“a conveyance of the land shall be issued to the purchaser as provided by the appropriate statutes according to the status of the land upon forfeiture. ... Any conveyance of forfeited lands shall be subject to exceptions and reservations in this state, in trust for the taxing districts of all minerals and mineral rights.”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.014; 282.12 · verified 2026-11-02

Can the former owner redeem after the sale? Statewide

The law provides for repurchase by an interested party before the public sale, by paying all delinquent taxes and assessments with penalties, interest and costs.

“Prior to the public sale required under this section, an interested party may repurchase the property by payment of the sum of all delinquent taxes and assessments computed under section 282.251 , together with penalties, interest, and costs, that accrued or would have accrued if the parcel of land had not forfeited.”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.005, subd. 3 · verified 2026-11-02

Which liens or interests survive the sale? Statewide

Forfeiture extinguishes all liens, claims and encumbrances except: interested parties' rights to surplus proceeds, redemption rights under federal law, easements and rights-of-way of holders who are not interested parties, and recorded real covenants.

“The forfeiture of the property extinguishes all liens, claims, and encumbrances other than: (1) the rights of interested parties to surplus proceeds under this section; (2) rights of redemption provided under federal law; (3) easements and rights-of-way holders who are not interested parties; and (4) benefits or burdens of any real covenants filed of record as of the date of forfeiture.”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.005, subd. 10 · verified 2026-11-02

What happens to property that does not sell? Statewide

If no buyer pays the minimum bid, the state is deemed to have bought the property by a credit bid, and the parcel may be disposed of under the rest of chapter 282.

“If no buyer is willing to pay the minimum bid, the state is deemed to have purchased the property through a credit bid and the parcels may be disposed of as otherwise provided in this chapter.”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.005, subd. 4(b) · verified 2026-11-02

What happens to money paid above what was owed (surplus)? Statewide

Proceeds above the minimum bid are available to interested parties. Within 60 days of the sale the county auditor must notify them of the surplus with a claim form, and they may claim it if they file within six months from the date the notice is first mailed.

“The proceeds in excess of the minimum bid shall be available for distribution pursuant to subdivision 6. ... If a sale under this section results in a surplus, within 60 days of the sale, the county auditor must notify interested parties, in a manner described in subdivision 7, of the surplus by sending notice of the surplus and a claim form to the interested parties. ... Interested parties are entitled to make a claim for surplus proceeds under this subdivision if they file a claim within six months from the date the notice is first mailed to the interested parties.”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.005, subd. 5, 6 · County auditor · verified 2026-11-02

Available

Tax-forfeited land the county offers for purchase.

Which office handles it and how do I contact them?

Wright County Taxpayer Services, Wright County Government Center, 3650 Braddock Avenue NE, Buffalo; county phone 763-682-3900 or 800-362-3667.

“If you have any questions, please reach out to Taxpayer Services at Wright County. ... 3650 Braddock Avenue NE Buffalo, MN 55313 Ph: 763-682-3900 or 800-362-3667”

Source: Online Tax Forfeit Parcel Auction is Live - Wright County, MN · verified 2026-11-02

What is this list and why is the property available? Statewide

After all parcels in a county sale have been offered to the highest bidder, the county auditor sells any remaining parcels to anyone offering the appraised value.

“and shall sell them to the highest bidder, but not for a sum less than the appraised value, until all of the parcels of land have been offered. Then the county auditor shall sell any remaining parcels to anyone offering to pay the appraised value”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.01, subd. 7 · verified 2026-11-02

How do I buy a property from this list? Statewide

Remaining parcels are sold by the county auditor to anyone offering to pay the appraised value. A person who could have repurchased the parcel may not buy it for less than all taxes, assessments, penalties, interest and costs due at forfeiture.

“Then the county auditor shall sell any remaining parcels to anyone offering to pay the appraised value, except that if the person could have repurchased a parcel of property under section 282.012 or 282.241 , that person may not purchase that same parcel of property at the sale under this subdivision for a purchase price less than the sum of all taxes, assessments, penalties, interest, and costs due at the time of forfeiture”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.01, subd. 7 · verified 2026-11-02

How is the price set? Statewide

Parcels are appraised by the county board, and the sale may not be for less than the appraised value, except under the city-sale alternate procedure.

“Any parcels of land proposed to be sold shall be first appraised by the county board of the county in which the parcels lie. ... The sale must not be for less than the appraised value except as provided in subdivision 7a.”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.01, subd. 3(a), 4(a) · verified 2026-11-02

What costs are added to the price? Statewide

A $25 fee is collected in addition to the sale price on full payment.

“and upon full payment for the land, plus a $25 fee in addition to the sale price, the sale shall be complete”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.014 · verified 2026-11-02

How and when do I pay? Statewide

Cash only, unless the county board has adopted a resolution for sale on terms. On terms, at least ten percent is paid at purchase and the balance in no more than ten equal annual installments.

“The parcels must be sold for cash only, unless the county board of the county has adopted a resolution providing for their sale on terms, in which event the resolution controls with respect to the sale. When the sale is made on terms other than for cash only (1) a payment of at least ten percent of the purchase price must be made at the time of purchase, and the balance must be paid in no more than ten equal annual installments”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.01, subd. 4(a) · verified 2026-11-02

After full payment, a conveyance is issued according to the land's status on forfeiture; all minerals and mineral rights are reserved to the state.

“a conveyance of the land shall be issued to the purchaser as provided by the appropriate statutes according to the status of the land upon forfeiture. ... Any conveyance of forfeited lands shall be subject to exceptions and reservations in this state, in trust for the taxing districts of all minerals and mineral rights.”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.014; 282.12 · verified 2026-11-02

How long does a property stay available? Statewide

The sale continues until all parcels are sold or until the county board orders a reappraisal or withdraws parcels from sale.

“The sale must continue until all the parcels are sold or until the county board orders a reappraisal or withdraws any or all of the parcels from sale.”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.01, subd. 7 · verified 2026-11-02

Which liens or interests stay with the property? Statewide

No sale frees a parcel from the lien for the special benefit of a public improvement made after forfeiture until the cost of that improvement, including any penalties, is paid.

“No sale of a parcel of land shall discharge or free the parcel of land from lien for the special benefit conferred upon it by reason of the public improvement until the cost of it, including penalties, if any, is paid.”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.01, subd. 3(d) · verified 2026-11-02
