How to buy tax-sale property in Ramsey County, MN

What the Ramsey County offices publish about buying tax-sale property: who runs each sale or list, when and where, and what a buyer must do. Every answer below was read on an official page and links to it. Questions not yet confirmed are listed as such.

Available 13 of 14 answered

Tax-forfeited land the county offers for purchase.

Step 1 · Find Find the sale or list

Which office handles it and how do I contact them?

Ramsey County Productive Properties, Plato Building, 90 Plato Blvd. West, Saint Paul; 651-266-2080; Monday - Friday 8 a.m. - 4:30 p.m.

Official wording
“Contact us Productive Properties Monday – Friday: 8 a.m. – 4:30 p.m. [email protected] 651-266-2080 Plato Building 90 Plato Blvd. West Saint Paul , MN 55107”

Source: Tax-Forfeited Public Sales | Ramsey County, Minnesota · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What is this list and why is the property available? Statewide

After all parcels in a county sale have been offered to the highest bidder, the county auditor sells any remaining parcels to anyone offering the appraised value.

Official wording
“and shall sell them to the highest bidder, but not for a sum less than the appraised value, until all of the parcels of land have been offered. Then the county auditor shall sell any remaining parcels to anyone offering to pay the appraised value”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.01, subd. 7 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

How long does a property stay available? Statewide

The sale continues until all parcels are sold or until the county board orders a reappraisal or withdraws parcels from sale.

Official wording
“The sale must continue until all the parcels are sold or until the county board orders a reappraisal or withdraws any or all of the parcels from sale.”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.01, subd. 7 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 2 · Check Check before you commit

How is the price set?

Parcels that received no bid in the online sale can be bought over the counter at the Tax-Forfeit Land Section for not less than the Minimum Bid price on the appraisal list, which equals the appraised value plus any special assessments certified after forfeiture.

Official wording
“Any parcels for which no bid is received during the online sale can be rebid for purchase beginning the next business day after the online auction closed, at 9:00 a.m. at the office of the Tax-Forfeit Land Section for not less than the Minimum Bid price set forth on the March 13, 2026 appraisal list. ... The Minimum Bid price equals the total of the appraised value and any special assessments that have been certified after the date of forfeiture.”

Source: Ramsey County Tax Forfeited Land Online Sales Terms and Conditions | Ramsey County, Minnesota · Ramsey County Productive Properties (Tax-Forfeited Land section) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Can I inspect the property before buying?

Buyers can obtain the county's authorization to perform soil testing at their own expense before purchasing; the sale is not rescinded for soil problems found afterwards, and the buyer relies solely on its own independent examination and inspection of the property.

Official wording
“Purchaser acknowledges that buyer(s) were able to obtain authorization from Ramsey County to perform soil testing at buyer’s own expense before purchasing parcel or parcels. The sale will not be rescinded if soil problems of any type are discovered after the sale. ... Buyer further acknowledges and agrees that it is relying solely upon its own independent examination, inspection, study, and knowledge of the property.”

Source: Ramsey County Tax Forfeited Land Online Sales Terms and Conditions | Ramsey County, Minnesota · Ramsey County Productive Properties (Tax-Forfeited Land section) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What does the law or the program tell buyers to check before buying?

The county highly recommends thoroughly researching any tax-forfeited parcel before purchase; most liens and mortgages, except federal and state tax liens, are canceled at forfeiture, and the purchaser is responsible for verifying this.

Official wording
“It is highly recommended that you thoroughly research any tax-forfeited parcels before purchasing. Most liens and mortgages, except federal and state tax liens, are canceled at the time of forfeiture, and it is the responsibility of the purchaser to verify this information. For specific legal issues, consult an attorney.”

Source: Tax-Forfeited Public Sales | Ramsey County, Minnesota · Ramsey County Productive Properties · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 3 · Buy How to buy

How do I buy a property from this list?

Properties that did not sell at the public auction are offered over the counter on a first-come, first-served basis. Review the purchase requirements and submit an inquiry.

Official wording
“Properties listed below are available for immediate purchase through Ramsey County's over-the-counter sale process. These tax-forfeited properties did not sell at the public auction and are now offered on a first-come, first-served basis. Browse the available properties and review the purchase requirements before submitting an inquiry.”

Source: Over-the-Counter Property List | Ramsey County, Minnesota · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What costs are added to the price?

At closing: an assurance fee of 3% of the total sales price, a $51.00 state deed recording fee, a $25.00 deed preparation fee and deed tax of $0.0034 of the total sales price.

Official wording
“At the closing of the sale, the following fees will be collected: Assurance fee: 3% of the total sales price State deed recording fee: $51.00 Deed preparation fee: $25.00 Deed tax: $0.0034 of the total sales price”

Source: Ramsey County Tax Forfeited Land Online Sales Terms and Conditions | Ramsey County, Minnesota · Ramsey County Productive Properties (Tax-Forfeited Land section) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

How and when do I pay?

No payment plan is permitted for auction or over-the-counter purchases; the county says payment in full is due within ten days of the award.

Official wording
“A payment plan is not permitted when purchasing property through the auction or over the counter process. The winning bidder must remit payment in full within ten days the property is awarded.”

Source: Auction Frequently Asked Questions | Ramsey County, Minnesota · Ramsey County Productive Properties (Tax-Forfeited Land section) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 4 · After After you buy

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive?

A state deed on a form prepared by the Minnesota Attorney General, executed by the Minnesota Department of Revenue for the State; the deed cannot be ordered until the County Auditor has received the full purchase price.

Official wording
“Conveyance of tax-forfeit property is made by state deed on a form prepared by the Minnesota Attorney General and executed by the Minnesota Department of Revenue on behalf of the State of Minnesota. By law, a state deed cannot be ordered until the full purchase price for the subject property has been received by the County Auditor.”

Source: Ramsey County Tax Forfeited Land Online Sales Terms and Conditions | Ramsey County, Minnesota · Ramsey County Productive Properties (Tax-Forfeited Land section) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Are sales final or can a purchase be refunded?

All sales are final; no refunds or exchanges are permitted.

Official wording
“All sales are final, and no refunds or exchanges are permitted. Ramsey County is not responsible for locating or determining property lines or boundaries.”

Source: Ramsey County Tax Forfeited Land Online Sales Terms and Conditions | Ramsey County, Minnesota · Ramsey County Productive Properties (Tax-Forfeited Land section) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 5 · Risks Risks to weigh

Which liens or interests stay with the property?

The county states that the majority of liens and mortgages, except federal and state tax liens, are canceled at forfeiture, and that verifying any liens is the purchaser's responsibility. Canceled special assessments may be reassessed by the municipality.

Official wording
“The majority of all liens and mortgages with the exception of federal and state tax liens are canceled at the time of forfeiture. It is the responsibility of the purchaser to verify any liens on the property. ... In addition, any remaining balance of canceled special assessments that existed before the tax forfeiture may be reassessed by the municipality.”

Source: Auction Frequently Asked Questions | Ramsey County, Minnesota · Ramsey County Productive Properties · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Is the title insurable or does it need a quiet-title action?

Neither the county nor the State of Minnesota warrants the condition of title; tax forfeiture creates a break in the chain of title, the seller conveys tax title, and an attorney's services may be necessary to make the title marketable.

Official wording
“The county or the State of Minnesota does not warrant the condition of title. ... Tax forfeiture will create a break in the chain of title. Seller conveys tax title, and the services of an attorney may be necessary to make the title marketable.”

Source: Ramsey County Tax Forfeited Land Online Sales Terms and Conditions | Ramsey County, Minnesota · Ramsey County Productive Properties (Tax-Forfeited Land section) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

How does a tax sale work here? Statewide

Before managing tax-forfeited land any other way, the county must first offer forfeited parcels at a public auction run by the county auditor and sell to the highest bidder.

Official wording
“Prior to managing tax-forfeited lands as otherwise provided in this chapter, a county must first offer tax-forfeited parcels for sale pursuant to this section ... The county auditor must sell the property at a public auction to the highest bidder in a manner reasonably calculated to facilitate public participation, including by online auction.”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.005, subd. 1, 4 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

When are sales held? Statewide

The sale must occur within six months of the filing of the certificate of forfeiture or the date the property is vacated, whichever is later, with notice published at least 30 days before the sale starts.

Official wording
“The sale under this section must occur within six months of either the filing of the certificate of forfeiture pursuant to section 281.23, subdivision 9 , or the date the property is vacated by the occupant, whichever is later. Notice of the sale under this subdivision must be provided by publication in newspapers, websites, and other forums that serve diverse communities in the county where the property is located at least 30 days before the commencement of the sale.”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.005, subd. 4(a) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Where do I bid - online or in person?

Online: the public sale is an online auction run by the Minnesota Department of Administration, Fleet & Surplus Division, on its MNBid website.

Official wording
“The public sale will be held via online auction and conducted by the Minnesota Department of Administration, Fleet & Surplus Division, using their MNBid website.”

Source: Ramsey County Tax Forfeited Land Online Sales Terms and Conditions | Ramsey County, Minnesota · Ramsey County Productive Properties (Tax-Forfeited Land section) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 2 · Check Check before you commit

What makes up the opening or minimum bid?

The starting bid in the initial EMV sale equals the most recent Estimated Market Value set by the Ramsey County Assessor's Office; if the property does not sell in that sale, the price is set to the Minimum Bid Price defined in Minnesota Statutes, section 282.005, subdivision 2.

Official wording
“The starting bid for the initial EMV sale is equal to the most recent Estimated Market Value as determined by the Ramsey County Assessor’s Office. If a property does not sell during the EMV sale, the price will be set to the Minimum Bid Price, as defined by Minnesota Statutes, section 282.005, subdivision 2.”

How do I register to bid?

Bidders register online through the Minnesota Department of Administration website and need valid identification.

Official wording
“You may register online by visiting the ... Minnesota Department of Administration website. ... Please note, all bidders will need valid identification to register.”

When and how must the winning bidder pay?

Within fourteen (14) business days of the bid award, the buyer submits the winning bid amount to Surplus Services by cashier's check or money order. At a later closing with Ramsey County, the buyer pays certified funds for the 3% Assurance Fee, State Deed Recording Fee, Deed Preparation Fee and Deed Tax.

Official wording
“PAYMENT 1. Within fourteen (14) business days from the date of the bid award, buyer shall submit payment to “Surplus Services” by either mailing or physically dropping off Cashier’s Check or Money Order made payable to “Surplus Services” at 5420 Old Highway 8, Arden Hills, MN 55112. ... At the closing with Ramsey County, Buyer must provide funds for the following: 3% Assurance Fee; State Deed Recording Fee; Deed Preparation Fee; and Deed Tax.”

Source: Auction Frequently Asked Questions | Ramsey County, Minnesota · Ramsey County Productive Properties · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Can the former owner redeem after the sale?

The county's FAQ says there is no waiting period for redemption by the previous owner once the property has been listed for sale at auction.

Official wording
“Is there a waiting period for the redemption of the property by the previous owner? There is not a waiting period once the property has been listed for sale at auction.”

What happens to property that does not sell?

Property unsold at the auction may be auctioned again later, made available for over-the-counter purchase, or managed and disposed of as otherwise allowed under MN Chapter 282.

Official wording
“The property that is unsold at the auction, meaning there are no bids or winning bidders, may be placed up for auction again in the future, made available for over-the-counter purchase, or managed and disposed of as otherwise allowed under MN Chapter 282.”

Source: Auction Frequently Asked Questions | Ramsey County, Minnesota · Ramsey County Productive Properties (Tax-Forfeited Land section) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What happens to money paid above what was owed (surplus)? Statewide

Proceeds above the minimum bid are available to interested parties. Within 60 days of the sale the county auditor must notify them of the surplus with a claim form, and they may claim it if they file within six months from the date the notice is first mailed.

Official wording
“The proceeds in excess of the minimum bid shall be available for distribution pursuant to subdivision 6. ... If a sale under this section results in a surplus, within 60 days of the sale, the county auditor must notify interested parties, in a manner described in subdivision 7, of the surplus by sending notice of the surplus and a claim form to the interested parties. ... Interested parties are entitled to make a claim for surplus proceeds under this subdivision if they file a claim within six months from the date the notice is first mailed to the interested parties.”

Source: Ch. 282 MN Statutes (Minnesota Revisor of Statutes) · Minn. Stat. 282.005, subd. 5, 6 · County auditor · Checked Nov 2, 2026
