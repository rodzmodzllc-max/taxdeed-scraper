How to buy tax-sale property in East Baton Rouge Parish, LA

What the East Baton Rouge Parish offices publish about buying tax-sale property: who runs each sale or list, when and where, and what a buyer must do. Every answer below was read on an official page and links to it. Questions not yet confirmed are listed as such.

Available 13 of 14 answered

Property adjudicated to the parish after no bid at the tax sale, offered through the parish's own process (a dated list, never 'available now').

Step 1 · Find Find the sale or list

Which office handles it and how do I contact them?

Sales of adjudicated property are handled through the Office of the Parish Attorney, telephone (225) 389-3114.

Official wording
“Abandoned adjudicated property sales for the Parish are handled through the Office of the Parish Attorney. Questions concerning the sale process and procedures should be addressed to that office, telephone number (225) 389-3114”

Source: MEMORANDUM - Office of the Parish Attorney, Property Division (purchase of adjudicated property) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What is this list and why is the property available? Statewide

Adjudicated property is property whose tax sale title a political subdivision acquired under R.S. 47:2196 before January 1, 2026.

Official wording
“"Adjudicated property" means property of which tax sale title is acquired by a political subdivision pursuant to R.S. 47:2196 prior to January 1, 2026.”

Source: Louisiana State Legislature - RS 47:2122 · La. R.S. 47:2122 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

How long does a property stay available?

The Parish Attorney will not open a file on property adjudicated for less than three years from recordation of the Act of Adjudication. The tax debtor or owner may redeem the property with the Sheriff at any time before the City-Parish sells it.

Official wording
“the tax debtor/owner of an adjudicated property has the right to redeem that property with the East Baton Rouge Parish Sheriff at any time prior to our sale of the property to you. ... We will not open a file for any property that has been adjudicated for less than three years from the date the Act of Adjudication was recorded.”

Source: City of Baton Rouge / Parish of East Baton Rouge - Office of the Parish Attorney, Adjudicated Property memorandum · Office of the Parish Attorney, Property Division · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 2 · Check Check before you commit

How is the price set?

Offers need not equal the appraised value or the taxes and public liens; the Metropolitan Council considers those amounts when deciding whether to accept or reject the offer.

Official wording
“Offers do not have to equal or exceed either the appraised value or the amount of taxes and public liens. However, these amounts will be considered by the Metropolitan Council at a regularly scheduled meeting in which it will be determined whether to accept or reject your offer.”

Source: MEMORANDUM - Office of the Parish Attorney, Property Division (purchase of adjudicated property) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What does the law or the program tell buyers to check before buying?

The memorandum calls the process complex and strongly advises consulting a private attorney, particularly one handling real estate. The City-Parish keeps no list of adjudicated property; buyers research the property with the Sheriff, the Clerk of Court and the Assessor.

Official wording
“The process of purchasing adjudicated property is complex. YOU ARE STRONGLY ADVISED TO CONSULT A PRIVATE ATTORNEY, PARTICULARLY ONE THAT HANDLES REAL ESTATE TRANSACTIONS. ... The City-Parish does not maintain a roster/ list/ database of properties that are adjudicated. You will need to research the property you are interested in with the East Baton Rouge Parish Sheriff”

Source: City of Baton Rouge / Parish of East Baton Rouge - Office of the Parish Attorney, Adjudicated Property memorandum · Office of the Parish Attorney, Property Division · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 3 · Buy How to buy

How do I buy a property from this list?

First confirm that the property was adjudicated and is still adjudicated to the City-Parish (the City-Parish keeps no list of adjudicated properties), then call the Parish Attorney's Office to confirm it is still available.

Official wording
“you must first determine that the property in which you are interested has in fact been adjudicated, and is still adjudicated, to the City-Parish. The City-Parish does not maintain a roster/ list/ database of properties that are adjudicated. ... you must call the Parish Attorney's Office so that someone in our office can confirm that the property is still currently available”

Source: MEMORANDUM - Office of the Parish Attorney, Property Division (purchase of adjudicated property) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What costs are added to the price?

Advanced costs are at least $500 per property, and some properties have additional costs; this fee is not part of the sale price. The buyer also pays all fees and expenses to record the sale documents.

Official wording
“This amount will be a MINIMUM of $500. However, each individual property may have additional amounts due for costs. ... This fee is not part of the sales price that is ultimately paid to buy the property. ... You are responsible for all fees and expense to record these documents.”

Source: MEMORANDUM - Office of the Parish Attorney, Property Division (purchase of adjudicated property) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What forms or documents are required?

To open a file: a completed 'Request to Purchase Adjudicated Property' form, the two-page tax print and current tax bill from the Sheriff, and a certified check or money order for advanced costs for each property, among other items.

Official wording
“A completed "Request to Purchase Adjudicated Property" form . ... A copy of the two page tax print and the current tax bill for that particular property. You will obtain this from the East Baton Rouge Parish Sheriff. ... A certified check or money order for advanced costs for each piece of property you open a file on.”

Source: MEMORANDUM - Office of the Parish Attorney, Property Division (purchase of adjudicated property) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

How and when do I pay?

Advanced costs are paid by certified check or money order payable to the City of Baton Rouge (no personal checks or cash). The sale price is paid with certified funds at the closing.

Official wording
“NO PERSONAL CHECKS OR CASH can be accepted. Checks must be made payable to the "City of Baton Rouge." ... Please note that you will be required to pay the sales price with certified funds at the time of the sale.”

Source: MEMORANDUM - Office of the Parish Attorney, Property Division (purchase of adjudicated property) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 4 · After After you buy

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive?

The buyer pays the sales price with certified funds at the sale. The City-Parish reserves all mineral rights, with a waiver as to surface rights, and the sale is without any warranty, even as to return of the purchase price.

Official wording
“Please note that you will be required to pay the sales price with certified funds at the time of the sale. Please also note that the City-Parish shall reserve all mineral rights in and to the property conveyed, but with a waiver as to surface rights. The sale shall be without any warranty whatsoever, even as to the return of the purchase price.”

Source: City of Baton Rouge / Parish of East Baton Rouge - Office of the Parish Attorney, Adjudicated Property memorandum · Office of the Parish Attorney, Property Division · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Are sales final or can a purchase be refunded?

The advanced costs are not applied to the purchase price and are not refunded if the Council rejects the offer or the buyer does not complete the sale. They are refunded only if the buyer is not the high bidder or the tax debtor redeems before the sale.

Official wording
“YOUR INITIAL PAYMENT OF COSTS SHALL NOT BE APPLIED TO THE PURCHASE PRICE. YOUR INITIAL CHECK FOR COSTS WILL NOT BE REFUNDED IF THE COUNCIL REJECTS YOUR OFFER, OR IF YOU ELECT NOT TO COMPLETE THE SALE FOR WHATEVER REASON. IT SHALL BE REFUNDED TO YOU ONLY IF: (a) YOU ARE NOT THE HIGH BIDDER, OR (b) IF THE TAX DEBTOR REDEEMS THE PROPERTY BEFORE THE SALE OF THE PROPERTY BY THE PARISH.”

Source: City of Baton Rouge / Parish of East Baton Rouge - Office of the Parish Attorney, Adjudicated Property memorandum · Office of the Parish Attorney, Property Division · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 5 · Risks Risks to weigh

Which liens or interests stay with the property? Statewide

For a sale of adjudicated property, filing the affidavit required by R.S. 47:2208(A) cancels the statutory impositions of all political subdivisions then due, all governmental liens, and the interests, liens, mortgages and other encumbrances recorded against the property and listed in the affidavit.

Official wording
“With respect to a sale of adjudicated property, the filing of the affidavit provided in Subsection A of this Section shall operate as a cancellation, termination, release, or erasure of record of all statutory impositions of all political subdivisions then due and owing, of all governmental liens, and of all interests, liens, mortgages, privileges, and other encumbrances recorded against the property sold and listed in the affidavit.”

Source: Louisiana State Legislature - RS 47:2208 · La. R.S. 47:2208(B) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Is the title insurable or does it need a quiet-title action?

The sale is without any warranty, even as to return of the purchase price. The Parish Attorney's Office gives the buyer a copy of La. R. S. 47:2208 on additional steps the buyer may elect to take regarding the title.

Official wording
“The sale shall be without any warranty whatsoever, even as to the return of the purchase price. ... The Parish Attorney's Office shall provide you a copy of La. R. S. 47:2208 concerning what additional steps you may elect to take regarding the title to the subject property.”

Source: City of Baton Rouge / Parish of East Baton Rouge - Office of the Parish Attorney, Adjudicated Property memorandum · Office of the Parish Attorney, Property Division · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Auctions 11 of 17 answered

Step 1 · Find Find the sale or list

How does a tax sale work here? Statewide

The tax collector advertises the tax lien for sale by public auction and issues a tax lien certificate in favor of the winning bidder, recorded in the mortgage records.

Official wording
“the tax collector will advertise for sale by public auction the tax lien and that the tax collector will issue in favor of the winning bidder and record in the mortgage records a tax lien certificate.”

Source: Louisiana State Legislature - RS 47:2153 · La. R.S. 47:2153 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

When are sales held? Statewide

The tax collector advertises the tax lien auction on or before May first of the year after the taxes were assessed, or as soon as possible after that. Bidding is held on weekdays, opening no earlier than 8:00 a.m. and closing no later than 8:00 p.m.

Official wording
“The tax collector shall advertise for sale by public auction the tax lien on or before May first of the year following the year in which the taxes were assessed, or as soon thereafter as possible. ... The tax lien auction shall be conducted on any weekday, with bidding opening not earlier than 8:00 a.m. and closing no later than 8:00 p.m.”

Source: Louisiana State Legislature - RS 47:2154 · La. R.S. 47:2154 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Where do I bid - online or in person? Statewide

The law allows a tax lien auction to be held online or electronically over several days; bids placed before the auction opens are void.

Official wording
“If a tax lien auction is conducted by using an online or electronic bidding process that is conducted over the course of multiple days, bids may be placed on any day at any time on any tax lien upon which bidding has not closed, provided that all bidding closes on a weekday within the hours prescribed in this Subsection. Any bid received prior to the opening of the auction shall be null and void.”

Source: Louisiana State Legislature - RS 47:2154 · La. R.S. 47:2154 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 2 · Check Check before you commit

What makes up the opening or minimum bid? Statewide

The auction price is the face value of the tax lien certificate. Bidders compete on the monthly interest rate: the maximum is one percent per month, bids go down in steps of one-tenth of one percent, and no bid below seven-tenths of one percent per month is accepted.

Official wording
“The auction price shall be the face value of the tax lien certificate. ... Subject to a maximum bid of one percent per month, bidders may submit bids reducing the monthly interest rate in increments of one-tenth of one percent or an integral multiple thereof. ... no bid shall be accepted that purports to reduce the rate of monthly interest below seven-tenths of one percent.”

Source: Louisiana State Legislature - RS 47:2154 · La. R.S. 47:2154 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 3 · Buy How to buy

Is a deposit required and how much? Statewide

The tax collector may require registered participants to pay a deposit, not to exceed one thousand dollars, before the auction. The winner's deposit is applied to the sale price; other bidders' deposits are returned no later than fourteen days after the auction closes.

Official wording
“The tax collector may require all registered tax lien auction participants to provide a deposit, not to exceed one thousand dollars, prior to the commencement of the tax lien auction. If a deposit is required, the deposit of the winning bidder shall be applied toward the sale price at the time of purchase. A deposit from a nonwinning bidder shall be returned or refunded to the depositor no later than fourteen days after the close of the auction.”

Source: Louisiana State Legislature - RS 47:2153 · La. R.S. 47:2153 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

When and how must the winning bidder pay? Statewide

The statutory advertisement says the sale is for cash or another payment method the tax collector accepts. The law quoted does not state a payment deadline.

Official wording
“At the auction, I will sell the tax lien to the winning bidder. The sale will be for cash or other payment method acceptable to the tax collector, in legal tender money of the United States.”

Source: Louisiana State Legislature - RS 47:2153 · La. R.S. 47:2153 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What costs are added on top of the winning bid? Statewide

The clerk of court's recording cost is included in the price paid at the auction and in the face value of the certificate.

Official wording
“The recording cost due to the clerk of court shall be included in the price paid at the tax lien auction and the face value of the tax lien certificate.”

Source: Louisiana State Legislature - RS 47:2154 · La. R.S. 47:2154 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 4 · After After you buy

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive? Statewide

After the later of three years from recording the tax lien certificate or six months after the required post-auction notices, a certificate holder may sue to have the delinquent obligation and the lien recognized.

Official wording
“Upon the expiration of the later of three years from recordation of the tax lien certificate, or the tax sale certificate if R.S. 47:2201(B) is applicable, or six months after providing the notices required by R.S. 47:2156, a certificate holder may, by verified petition, institute in a court of competent jurisdiction”

Source: Louisiana State Legislature - RS 47:2266.1 · La. R.S. 47:2266.1 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Can the former owner redeem after the sale? Statewide

Anyone may extinguish the tax lien by paying the termination price to the tax collector until close of business on the thirtieth day after the last party is served in the certificate holder's enforcement suit.

Official wording
“Until close of business on the thirtieth day following the date on which the last party was served, or the next day that is not a legal holiday if the thirtieth day is a legal holiday, any person may cause the tax lien to be extinguished by delivering the termination price to the tax collector.”

Source: Louisiana State Legislature - RS 47:2266.1 · La. R.S. 47:2266.1 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What happens to property that does not sell? Statewide

If no one bids, the tax collector issues and records the tax lien certificate in favor of the political subdivision.

Official wording
“If there is no bidder, the tax collector shall issue and record the tax lien certificate in favor of the political subdivision.”

Source: Louisiana State Legislature - RS 47:2154 · La. R.S. 47:2154 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

A judicial sale to enforce the tax lien ends all interests in the property, except rights created by certain written instruments listed in the statute that were filed before the tax lien certificate was recorded.

Liens & Certificates 6 of 11 answered

Step 1 · Find Find the sale or list

What is a tax lien or certificate here? Statewide

At the tax lien auction the winning bidder receives a tax lien certificate, recorded in the parish mortgage records, which is prima facie evidence of the lien and its assignment to the holder.

Official wording
“No later than thirty days after conclusion of the tax lien auction, the tax collector shall issue and file in the mortgage records of the parish in which the property is situated a tax lien certificate in favor of the winning bidder. ... The tax lien certificate shall be prima facie evidence of the validity of the tax lien and the assignment to the person named thereon.”

Source: Louisiana State Legislature - RS 47:2154 · La. R.S. 47:2154 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

  • Which office handles it and how do I contact them?Not yet verified - check with the parish office.

Step 2 · Check Check before you commit

What interest or penalty does the holder earn? Statewide

Interest accrues on the certificate's face value at the monthly rate set by the winning bid, or at one percent per month when the political subdivision holds the certificate, in both cases without compounding.

Official wording
“Upon the issuance of a tax lien certificate, interest shall accrue on the face value of the tax lien certificate at the monthly rate established by the winning bid at the tax lien auction. If the tax lien certificate is issued in favor of the political subdivision, interest shall accrue on the face value of the tax lien certificate at the rate of one percent per month. In both cases, interest shall be calculated on a noncompounding basis.”

Source: Louisiana State Legislature - RS 47:2154 · La. R.S. 47:2154 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

How long does the owner have to redeem? Statewide

The taxes, interest, penalties and costs may be paid at any time until thirty days after service of a petition to enforce the tax lien.

Official wording
“the statutory impositions, together with interest, penalties, and costs, may be paid at any time prior to the expiration of thirty days after service of a petition to enforce the tax lien.”

Source: Louisiana State Legislature - RS 47:2155 · La. R.S. 47:2155 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 3 · Buy How to buy

How do I buy one (auction / over the counter / assignment)? Statewide

Bidding is on the monthly interest rate that will accrue on the certificate's face value; the lowest rate wins, and a tie goes to the bidder who bid first.

Official wording
“The subject of the competitive bidding shall be the monthly rate at which interest will accrue on the face value of the tax lien certificate following its issuance. ... The bid that requires the assessment of interest at the lowest rate shall be declared the winner. If multiple bidders submit the same lowest bid, the winner shall be the first in time to submit the bid.”

Source: Louisiana State Legislature - RS 47:2154 · La. R.S. 47:2154 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 4 · After After you buy

How does a holder get the property if it is not redeemed? Statewide

A judgment for the certificate holder in an enforcement action under R.S. 47:2266.1 is executed by a writ of fieri facias under the Code of Civil Procedure (a judicial sale).

Official wording
“a judgment rendered in favor of the tax lien certificate holder in an action brought pursuant to R.S. 47:2266.1 shall be executed by a writ of fieri facias in accordance with the provisions of Title II of Book IV of the Code of Civil Procedure”

Source: Louisiana State Legislature - RS 47:2267 · La. R.S. 47:2267 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

When does a certificate or lien expire? Statewide

An action to enforce the tax lien must be brought within seven years from recording the tax lien certificate in the parish mortgage records; after that the tax lien is extinguished.

Official wording
“An action to enforce the tax lien pursuant to R.S. 47:2266.1 shall be brought no later than seven years from the date that the tax lien certificate is recorded in the mortgage records of the parish in which the property is located. Upon the expiration of this time period, the tax lien shall be extinguished”

Source: Louisiana State Legislature - RS 47:2155 · La. R.S. 47:2155 · Checked Nov 2, 2026
