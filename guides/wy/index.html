Auctions 12 of 17 answered

The county's annual tax sale: a lien certificate is sold on each listed parcel, not the land.

Step 1 · Find Find the sale or list

How does a tax sale work here?

The county treasurer sells real property for delinquent taxes. The person who offers to pay the taxes, interest, penalties and costs is the purchaser and receives a certificate of purchase from the treasurer.

Official wording
“Any person who offers to pay the amount of taxes, interest, penalties and costs including charges provided by paragraph (ix) of this subsection due on any real property is considered the purchaser thereof. ... the county treasurer upon payment of the fee provided by subparagraph (ix)(B) of this subsection shall make out, sign and deliver a certificate of purchase to the purchaser”

Source: Wyoming Statutes Title 39 - Taxation and Revenue (wyoleg.gov) · W.S. 39-13-108(e)(iii)(B), (C) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

When are sales held?

The sale is advertised once a week for three weeks in a legal newspaper, with the first publication at least four weeks before the sale and before the first week in September. Sales run between 9:00 a.m. and 5:00 p.m., Sundays excluded, and may be adjourned day to day until all lands are sold.

Official wording
“he shall advertise notice of all sales of real property by publication thereof, once a week for three (3) weeks in a legal newspaper in the county, the first publication to be at least four (4) weeks prior to the day of sale and prior to the first week in September. ... between 9:00 a.m. and 5:00 p.m., Sundays excluded, and may be adjourned from day-to-day until all lands are sold”

Source: Wyoming Statutes Title 39 - Taxation and Revenue (wyoleg.gov) · W.S. 39-13-108(e)(ii)(A), (iii)(A) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Where do I bid - online or in person?

Sales of real property are held at the county courthouse or county building.

Official wording
“Sales of real property shall be held at the county courthouse or county building between 9:00 a.m. and 5:00 p.m., Sundays excluded”

Source: Wyoming Statutes Title 39 - Taxation and Revenue (wyoleg.gov) · W.S. 39-13-108(e)(iii)(A) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What makes up the opening or minimum bid?

The amount is the taxes, interest, penalties and costs due on the property, including the statutory charges.

Official wording
“Any person who offers to pay the amount of taxes, interest, penalties and costs including charges provided by paragraph (ix) of this subsection due on any real property is considered the purchaser thereof.”

Source: Wyoming Statutes Title 39 - Taxation and Revenue (wyoleg.gov) · W.S. 39-13-108(e)(iii)(B) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What if the property is occupied?

Before applying for a tax deed, the certificate holder must show that at least three (3) months earlier a notice was served on each person in actual possession or occupancy of the property. A tax deed grantee and successors in title are entitled to possession of the property.

Official wording
“That at least three (3) months prior to the application a written or printed notice was served on each person in actual possession or occupancy of the real property ... Any grantee of a tax deed or county commissioner's deed pursuant to paragraph (iv) or (v) of this subsection, and successors in title are entitled to possession of the real property conveyed by the deed”

Source: Wyoming Statutes Title 39 - Taxation and Revenue (wyoleg.gov) · W.S. 39-13-108(e)(v)(B)(I), (vii)(B) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 3 · Buy How to buy

When and how must the winning bidder pay?

The purchaser must pay the county treasurer all amounts due immediately; otherwise the property is offered again and the original purchaser is disqualified.

Official wording
“The purchaser shall immediately pay the county treasurer all amounts due on the real property in the absence of which the real property shall again be offered for sale and the original purchaser disqualified.”

Source: Wyoming Statutes Title 39 - Taxation and Revenue (wyoleg.gov) · W.S. 39-13-108(e)(iii)(B) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What costs are added on top of the winning bid?

The treasurer collects up to $20.00 for advertising a real property sale and up to $20.00 for issuing a certificate of purchase.

Official wording
“not to exceed twenty dollars ($20.00) for advertising in the case of sale of real property to collect delinquent taxes; (B) Not to exceed twenty dollars ($20.00) for issuing a certificate of purchase”

Source: Wyoming Statutes Title 39 - Taxation and Revenue (wyoleg.gov) · W.S. 39-13-108(e)(ix)(A), (B) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 4 · After After you buy

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive?

The buyer first receives a certificate of purchase. The treasurer issues a tax deed for unredeemed property 4 to 6 years after the sale on proper application; the tax deed is prima facie evidence of title, subject to special assessments for local or public improvements.

Official wording
“The county treasurer shall accept applications and issue tax deeds for unredeemed real property subject to a certificate of purchase not less than four (4) nor more than six (6) years from the date of the original sale ... the deed is prima facie evidence of title to the property described subject to special assessments for local or public improvements.”

Source: Wyoming Statutes Title 39 - Taxation and Revenue (wyoleg.gov) · W.S. 39-13-108(e)(v)(A), (vii)(B) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Can the former owner redeem after the sale?

Yes. The legal owner may redeem after the sale until a valid tax deed application has been filed and accepted by the county treasurer.

Official wording
“Real property sold for delinquent taxes may be redeemed by the legal owner after the date of sale but before a valid tax deed application has been filed and accepted by the county treasurer pursuant to W.S. 39-13-108(e)(v)(A)”

Source: Wyoming Statutes Title 39 - Taxation and Revenue (wyoleg.gov) · W.S. 39-13-109(e)(i) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What happens to property that does not sell?

Property that cannot be sold for the taxes, interest, penalties and costs is bid in for the county. The county commissioners may sell and assign those certificates at public or private sale at any time; after four years the treasurer issues a tax deed to the county for unredeemed certificates it holds.

Official wording
“Any real property which cannot be sold for the amount of taxes, interest, penalties and costs shall be bid in for the county by the county treasurer. ... The county commissioners of any county may sell and assign any certificate of purchase for real property bid in for the county ... at public or private sale at any time.”

Source: Wyoming Statutes Title 39 - Taxation and Revenue (wyoleg.gov) · W.S. 39-13-108(e)(iii)(B), (D), (iv)(A) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 5 · Risks Risks to weigh

Which liens or interests survive the sale?

The certificate holder takes subject to special assessments for local or public improvements. The certificate or tax deed holder's lien is superior to all other liens except those created by junior tax sales or payment of subsequent taxes by another person.

Official wording
“The holder of the certificate of purchase takes subject to any special assessments for local or public improvements. ... The lien is superior to all other liens except those created by junior tax sales or payment of subsequent taxes by another person.”

Source: Wyoming Statutes Title 39 - Taxation and Revenue (wyoleg.gov) · W.S. 39-13-108(e)(iii)(E), (d)(ii) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Is the title insurable or does it need a quiet-title action?

A tax deed or county commissioner's deed is prima facie evidence of title to the property, subject to special assessments for local or public improvements. Anyone seeking to invalidate that title carries the burden of proof.

Official wording
“the deed is prima facie evidence of title to the property described subject to special assessments for local or public improvements. The burden of proof shall be upon any party seeking to invalidate title conveyed by a tax or county commissioner's deed in any action in any court in Wyoming”

Source: Wyoming Statutes Title 39 - Taxation and Revenue (wyoleg.gov) · W.S. 39-13-108(e)(vii)(B) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Liens & Certificates 6 of 11 answered

A tax lien certificate sold at the county tax sale; the certificate, not the land.

Step 1 · Find Find the sale or list

What is a tax lien or certificate here?

The tax sale buyer receives a certificate of purchase describing the property, the taxes and costs paid and any special assessments. Certificates may be assigned by endorsement.

Official wording
“The certificate of purchase shall describe the real property purchased, taxes and costs paid and shall state the amount of any special assessments for local or public improvements. Certificates of purchase may be assigned by endorsement”

Source: Wyoming Statutes Title 39 - Taxation and Revenue (wyoleg.gov) · W.S. 39-13-108(e)(iii)(C) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 2 · Check Check before you commit

What interest or penalty does the holder earn?

On redemption the holder receives the sale amount plus 3%, plus interest at 15% per year since the sale for property sold at the 1982 tax sale and later; subsequent taxes the holder paid also earn 15% per year.

Official wording
“The amount for which the property was sold at the tax sale ... plus: (I) Three percent (3%); plus (II) Interest at eight percent (8%) per annum since the date of sale except fifteen percent (15%) per annum on all property sold at date of 1982 tax sale and thereafter.”

Source: Wyoming Statutes Title 39 - Taxation and Revenue (wyoleg.gov) · W.S. 39-13-109(e)(iv)(A) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

How long does the owner have to redeem?

The legal owner may redeem after the sale until a valid tax deed application has been filed and accepted; the treasurer accepts tax deed applications no less than four years after the sale.

Official wording
“may be redeemed by the legal owner after the date of sale but before a valid tax deed application has been filed and accepted by the county treasurer ... accept applications and issue tax deeds for unredeemed real property subject to a certificate of purchase not less than four (4) nor more than six (6) years from the date of the original sale”

Source: Wyoming Statutes Title 39 - Taxation and Revenue (wyoleg.gov) · W.S. 39-13-109(e)(i); 39-13-108(e)(v)(A) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 4 · After After you buy

How does a holder get the property if it is not redeemed?

The holder applies to the treasurer, returns the certificate of purchase, pays the fees and proves the required notice was given - including a notice served at least three (3) months before the application on each person in possession and on the person in whose name the property was taxed.

Official wording
“upon proper application, return of the certificate of purchase, payment of fees and proof of compliance with the notice requirements of this section ... That at least three (3) months prior to the application a written or printed notice was served on each person in actual possession or occupancy of the real property and the person in whose name the property was taxed”

Source: Wyoming Statutes Title 39 - Taxation and Revenue (wyoleg.gov) · W.S. 39-13-108(e)(v)(A), (B) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

When does a certificate or lien expire?

The treasurer accepts tax deed applications no more than six years after the original sale. An action to enforce the certificate holder's lien may be started no earlier than four and no later than ten years after the sale.

Official wording
“not less than four (4) nor more than six (6) years from the date of the original sale for taxes ... No action provided by this section may be commenced less than four (4) years nor more than ten (10) years from the date of the original tax sale.”

Source: Wyoming Statutes Title 39 - Taxation and Revenue (wyoleg.gov) · W.S. 39-13-108(e)(v)(A); 39-13-110(c) · Checked Nov 2, 2026
