How to buy tax-sale property in York County, SC

Available 9 of 14 answered

Forfeited Land Commission property: parcels bid in by the county at the tax sale, offered after the redemption period.

Step 1 · Find Find the sale or list

What is this list and why is the property available? Statewide

These are lands bid in for the county forfeited land commission at the delinquent tax sale. The commission holds them as assets of the county and sells them, and it may also assign its bids before the tax deed is made.

Official wording
“All lands deeded to the forfeited land commission of any county shall be held by it as assets of the county and sold to the best interest of the county. ... The forfeited land commission may assign its bids at any time before title deed being made pursuant to sale”

Source: Code of Laws - Title 12 - Chapter 59 - TAXATION (South Carolina Code of Laws) · S.C. Code 12-59-40; 12-59-80 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

How long does a property stay available? Statewide

Forfeited land commission sales may not give more than ten years for full payment of the purchase price. The commission may assign its bids at any time before the title deed is made.

Official wording
“the terms of sale shall not in any case provide for a longer term than ten years for the full payment of the purchase price of such property and shall be secured by a first real estate mortgage upon the property sold. ... The forfeited land commission may assign its bids at any time before title deed being made pursuant to sale”

Source: Code of Laws - Title 12 - Chapter 59 - TAXATION (South Carolina Code of Laws) · S.C. Code 12-59-40; 12-59-80 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

An assignment of a commission bid may not be for less than the taxes, penalties and costs for which the property was sold.

Official wording
“provided the consideration to be paid for such assignments shall not be less than the amount of taxes, penalties and costs for which the property was sold.”

Step 3 · Buy How to buy

How do I buy a property from this list? Statewide

For assignments of the commission's bids, the chairman may accept sealed bids for a set period; assignments not made then may be made first come, first served. The commission keeps a list of available properties at a location it chooses.

Official wording
“The chairman or his designee may accept sealed bids for assignments of the forfeited land commission bids for a designated time period. Assignments not made during this time may then be assigned on a first come, first served basis. A list of available forfeited land commission properties is to be maintained at an assigned location as determined by the county forfeited land commission.”

Source: Code of Laws - Title 12 - Chapter 59 - TAXATION (South Carolina Code of Laws) · S.C. Code 12-59-80 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What costs are added to the price? Statewide

The purchaser, or the assignee of the Forfeited Land Commission's bid, pays the actual cost of preparing the tax title plus the documentary stamps and recording fees, before the tax title is delivered for recording.

Official wording
“The successful purchaser, or assignee, is responsible for the actual cost of preparing the tax title plus documentary stamps necessary to be affixed and recording fees. The successful purchaser, or assignee, shall pay the amounts to the person officially charged with the collection of delinquent taxes before delivery of the tax title to the clerk of court or register of deeds”

Source: South Carolina Code of Laws, Title 12, Chapter 51 - Alternate Procedure for Collection of Property Taxes · S.C. Code 12-51-130 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What forms or documents are required? Statewide

The commission's chairman or designee may accept sealed bids for assignments of the commission's bids for a set period; assignments not made then may be made first come, first served. A list of available commission properties is kept at a location the county commission chooses.

Official wording
“The chairman or his designee may accept sealed bids for assignments of the forfeited land commission bids for a designated time period. Assignments not made during this time may then be assigned on a first come, first served basis. A list of available forfeited land commission properties is to be maintained at an assigned location as determined by the county forfeited land commission.”

Source: Code of Laws - Title 12 - Chapter 59 - TAXATION (South Carolina Code of Laws) · S.C. Code 12-59-80 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

How and when do I pay? Statewide

Terms are set by the commission, but any terms may not run longer than ten years for full payment and must be secured by a first real estate mortgage on the property.

Official wording
“but the terms of sale shall not in any case provide for a longer term than ten years for the full payment of the purchase price of such property and shall be secured by a first real estate mortgage upon the property sold.”

Source: Code of Laws - Title 12 - Chapter 59 - TAXATION (South Carolina Code of Laws) · S.C. Code 12-59-40 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 4 · After After you buy

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive? Statewide

Deeds for land the commission sells are made by the forfeited land commission of the county holding title, or by a majority of its members.

Official wording
“All deeds for lands sold under the authority of Section 12-59-40 shall be made by the forfeited land commission of the county holding title thereto or by a majority of the members thereof”

Source: Code of Laws - Title 12 - Chapter 59 - TAXATION (South Carolina Code of Laws) · S.C. Code 12-59-90 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 5 · Risks Risks to weigh

Is the title insurable or does it need a quiet-title action? Statewide

A tax sale deed, including one made to a forfeited land commission, is prima facie evidence of good title and of regular proceedings; an action to recover the land must be brought within two years from the date of sale.

Official wording
“In all cases of tax sale the deed of conveyance, whether executed to a private person, a corporation, or a forfeited land commission, is prima facie evidence of a good title in the holder, that all proceedings have been regular and that all legal requirements have been complied with. An action for the recovery of land sold pursuant to this chapter or for the recovery of the possession must not be maintained unless brought within two years from the date of sale as provided in Section 12-51-90(C).”

Source: South Carolina Code of Laws, Title 12, Chapter 51 - Alternate Procedure for Collection of Property Taxes · S.C. Code 12-51-160 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

A county tax sale of the property, subject to the former owner's redemption period.

Step 1 · Find Find the sale or list

How does a tax sale work here? Statewide

Real estate whose property taxes are delinquent is advertised and sold at public auction by the person officially charged with the collection of delinquent taxes. The advertisement is titled "Delinquent Tax Sale".

Official wording
“The property duly advertised must be sold, by the person officially charged with the collection of delinquent taxes, at public auction at the courthouse or other convenient place within the county ... The property must be advertised for sale at public auction. The advertisement must be in a newspaper of general circulation within the county or municipality, if applicable, and must be entitled "Delinquent Tax Sale".”

Source: Code of Laws - Title 12 - Chapter 51 - TAXATION (South Carolina Code of Laws) · S.C. Code 12-51-50; 12-51-40(d) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What makes up the opening or minimum bid?

The opening bid is made on behalf of the Forfeited Land Commission and consists of all delinquent taxes, penalties and costs plus the current year taxes.

Official wording
“Bidding: The opening bid, which is made on behalf of the Forfeited Land Commission consist of all delinquent taxes, penalties and cost, plus the current year taxes.”

Source: York County Tax Sale Fact Sheet (Tax Collection Office) · York County Tax Collection Office · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What if the property is occupied?

During the redemption period the successful bidder has no ownership rights and no right to enter the premises or contact the owner.

Official wording
“During the redemption period, successful bidders have no ownership rights to the property and have no right to enter the premises or contact the owner.”

Source: York County Tax Sale Fact Sheet (Tax Collection Office) · York County Tax Collection Office · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What does the law or the county tell buyers to check before buying?

There are no guarantees at a tax sale and bidding is at the bidder's own risk; the county makes no warranty or representation about the property, its value, liens or judgments, its condition or the quality of the title.

Official wording
“There are no guarantees when purchasing properties at any tax sale – bidding is at your own risk. ... York County makes no warranty and no representation whatsoever regarding the property or its value, the status of any liens or judgments, the physical characteristics, the condition of the property, the number or condition of any buildings, any defects relating to the quality of the title, or any other matter pertaining to the property or its value”

Source: York County Tax Sale Fact Sheet (Tax Collection Office) · York County Tax Collection Office · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 3 · Buy How to buy

When and how must the winning bidder pay?

All bids must be paid in full on the day of the sale, by certified funds, cash or business check. All sales are final.

Official wording
“Payments: All sales are final, with no exceptions. Payments can be made by certified funds, cash or business check. ... All bids must be paid in full on the day of the sale”

Source: Tax Sale Fact Sheet and Disclaimer (York County, SC) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What costs are added on top of the winning bid?

Successful bidders pay all recording costs, including deed stamps.

Official wording
“Successful bidders are responsible for all recording costs, including deed stamps.(Section 12-51-130)”

Step 4 · After After you buy

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive? Statewide

If the property is not redeemed, the collector makes a tax title to the purchaser. The tax deed is prima facie evidence of good title, and after a further twelve months it is incontestable on procedural or other grounds.

Official wording
“shall make a tax title to the purchaser or the purchaser's assignee. ... the tax deed issued is incontestable on procedural or other grounds. ... the deed of conveyance, whether executed to a private person, a corporation, or a forfeited land commission, is prima facie evidence of a good title in the holder”

Source: Code of Laws - Title 12 - Chapter 51 - TAXATION (South Carolina Code of Laws) · S.C. Code 12-51-130; 12-51-90(C); 12-51-160 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Can the former owner redeem after the sale?

The defaulting taxpayer, a grantee or any mortgage or judgment creditor may redeem within twelve months from the day of the tax sale; the bidder is then refunded the bid amount plus interest at 12% pro-rated quarterly.

Official wording
“On all sales, the defaulting taxpayer, any grantee for the owner or any mortgage or judgment creditor may redeem the property within twelve months from the day of the tax sale. ... If a property is redeemed, the successful bidder will receive a refund for the bid amount plus interest on the bid amount at the rate of 12% pro-rated quarterly.”

Source: York County Tax Sale Fact Sheet (Tax Collection Office) · York County Tax Collection Office · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What happens to property that does not sell? Statewide

The opening bid is made on behalf of the county forfeited land commission. The commission sells the lands bid in for it, on terms it decides are in the best interest of the county.

Official wording
“shall effect the sale of lands forfeited and bid in for the various forfeited land commissions of the State by the county auditors or the tax collectors of the several counties of the State in pursuance of Section 12-51-55. All lands deeded to the forfeited land commission of any county shall be held by it as assets of the county and sold to the best interest of the county.”

Source: Code of Laws - Title 12 - Chapter 59 - TAXATION (South Carolina Code of Laws) · S.C. Code 12-59-40 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What happens to money paid above what was owed (surplus)? Statewide

If the tax sale brings more than the taxes, assessments, penalties and costs due, the overage is applied first to any outstanding municipal tax liens; the rest belongs to the owner of record immediately before the end of the redemption period. The sums are payable ninety days after the deed is executed, and an overage neither claimed nor assigned within five years escheats to the governing body's general fund.

Official wording
“If the tax sale of an item produced more cash than the full amount due in taxes, assessments, penalties, and costs, the overage must be applied to any outstanding municipal tax liens on the property. Any remaining overage belongs to the owner of record immediately before the end of the redemption period ... These sums are payable ninety days after execution of the deed ... If neither claimed nor assigned within five years of date of public auction tax sale, the overage shall escheat to the general fund of the governing body.”

Source: Code of Laws - Title 12 - Chapter 51 - TAXATION (South Carolina Code of Laws) · S.C. Code 12-51-130 · Person officially charged with the collection of delinquent taxes · Checked Nov 2, 2026
