How to buy tax-sale property in Horry County, SC

What the Horry County offices publish about buying tax-sale property: who runs each sale or list, when and where, and what a buyer must do. Every answer below was read on an official page and links to it. Questions not yet confirmed are listed as such.

Auctions

A county tax sale of the property, subject to the former owner's redemption period.

Which office handles it and how do I contact them?

Questions about properties sold at the tax sale go to the Horry County Delinquent Tax Department at 843-915-5472.

“For questions concerning properties sold at the tax sale, please contact the Horry County Delinquent Tax Department at 843-915-5472.”

Source: Delinquent Tax - Horry County SC.Gov · verified 2026-11-02

How does a tax sale work here? Statewide

Real estate whose property taxes are delinquent is advertised and sold at public auction by the person officially charged with the collection of delinquent taxes. The advertisement is titled "Delinquent Tax Sale".

“The property duly advertised must be sold, by the person officially charged with the collection of delinquent taxes, at public auction at the courthouse or other convenient place within the county ... The property must be advertised for sale at public auction. The advertisement must be in a newspaper of general circulation within the county or municipality, if applicable, and must be entitled "Delinquent Tax Sale".”

Source: Code of Laws - Title 12 - Chapter 51 - TAXATION (South Carolina Code of Laws) · S.C. Code 12-51-50; 12-51-40(d) · verified 2026-11-02

When are sales held?

The tax sale's time, date and location are listed in a newspaper advertisement in November; the delinquent real estate and mobile homes are advertised for three consecutive Thursdays before the sale.

“Advertisement of delinquent real estate and mobile homes will be listed in a paper of general circulation within the county for three consecutive Thursdays prior to the tax sale. Tax Sale is a sale of distressed property for default on delinquent taxes held. Tax Sale time, date and location will be listed in newspaper advertisement in November.”

Source: Delinquent Tax - Horry County SC.Gov · Horry County Delinquent Tax Department · verified 2026-11-02

Where do I bid - online or in person?

In person, at the Horry County Government and Judicial Building in Conway. It is an open bid sale using numbered bidder cards.

“Location: Horry County Government and Judicial Building, 1301 2nd Ave, Conway SC 29526 ... This is an open bid sale, and bidders must use their numbered bidder cards to place a bid.”

Source: Treasurer of Horry County, Delinquent Tax Department - December 1, 2026, Tax Sale (2026 Tax Sale Information) · verified 2026-11-02

How do I register to bid?

Online registration ends at 5:00 PM on Friday, November 20, 2026; in-person registration ends at 4:00 PM on Monday, November 30, 2026. There is no registration on the morning of the sale, and the fee is $25.00.

“Registration: Online bidder registration is now open and will end at 5:00 PM, Friday, November 20, 2026. In person registration will end at 4:00 PM, Monday, November 30, 2026. Registration will not be allowed on the morning of the tax sale, Tuesday, December 1, 2026. There is a $25.00 bidder registration fee.”

Source: Treasurer of Horry County, Delinquent Tax Department - December 1, 2026, Tax Sale (2026 Tax Sale Information) · verified 2026-11-02

What makes up the opening or minimum bid? Statewide

The officer conducting the sale submits a bid on behalf of the forfeited land commission equal to all unpaid property taxes, penalties, assessments and costs, including the taxes levied for the year in which the redemption period begins.

“shall submit a bid on behalf of the forfeited land commission equal to the amount of all unpaid property taxes, penalties, assessments including, but not limited to, assessments owed to a special taxing district established pursuant to Section 4-9-30, Chapter 19, Title 4, or an assessment district established pursuant to Chapter 15, Title 6, and costs including taxes levied for the year in which the redemption period begins.”

Source: Code of Laws - Title 12 - Chapter 51 - TAXATION (South Carolina Code of Laws) · S.C. Code 12-51-55 · verified 2026-11-02

When and how must the winning bidder pay?

All bids must be paid at the close of the sale, in cash or certified funds only. Personal or business checks and wire transfers are not accepted, and all sales are final.

“All sales are final, with no exceptions. Terms of sale are cash or certified funds only. No personal/Business checks or wire transfers will be accepted. Any bids paid in cash over $10,000.00 will be reported to IRS. All bids must be paid at the close of the sale.”

Source: Treasurer of Horry County, Delinquent Tax Department - December 1, 2026, Tax Sale (2026 Tax Sale Information) · verified 2026-11-02

What costs are added on top of the winning bid?

A $450.00 title search and deed preparation charge is added to each property sold. If the property is not redeemed, the bidder is notified of recording costs and deed stamps.

“A $450.00 title search and deed prep charge will be added to each property sold. ... Tax Title: If the property is not redeemed, successful bidders will be notified of recording costs and deed stamps.”

Source: Treasurer of Horry County, Delinquent Tax Department - December 1, 2026, Tax Sale (2026 Tax Sale Information) · verified 2026-11-02

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive? Statewide

If the property is not redeemed, the collector makes a tax title to the purchaser. The tax deed is prima facie evidence of good title, and after a further twelve months it is incontestable on procedural or other grounds.

“shall make a tax title to the purchaser or the purchaser's assignee. ... the tax deed issued is incontestable on procedural or other grounds. ... the deed of conveyance, whether executed to a private person, a corporation, or a forfeited land commission, is prima facie evidence of a good title in the holder”

Source: Code of Laws - Title 12 - Chapter 51 - TAXATION (South Carolina Code of Laws) · S.C. Code 12-51-130; 12-51-90(C); 12-51-160 · verified 2026-11-02

Can the former owner redeem after the sale?

The owner, mortgage company or grantee has 12 months from the date of the sale to pay the taxes and the costs of the sale and regain ownership.

“Redemption period is a period of time allowed by the law (12 months from the date of the sale) for the owner, mortgage company or grantee to pay the taxes and cost associated with the sale of the property to regain ownership.”

Source: Delinquent Tax - Horry County SC.Gov · Horry County Delinquent Tax Department · verified 2026-11-02

Is the title insurable or does it need a quiet-title action?

Each property is sold as is, and Horry County makes no warranty about the quality of the title or any liens, including other tax liens. Bidders bid at their own risk.

“Conditions of Sale: You bid at your own risk. Each property is sold “as is”. Horry County makes no warranty regarding the value of the property, its physical characteristics, and any improvements on the property, the quality of the title, or any liens, including other tax liens, on the property”

Source: Treasurer of Horry County, Delinquent Tax Department - December 1, 2026, Tax Sale · Horry County Delinquent Tax Department · verified 2026-11-02

What if the property is occupied?

During the 12-month redemption period, successful bidders have no ownership rights to the property and cannot enter the premises or contact the owner.

“Ownership Rights: During the 12-month redemption period, successful bidders have no ownership rights to the property, cannot enter the premises or contact the owner.”

Source: Treasurer of Horry County, Delinquent Tax Department - December 1, 2026, Tax Sale · Horry County Delinquent Tax Department · verified 2026-11-02

What happens to property that does not sell? Statewide

The opening bid is made on behalf of the county forfeited land commission. The commission sells the lands bid in for it, on terms it decides are in the best interest of the county.

“shall effect the sale of lands forfeited and bid in for the various forfeited land commissions of the State by the county auditors or the tax collectors of the several counties of the State in pursuance of Section 12-51-55. All lands deeded to the forfeited land commission of any county shall be held by it as assets of the county and sold to the best interest of the county.”

Source: Code of Laws - Title 12 - Chapter 59 - TAXATION (South Carolina Code of Laws) · S.C. Code 12-59-40 · verified 2026-11-02

What happens to money paid above what was owed (surplus)? Statewide

If the tax sale brings more than the taxes, assessments, penalties and costs due, the overage is applied first to any outstanding municipal tax liens; the rest belongs to the owner of record immediately before the end of the redemption period. The sums are payable ninety days after the deed is executed, and an overage neither claimed nor assigned within five years escheats to the governing body's general fund.

“If the tax sale of an item produced more cash than the full amount due in taxes, assessments, penalties, and costs, the overage must be applied to any outstanding municipal tax liens on the property. Any remaining overage belongs to the owner of record immediately before the end of the redemption period ... These sums are payable ninety days after execution of the deed ... If neither claimed nor assigned within five years of date of public auction tax sale, the overage shall escheat to the general fund of the governing body.”

Source: Code of Laws - Title 12 - Chapter 51 - TAXATION (South Carolina Code of Laws) · S.C. Code 12-51-130 · Person officially charged with the collection of delinquent taxes · verified 2026-11-02

What does the law or the county tell buyers to check before buying?

The county strongly urges bidders to research all properties before bidding and points to its GIS Online Mapping and Property Search tool for location and characteristics. The guidance is not legal advice.

“we strongly urge bidders to research all properties before placing bids. The Horry County website, www.horrycountysc.gov, provides a research tool, GIS Online Mapping and Property Search, which provides information on location and characteristics. ... This information is provided as guidance only and does not constitute legal advice of any kind.”

Source: Treasurer of Horry County, Delinquent Tax Department - December 1, 2026, Tax Sale · Horry County Delinquent Tax Department · verified 2026-11-02

Available

Forfeited Land Commission property: parcels bid in by the county at the tax sale, offered after the redemption period.

Which office handles it and how do I contact them?

Questions about properties sold at the tax sale go to the Horry County Delinquent Tax Department at 843-915-5472.

“For questions concerning properties sold at the tax sale, please contact the Horry County Delinquent Tax Department at 843-915-5472.”

Source: Delinquent Tax - Horry County SC.Gov · verified 2026-11-02

What is this list and why is the property available? Statewide

These are lands bid in for the county forfeited land commission at the delinquent tax sale. The commission holds them as assets of the county and sells them, and it may also assign its bids before the tax deed is made.

“All lands deeded to the forfeited land commission of any county shall be held by it as assets of the county and sold to the best interest of the county. ... The forfeited land commission may assign its bids at any time before title deed being made pursuant to sale”

Source: Code of Laws - Title 12 - Chapter 59 - TAXATION (South Carolina Code of Laws) · S.C. Code 12-59-40; 12-59-80 · verified 2026-11-02

How do I buy a property from this list?

Fill out a separate FLC bid form for each property and submit it sealed, in person at the Horry County Assessor's Office in Conway or by certified mail marked "Confidential -FLC Bid", with the PIN on the outside. Do not include payment; the winning bidder is contacted with payment instructions.

“Bids should be submitted in person to the Horry County Assessor's Office in Conway, SC or sent via certified mail in an envelope marked "Confidential -FLC Bid". Please include the PIN number on the OUTSIDE of your sealed bid envelope. Please do NOT include payment with your bid. The winning bidder will be contacted and provided with payment instructions at that time. ... A separate offer form must be filled out for each property that you wish to make an offer on.”

Source: Horry County Forfeited Lands Commission - FLC Bid Form · Horry County Forfeited Land Commission · verified 2026-11-02

How is the price set?

The minimum bid is the past and current taxes due, penalties and costs, plus 15% administrative fees set by the FLC. Offers above or below it may be made; the FLC may accept, decline or offer a compromise price.

“The FLC may accept a bid, decline a bid, or offer a compromise sale price. The minimum bid amount is determined by the amount of past and current taxes due, any penalties and costs, plus 15% additional administrative fees as defined by the FLC.”

Source: Horry County Forfeited Lands Commission - FLC Bid Form · verified 2026-11-02

What costs are added to the price? Statewide

The purchaser, or the assignee of the Forfeited Land Commission's bid, pays the actual cost of preparing the tax title plus the documentary stamps and recording fees, before the tax title is delivered for recording.

“The successful purchaser, or assignee, is responsible for the actual cost of preparing the tax title plus documentary stamps necessary to be affixed and recording fees. The successful purchaser, or assignee, shall pay the amounts to the person officially charged with the collection of delinquent taxes before delivery of the tax title to the clerk of court or register of deeds”

Source: South Carolina Code of Laws, Title 12, Chapter 51 - Alternate Procedure for Collection of Property Taxes · S.C. Code 12-51-130 · verified 2026-11-02

What forms or documents are required?

The FLC Bid Form, one per property, including either the Real Estate Property Identification Number or the Mobile Home Decal Number.

“Each offer form must include EITHER the Mobile Home Decal Number OR the Real Estate Property Identification Number. A separate offer form must be filled out for each property that you wish to make an offer on.”

Source: Horry County Forfeited Lands Commission - FLC Bid Form · verified 2026-11-02

How and when do I pay?

Do not send payment with the bid. The winning bidder is contacted and given payment instructions.

“Please do NOT include payment with your bid. The winning bidder will be contacted and provided with payment instructions at that time.”

Source: Horry County Forfeited Lands Commission - FLC Bid Form · verified 2026-11-02

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive? Statewide

Deeds for land the commission sells are made by the forfeited land commission of the county holding title, or by a majority of its members.

“All deeds for lands sold under the authority of Section 12-59-40 shall be made by the forfeited land commission of the county holding title thereto or by a majority of the members thereof”

Source: Code of Laws - Title 12 - Chapter 59 - TAXATION (South Carolina Code of Laws) · S.C. Code 12-59-90 · verified 2026-11-02

How long does a property stay available? Statewide

Forfeited land commission sales may not give more than ten years for full payment of the purchase price. The commission may assign its bids at any time before the title deed is made.

“the terms of sale shall not in any case provide for a longer term than ten years for the full payment of the purchase price of such property and shall be secured by a first real estate mortgage upon the property sold. ... The forfeited land commission may assign its bids at any time before title deed being made pursuant to sale”

Source: Code of Laws - Title 12 - Chapter 59 - TAXATION (South Carolina Code of Laws) · S.C. Code 12-59-40; 12-59-80 · verified 2026-11-02

Is the title insurable or does it need a quiet-title action?

Forfeited Land Commission property is offered as is, where is; the deed is a quit claim deed transferring whatever interest the commission may have acquired, and no warranty of any kind is offered.

“All property offered for sale by the Forfeited Land Commission of Horry County is offered on an "AS IS, WHERE IS" basis meaning that the deed transferring the property will be a "Quit Claim Deed" transferring whatever interest the Commission may have acquired. ... NO WARRANTY of any kind is offered to the purchase r.”

Source: Horry County Forfeited Lands Commission - FLC Bid Form · Horry County Forfeited Land Commission · verified 2026-11-02

What does the law or the program tell buyers to check before buying?

It is solely the purchaser's responsibility to determine whether the property is usable, for example under local planning and zoning regulations.

“It is solely the responsibility of the purchaser to determine whether or not the property is usable (local planning and zoning regulations, etc.)”

Source: Horry County Forfeited Lands Commission - FLC Bid Form · Horry County Forfeited Land Commission · verified 2026-11-02
