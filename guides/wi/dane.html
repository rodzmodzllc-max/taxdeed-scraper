How to buy tax-sale property in Dane County, WI

What the Dane County offices publish about buying tax-sale property: who runs each sale or list, when and where, and what a buyer must do. Every answer below was read on an official page and links to it. Questions not yet confirmed are listed as such.

Auctions

A county sale of tax-deeded land by bid on the county's form.

Which office handles it and how do I contact them?

Dane County Treasurer's Office, 210 Martin Luther King Jr. Blvd, room 114, Madison; treasurer@danecounty.gov. The office says it cannot give strategic advice or help prepare bids.

“The Bid Opening will be held in the Dane County Treasurer's Office (210 Martin Luther King Jr. Blvd, room 114, Madison, Wisconsin, 53703) ... A walk-through can be scheduled by emailing treasurer@danecounty.gov . ... IMPORTANT: Do not call the Treasurer's Office for sale information. Staff cannot provide strategic advice or assist in bid preparation in any way.”

Source: Tax Deed Details | Dane County Treasurer Office · verified 2026-11-02

When are sales held?

Auctions are regularly held in October/November, with one in April/May as needed. Bids are due Tuesday at 1:00pm and opened Wednesday at 10:00am.

“Auctions are regularly held in October/November. There may be one in April/May as needed. ... Bid Due date & time: Tuesday at 1:00pm, day before Bid Opening ... Bid Opening date & time: Wednesday at 10:00am, day after Bid Due”

Source: Tax Deed Details | Dane County Treasurer Office · verified 2026-11-02

Where do I bid - online or in person?

By sealed bid, using the "Bid Form" button next to each parcel on the Tax Deed Auction page; bids are opened at the Treasurer's Office.

“Auctions are conducted through a sealed bid. The Bid Due date is the day before the Bid Opening. ... Bids can be accepted using the "Bid Form" button next to the specific parcel.”

Source: Tax Deed Details | Dane County Treasurer Office · verified 2026-11-02

How do I register to bid?

No registration is required to submit a bid.

“No registration required to submit a bid.”

Source: Tax Deed Details | Dane County Treasurer Office · verified 2026-11-02

Is a deposit required and how much?

Yes: an earnest deposit of 10% of the bid, in cash or cashier's check, must be in the sealed envelope with each bid form.

“All payments must be in the form of cash or cashiers check. Auction Bidder: Earnest Deposit (10% of the bid) must accompany each completed corresponding bid form within the sealed envelope”

Source: Tax Deed Details | Dane County Treasurer Office · verified 2026-11-02

What makes up the opening or minimum bid?

The sale notice lists each parcel's appraised value and the date it becomes available; the treasurer sells each parcel to the highest bidder whose unconditional bid equals or exceeds the advertised appraised value.

“The treasurer will publish a notice for the sale of tax-deeded lands, describing each parcel for sale, its appraised value, and the date on and after which the parcel will be available for purchase. ... the treasurer shall sell each parcel to the highest bidder whose unconditional bid is equal to or exceeds the advertised appraised value of the parcel.”

Source: Tax Deeded Land Sales - Access Dane · Dane County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

When and how must the winning bidder pay?

The balance of the bid must reach the Treasurer's Office within ten days of receiving the award letter, in cash or cashier's check. On default the 10% earnest money is kept and the next bidder is offered the parcel.

“Successful Bidder: Balance of bid must be received by the Dane County Treasurer's Office within ten (10) days of receiving the bid award notification letter. IMPORTANT: No payment arrangements, personal or business checks, credit or debit card transactions, ACH, or wire payments. ... The 10% earnest money will be kept by Dane County.”

Source: Tax Deed Details | Dane County Treasurer Office · verified 2026-11-02

What costs are added on top of the winning bid?

Depending on when the property is bought, the purchaser may have to pay the most recent tax year's taxes and specials to the municipality (for example, December - January purchases, and Madison parcels).

“December - January: Successful Purchaser will pay in-full the most recent taxes and any associated specials to the municipality (city/village/town). ... Madison parcels: Successful Purchaser will pay in-full the most recent taxes and any associated specials, interest, and penalty to Madison”

Source: Tax Deed Details | Dane County Treasurer Office · verified 2026-11-02

Can the former owner redeem after the sale?

Former owners have the right to repurchase until the public auction; in Dane County a repurchase is possible up to 2 weeks before the auction Bid Due date.

“State law provides former owners the right to repurchase until public auction. ... Repurchasing tax-deeded property to avoid public auction is possible up to 2 weeks prior to the auction Bid Due date”

Source: Tax Deed Details | Dane County Treasurer Office · Dane County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

What if the property is occupied?

Occupants may be using parcels at the time of a fall auction; parcels are quit-claimed to the successful bidder as-is, with or without occupants, and no walk-through is provided for parcels with occupants.

“Occupants may be using available parcels at the time of a Fall auction. State law provides former owners the right to repurchase until public auction. Available parcels are quit-claimed to the successful bidders "as-is," with or without occupants. ... Parcels with occupants: No walk-through will be provided.”

Source: Tax Deed Details | Dane County Treasurer Office · Dane County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

What happens to property that does not sell?

A parcel not sold by bid on the first sale date may be sold by the treasurer at any time afterwards to anyone offering at or above the appraised value; offers go to the Treasurer's Office in writing, no earlier than the day after the bid opening.

“a parcel is not sold by bid at the first date offered for sale, the treasurer may sell the parcel at anytime thereafter to any person making an offer at or exceeding the appraised value. ... To make an offer after the sealed bid auction date, contact the Dane County Treasurer’s Office in writing (an email to treasurer@danecounty.gov is acceptable) no earlier than the day after the bid opening.”

Source: Tax Deed Details | Dane County Treasurer Office · Dane County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

What happens to money paid above what was owed (surplus)?

After a sale the county returns any net proceeds to the former owner, on or about the time of the next auction (typically October and April); unclaimed net proceeds go into the Unclaimed Funds process.

“The county will return any net proceeds to the former owner upon sale. Any net proceeds would be returned on or about the timing of the subsequent auction after sale (typically October & April). If net proceeds go unclaimed, the net proceeds will be transferred into the Unclaimed Funds process.”

Source: Tax Deed Details | Dane County Treasurer Office · Dane County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

What does the law or the county tell buyers to check before buying?

The county puts the research on the bidder: investigate the property before bidding, check with the municipality for outstanding assessments or charges not on a tax bill, identify current-year taxes being collected by the municipality, and research anything not in the advertisement.

“It is the bidder’s responsibility to: Investigate property details prior to putting in a bid to Dane County Check with the municipality for any outstanding assessments or charges not on a tax bill Identify current tax year amounts to pay if being collected by the municipality ( see Delinquencies: who pays? below ) Research any other additional information not included in the advertisement”

Source: Tax Deed Details | Dane County Treasurer Office · Dane County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02
