How to buy tax-sale property in Oscoda County, MI

What the Oscoda County offices publish about buying tax-sale property: who runs each sale or list, when and where, and what a buyer must do. Every answer below was read on an official page and links to it. Questions not yet confirmed are listed as such.

Auctions

A county treasurer's sale of tax-foreclosed parcels.

How does a tax sale work here? Statewide

Property foreclosed for delinquent taxes is sold at auction by the foreclosing governmental unit (or its authorized agent) at one or more sales, which may include an online auction.

“the foreclosing governmental unit, or its authorized agent, at the option of the foreclosing governmental unit, shall hold 1 or more property sales at 1 or more convenient locations at which property foreclosed by a judgment entered under section 78k will be sold by auction sale, which may include an auction sale conducted via an internet website.”

Source: MCL - Section 211.78m - Michigan Legislature · MCL 211.78m(2) · verified 2026-11-02

When are sales held?

Tax-Sale.info's 2026 calendar lists Oscoda in the August 19, 2026 auction, 10:00am to 7:00pm EDT.

“19 Aug North Central Lower Peninsula 10:00am EDT to 7:00pm EDT August 19, 2026 Ready to Browse: Crawford , Crawford DNR , Kalkaska , Kalkaska DNR , Missaukee , Montmorency , Ogemaw , Ogemaw DNR , Oscoda”

Source: Auctions - Michigan Public Land Auction | Tax-Sale.info · verified 2026-11-02

Where do I bid - online or in person?

The county's auctions on Tax-Sale.info are conducted online.

“All auctions will be conducted online.”

Source: Frequently Asked Questions - Michigan Public Land Auction | Tax-Sale.info · verified 2026-11-02

How do I register to bid?

Create a free Tax-Sale.info account; identity is verified during registration, so the information must match a government-issued ID.

“To view more detailed information about the listings you will need to create an account and log in. Creating an account is free. We will need to verify your identity during the registration process, so please use accurate information that matches your government issued ID.”

Source: Frequently Asked Questions - Michigan Public Land Auction | Tax-Sale.info · verified 2026-11-02

Is a deposit required and how much?

To bid you must provide a $1000 pre-authorization on a major credit card; it is a hold, not a charge, and is collected only if you fail to pay for property you win.

“To place bids, you must be logged into a valid active account and must provide a $1000 pre-authorization on a major credit card. Pre-authorization is valid for 30-days. This pre-authorization is not a charge, but rather a hold by your financial institution for up to 30 days, which allows you to bid and serves as a security deposit. This hold will only be collected against if you fail to pay for property that you win.”

Source: Frequently Asked Questions - Michigan Public Land Auction | Tax-Sale.info · verified 2026-11-02

What makes up the opening or minimum bid? Statewide

The minimum bid must include all delinquent taxes, interest, penalties and fees on the property, and may include the foreclosing governmental unit's additional expenses for forfeiture, foreclosure, maintenance, repair or remediation.

“The minimum bid must include all of the delinquent taxes, interest, penalties, and fees due on the property, and may include any additional expenses incurred by the foreclosing governmental unit in connection with the forfeiture, foreclosure, maintenance, repair, or remediation of the property”

Source: MCL - Section 211.78m - Michigan Legislature · MCL 211.78m(16)(c) · verified 2026-11-02

When and how must the winning bidder pay?

Winning online bidders have 5 business days after the sale ends to submit payment and notarized paperwork, by wire transfer, certified (cashier's) check, or credit/debit card with a 2.75% processing fee.

“Winning online bidders have 5 business days after the sale ends to submit payment and notarized paperwork to our office. Payments can be made by wire transfer, certified (cashier's) check, or online via credit/debit card (with additional 2.75% processing fee).”

Source: Frequently Asked Questions - Michigan Public Land Auction | Tax-Sale.info · verified 2026-11-02

What costs are added on top of the winning bid?

The total is the winning bid plus a 10% buyer's premium, the current summer taxes, and a $30 deed recording fee.

“Winning Bid Amount + 10% of Winning Bid Amount (Buyer’s Premium) + Current Taxes (this year's Summer Taxes) + Deed Recording Fee ($30.00) = Grand Total”

Source: Frequently Asked Questions - Michigan Public Land Auction | Tax-Sale.info · verified 2026-11-02

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive? Statewide

The deed must vest fee simple title in the highest bidder, unless the county finds a defect in the foreclosure or the sale is canceled.

“The deed must vest fee simple title to the property in the person bidding the highest amount above the minimum bid, unless the foreclosing governmental unit discovers a defect in the foreclosure of the property under sections 78 to 78l or the sale is canceled under this subsection or subsection (5).”

Source: MCL - Section 211.78m - Michigan Legislature · MCL 211.78m(2) · verified 2026-11-02

Can the former owner redeem after the sale? Statewide

All redemption rights expire on the March 31 after the foreclosure judgment is entered, or 21 days after the judgment in a contested case.

“All redemption rights to the property expire on the March 31 immediately succeeding the entry of a judgment foreclosing the property under this section, or in a contested case 21 days after the entry of a judgment foreclosing the property under this section.”

Source: MCL - Section 211.78k - Michigan Legislature · MCL 211.78k(5) · verified 2026-11-02

Which liens or interests survive the sale? Statewide

The foreclosure judgment extinguishes all existing recorded and unrecorded interests except, among others listed in the statute, a visible or recorded easement or right-of-way, private deed restrictions, and the interest of a lessee under a recorded oil or gas lease.

“That all existing recorded and unrecorded interests in that property are extinguished, except a visible or recorded easement or right-of-way, private deed restrictions, interests of a lessee or an assignee of an interest of a lessee under a recorded oil or gas lease”

Source: MCL - Section 211.78k - Michigan Legislature · MCL 211.78k(5)(e) · verified 2026-11-02

Is the title insurable or does it need a quiet-title action? Statewide

Tax-Sale.info says insuring tax reverted land is often more complicated than other purchases and some insurers are more willing than others to write a policy on it.

“Insuring tax reverted lands will often be more complicated than with property purchased in other types of sale. Some insurers are more willing to write a policy on tax reverted property than others.”

Source: Frequently Asked Questions - Michigan Public Land Auction | Tax-Sale.info · verified 2026-11-02

What if the property is occupied? Statewide

Tax-Sale.info says occupants at the time of sale must be treated as tenants-holding-over and removed through formal eviction proceedings in District Court, not by force, threat or coercion.

“Any occupants of the property at the time of sale must be treated as "tenants-holding-over" (like a tenant whose lease has expired). You must pursue formal eviction proceeds in the District Court. Removing occupants by force, threat, or coercion (shutting off the utilities or changing locks) can create major legal issues.”

Source: Frequently Asked Questions - Michigan Public Land Auction | Tax-Sale.info · verified 2026-11-02

What happens to property that does not sell? Statewide

By the December 1 after the judgment, a list of all unsold property goes to the clerk of the city, village or township where it lies, which may object in writing to the transfer of any of it.

“On or before December 1 immediately succeeding the entry of judgment under section 78k, a list of all property not previously sold by the foreclosing governmental unit under this section must be transferred to the clerk of the city, village, or township in which the property is located. The city, village, or township may object in writing to the transfer of 1 or more properties.”

Source: MCL - Section 211.78m - Michigan Legislature · MCL 211.78m(6), (7) · verified 2026-11-02

What does the law or the county tell buyers to check before buying? Statewide

Tax-Sale.info, which runs auctions for many Michigan county treasurers, says property is sold as is with no refunds and buyers must research a property before the auction.

“There are no refunds on property purchased at any of our auctions. All property is sold as is/where is and without warranty. It is the buyer’s responsibility to conduct research prior to the auction date and gain a full understanding of the property they intend to purchase.”

Source: Frequently Asked Questions - Michigan Public Land Auction | Tax-Sale.info · verified 2026-11-02

Available

Land bank inventory: publicly held parcels offered under the land bank's own programs.

What is this list and why is the property available? Statewide

Property not sold at the tax-foreclosure sales and not transferred to the city, village or township stays with the foreclosing governmental unit.

“If property not previously sold is not transferred to the city, village, or township in which the property is located under subsection (6), the foreclosing governmental unit shall retain possession of that property.”

Source: MCL - Section 211.78m - Michigan Legislature · MCL 211.78m(7) · verified 2026-11-02

How do I buy a property from this list? Statewide

A land bank authority may convey, sell, transfer, exchange, lease or otherwise dispose of its property to any public or private person on the terms and conditions, in the manner and for the consideration it considers proper, fair and valuable; the process is therefore set by each authority.

“on terms and conditions, and in a manner and for an amount of consideration an authority considers proper, fair, and valuable, including for no monetary consideration, the authority may convey, sell, transfer, exchange, lease as lessor, or otherwise dispose of property or rights or interests in property in which the authority holds a legal interest to any public or private person for value determined by the authority.”

Source: MCL - Section 124.757 - Michigan Legislature · MCL 124.757(1) · verified 2026-11-02

How is the price set? Statewide

A land bank may sell property for the consideration it considers proper, fair and valuable, including for no money, for value determined by the authority.

“on terms and conditions, and in a manner and for an amount of consideration an authority considers proper, fair, and valuable, including for no monetary consideration, the authority may convey, sell, transfer, exchange, lease as lessor, or otherwise dispose of property or rights or interests in property in which the authority holds a legal interest to any public or private person for value determined by the authority.”

Source: MCL - Section 124.757 - Michigan Legislature · MCL 124.757(1) · verified 2026-11-02

Which liens or interests stay with the property? Statewide

After a land bank authority's expedited quiet title judgment, all liens against the property are extinguished except future installments of special assessments and liens recorded by the state or the authority under the natural resources and environmental protection act, and all other recorded and unrecorded interests are extinguished except a visible or recorded easement or right-of-way, private deed restrictions, plat restrictions, and governmental interests under that act.

“That all liens against the property, including any lien for unpaid taxes or special assessments, except future installments of special assessments and liens recorded by this state or the authority under the natural resources and environmental protection act ... That all existing recorded and unrecorded interests in that property are extinguished, except a visible or recorded easement or right-of-way, private deed restrictions, plat restrictions, or restrictions or other governmental interests imposed under the natural resources and environmental protection act”

Source: MCL - Section 124.759 - Michigan Legislature · MCL 124.759(11)(c), (11)(e) · verified 2026-11-02

Is the title insurable or does it need a quiet-title action? Statewide

A land bank authority may bring an expedited quiet title and foreclosure action on property it holds; the circuit court's judgment specifies that fee simple title is vested absolutely in the authority without further rights of redemption and that the authority has good and marketable fee simple title, with the exceptions the statute lists.

“An authority may initiate an expedited quiet title and foreclosure action under this section to quiet title to real property held by the authority or interests in tax reverted property held by the authority ... That fee simple title to property foreclosed by the judgment is vested absolutely in the authority, except as otherwise provided in subdivisions (c) and (e), without any further rights of redemption. ... That, except as otherwise provided in subdivisions (c) and (e), the authority has good and marketable fee simple title to the property.”

Source: MCL - Section 124.759 - Michigan Legislature · MCL 124.759(1), (11)(b), (11)(d) · verified 2026-11-02

Liens & Certificates

What is a tax lien or certificate here? Statewide

Michigan tax sales do not sell tax liens; property is transferred directly to the buyer by quitclaim deed.

“Do I get a tax lien when I buy property from Michigan tax sales? No, under Michigan law all buyers receive a quitclaim deed. While some other states sell tax liens at public auction, all properties sold in Michigan will be transferred directly to the bidder via quitclaim deed.”

Source: Frequently Asked Questions - Michigan Public Land Auction | Tax-Sale.info · verified 2026-11-02
