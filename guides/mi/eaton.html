How to buy tax-sale property in Eaton County, MI

What the Eaton County offices publish about buying tax-sale property: who runs each sale or list, when and where, and what a buyer must do. Every answer below was read on an official page and links to it. Questions not yet confirmed are listed as such.

Auctions

A county treasurer's sale of tax-foreclosed parcels.

How does a tax sale work here? Statewide

Property foreclosed for delinquent taxes is sold at auction by the foreclosing governmental unit (or its authorized agent) at one or more sales, which may include an online auction.

“the foreclosing governmental unit, or its authorized agent, at the option of the foreclosing governmental unit, shall hold 1 or more property sales at 1 or more convenient locations at which property foreclosed by a judgment entered under section 78k will be sold by auction sale, which may include an auction sale conducted via an internet website.”

Source: MCL - Section 211.78m - Michigan Legislature · MCL 211.78m(2) · verified 2026-11-02

When are sales held?

The 2026 package sets the first Public Land Sale Auction on Thursday, September 17, 2026 (minimum bid) and the second on Thursday, October 22, 2026, with registration at 5:00 PM and the auction at 6:00 PM.

“Thursday, October 22, 2026 Registration at 5:00 PM - Auction at 6:00 PM ... At the first 2026 Public Land Sale Auction held on Thursday, September 17, 2026, no sales will be made for less than the minimum bid price indicated. ... At the second 2026 Public Land Sale Auction held on Thursday, October 22, 2026, the Treasurer as the foreclosing governmental unit will establish a reasonable opening bid at the sale to recover the sale cost”

Source: Eaton County Treasurer's Real Estate Auction Property Information Package · Eaton County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

Where do I bid - online or in person?

The auction is held in person at the Eaton County Governmental Complex, 1045 Independence Blvd, Charlotte MI 48813, with registration at 5:00 PM and the auction at 6:00 PM; pre-registration is available at www.BippusUSA.com.

“Auction Location: Eaton County Governmental Complex 1045 Independence Blvd Charlotte MI 48813 Thursday, October 22, 2026 Registration at 5:00 PM - Auction at 6:00 PM ... Pre-registration is available at www.BippusUSA.com.”

Source: Eaton County Treasurer's Real Estate Auction Property Information Package · Eaton County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

How do I register to bid?

Pre-registration is available at www.BippusUSA.com; on-site registration begins at 5:00 p.m. and no bids are accepted unless the bidder has registered and received a pre-numbered bid card. A valid driver's license or state I.D. and social security number are required, and no one owing delinquent real property taxes to the Treasurer may register.

“REGISTRATION Pre-registration is available at www.BippusUSA.com. Bidders are strongly encouraged to pre-register. The sale will not be delayed to accommodate on-site registrants. On-site registration will begin at 5:00 p.m. and the sale will begin promptly at 6:00 p.m. No bids will be accepted unless the bidder has registered and received a pre-numbered bid card. A valid driver’s license or state I.D. and social security number will be required to register. ... No individual or entity currently owing delinquent real property taxes to the Treasurer will be allowed to register.”

Source: Eaton County Treasurer's Real Estate Auction Property Information Package · Eaton County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

Is a deposit required and how much?

Bidders must bring a minimum deposit of $1,000.00 in cash or certified funds to receive a bidder card, with no exceptions; a successful bidder signs the check over as payment towards the purchase.

“Bidders will be required to bring a minimum deposit of $1,000.00 in cash or certified funds to receive a bidder card. (Cashier’s checks should be made payable to yourself, if you are not a successful bidder at the auction you will then be able to redeposit the funds back into your account. If you are a successful bidder, you will then sign the check over to us as payment towards your purchase.) There are no exceptions to this rule.”

Source: Eaton County Treasurer's Real Estate Auction Property Information Package · Eaton County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

What makes up the opening or minimum bid? Statewide

The minimum bid must include all delinquent taxes, interest, penalties and fees on the property, and may include the foreclosing governmental unit's additional expenses for forfeiture, foreclosure, maintenance, repair or remediation.

“The minimum bid must include all of the delinquent taxes, interest, penalties, and fees due on the property, and may include any additional expenses incurred by the foreclosing governmental unit in connection with the forfeiture, foreclosure, maintenance, repair, or remediation of the property”

Source: MCL - Section 211.78m - Michigan Legislature · MCL 211.78m(16)(c) · verified 2026-11-02

When and how must the winning bidder pay?

Full payment plus a ten percent (10%) buyer's premium and a $59.00 processing fee is due by 7:00 P.M. the day of sale; after the $1,000 in certified funds, a personal check is accepted for the balance, and the deposit is forfeited if the check does not clear within fourteen (14) days. Checks are payable to Bippus USA.com.

“TERMS OF THE SALE [FULL PAYMENT] plus a ten percent (10%) buyer’s premium, and a $59.00 processing fee¹ must be paid by 7:00 P.M. the day of sale. After the $1,000 in certified funds (cash or certified check), a personal check will be accepted for the balance due. In the event personal checks do not clear within fourteen (14) days, the deposit paid on the day of the Land Sale Auction will be forfeited to the Treasurer. ... Checks should be made payable to “Bippus USA.com””

Source: Eaton County Treasurer's Real Estate Auction Property Information Package · Eaton County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

What costs are added on top of the winning bid?

The purchase price is the final bid plus a ten percent (10%) auctioneer fee and a $59.00 per parcel deed preparation fee; the new owner also pays the current year summer and winter taxes, and the 2026 July summer bill must be paid before a deed is issued. A $25,000.00 performance bond is required for a parcel under a demolition order.

“The purchase price consists of the final bid price, a ten percent (10%) auctioneer fee , and a $59.00 per parcel deed preparation fee. ... The new owner will be responsible for the current year summer and winter taxes including any penalties and fees that become due and payable since the foreclosure hearing circuit court date without any prorating to the seller. ... The 2026 July summer bill must be paid before a deed can be issued to the buyer. ... The Eaton County Treasurer requires a $25,000.00 performance bond for any parcel subject to an existing demolition order.”

Source: Eaton County Treasurer's Real Estate Auction Property Information Package · Eaton County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive?

A Quit Claim deed conveying fee simple title, in the name entered at registration, is recorded by the Eaton County Treasurer's Office within thirty (30) days of the sale; the deed contains a right of reverter clause if the purchaser allows the property to go into forfeiture for unpaid delinquent real property taxes.

“TITLE CONVEYANCE A Quit Claim deed pursuant to PA 123 of 1999, conveying fee simple title and, drafted with the name(s) as entered on the registration, will be recorded by the Eaton County Treasurer’s Office with the Eaton County Register of Deeds’ Office within thirty (30) days from the date of the sale. The Quit Claim deed will contain and be subject to the following reverter clause: Right of Reverter The following right of reverter will run with the land: If the purchaser(s) allow the property to go into forfeiture for unpaid delinquent real property taxes”

Source: Eaton County Treasurer's Real Estate Auction Property Information Package · Eaton County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

Can the former owner redeem after the sale? Statewide

All redemption rights expire on the March 31 after the foreclosure judgment is entered, or 21 days after the judgment in a contested case.

“All redemption rights to the property expire on the March 31 immediately succeeding the entry of a judgment foreclosing the property under this section, or in a contested case 21 days after the entry of a judgment foreclosing the property under this section.”

Source: MCL - Section 211.78k - Michigan Legislature · MCL 211.78k(5) · verified 2026-11-02

Which liens or interests survive the sale?

All prior liens other than IRS liens, future installments of special assessments, and liens recorded by the state or Eaton County under the Natural Resources and Environmental Protection Act are cancelled by the Eaton Circuit Court Order dated February 10, 2026; bidders are told to check with local offices for special assessments for future tax years.

“According to state statutes, all prior liens other than IRS liens, future installments of special assessments, and liens recorded by this state or Eaton County pursuant to the Natural Resources and Environmental Protection Act of 1994 (“NREPA”), 1994 PA 451, as amended), are cancelled by the Eaton Circuit Court Order dated February 10, 2026. ... All bidders should contact the county, city, township, or village office to determine if there are any special assessments for future tax years on the properties offered.”

Source: Eaton County Treasurer's Real Estate Auction Property Information Package · Eaton County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

Is the title insurable or does it need a quiet-title action?

Title insurance companies may or may not insure properties bought at the sale; the Treasurer makes no representation about it, unavailability of title insurance is not grounds for re-conveyance, and the purchaser bears any legal costs of a quiet title action required by an insurer.

“Title insurance companies may or may not issue title insurance on properties purchased at the sale. The Treasurer makes no representation as to the availability of title insurance. The unavailability of title insurance is not grounds for re- conveyance to the Treasurer. Purchaser may incur legal costs for a quiet title action to satisfy the requirements of title insurance companies in order to obtain title insurance. These legal costs are solely the responsibility of the purchaser.”

Source: Eaton County Treasurer's Real Estate Auction Property Information Package · Eaton County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

What if the property is occupied?

No purchaser may take possession until the deed is executed and delivered; occupied structures may not be entered without the tenant's permission, the purchaser must insure occupied property, and buyers handle all procedures and legal requirements for evictions.

“Occupied structures may not be entered without the tenant’s permission; secured vacant structures may not be entered. ... No purchaser should take physical possession of any property upon sale until a deed has been executed and delivered to the purchaser. ... The purchaser is responsible for obtaining insurance for occupied property. Buyers will be responsible for all procedures and legal requirements for conducting evictions.”

Source: Eaton County Treasurer's Real Estate Auction Property Information Package · Eaton County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

What happens to property that does not sell? Statewide

By the December 1 after the judgment, a list of all unsold property goes to the clerk of the city, village or township where it lies, which may object in writing to the transfer of any of it.

“On or before December 1 immediately succeeding the entry of judgment under section 78k, a list of all property not previously sold by the foreclosing governmental unit under this section must be transferred to the clerk of the city, village, or township in which the property is located. The city, village, or township may object in writing to the transfer of 1 or more properties.”

Source: MCL - Section 211.78m - Michigan Legislature · MCL 211.78m(6), (7) · verified 2026-11-02

What happens to money paid above what was owed (surplus)?

Under MCL 211.78t, those with title or equity interest at foreclosure may file to claim leftover proceeds; for properties sold after December 22, 2020, they file form 5743 with the Eaton County Treasurers office by July 1 immediately following the effective date of the foreclosure, and may then file a motion with the circuit court between the subsequent February 1st and May 15th.

“In accordance with MCL 211.78t , those who hold title or equity interest in a property at the time of foreclosure, may file to claim leftover proceeds ... For foreclosed properties sold after December 22, 2020 : File form 5743 Notice of Intention to Claim Interest in Foreclosure Sales Proceeds with the Eaton County Treasurers office by July 1 st immediately following the effective date of the foreclosure . ... the claimant may then file a motion with the circuit court between the subsequent February 1st and May 15th”

Source: Foreclosure Auction Claimants | Eaton County, MI · MCL 211.78t · Eaton County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

What does the law or the county tell buyers to check before buying?

Property is sold as is with no warranties, and the Treasurer does not guarantee usability or access. The purchaser is responsible for researching the intended use and making a personal ground inspection, and there are no refunds or cancellations of sales.

“The property is sold “as is” with no warranties expressed or implied. ... The Treasurer does not guarantee the usability or access to any of these lands. It is the responsibility of the purchaser to research the use of the land for their intended purpose and to make a personal ground inspection of the property to determine if it will be suitable for the purposes for which it is being purchased. ... PLEASE KNOW WHAT YOU ARE BUYING. THERE ARE NO REFUNDS OR CANCELLATIONS OF SALES.”

Source: Eaton County Treasurer's Real Estate Auction Property Information Package · Eaton County Treasurer · verified 2026-11-02

Available

Land bank inventory: publicly held parcels offered under the land bank's own programs.

What is this list and why is the property available? Statewide

Property not sold at the tax-foreclosure sales and not transferred to the city, village or township stays with the foreclosing governmental unit.

“If property not previously sold is not transferred to the city, village, or township in which the property is located under subsection (6), the foreclosing governmental unit shall retain possession of that property.”

Source: MCL - Section 211.78m - Michigan Legislature · MCL 211.78m(7) · verified 2026-11-02

How do I buy a property from this list? Statewide

A land bank authority may convey, sell, transfer, exchange, lease or otherwise dispose of its property to any public or private person on the terms and conditions, in the manner and for the consideration it considers proper, fair and valuable; the process is therefore set by each authority.

“on terms and conditions, and in a manner and for an amount of consideration an authority considers proper, fair, and valuable, including for no monetary consideration, the authority may convey, sell, transfer, exchange, lease as lessor, or otherwise dispose of property or rights or interests in property in which the authority holds a legal interest to any public or private person for value determined by the authority.”

Source: MCL - Section 124.757 - Michigan Legislature · MCL 124.757(1) · verified 2026-11-02

How is the price set? Statewide

A land bank may sell property for the consideration it considers proper, fair and valuable, including for no money, for value determined by the authority.

“on terms and conditions, and in a manner and for an amount of consideration an authority considers proper, fair, and valuable, including for no monetary consideration, the authority may convey, sell, transfer, exchange, lease as lessor, or otherwise dispose of property or rights or interests in property in which the authority holds a legal interest to any public or private person for value determined by the authority.”

Source: MCL - Section 124.757 - Michigan Legislature · MCL 124.757(1) · verified 2026-11-02

Which liens or interests stay with the property? Statewide

After a land bank authority's expedited quiet title judgment, all liens against the property are extinguished except future installments of special assessments and liens recorded by the state or the authority under the natural resources and environmental protection act, and all other recorded and unrecorded interests are extinguished except a visible or recorded easement or right-of-way, private deed restrictions, plat restrictions, and governmental interests under that act.

“That all liens against the property, including any lien for unpaid taxes or special assessments, except future installments of special assessments and liens recorded by this state or the authority under the natural resources and environmental protection act ... That all existing recorded and unrecorded interests in that property are extinguished, except a visible or recorded easement or right-of-way, private deed restrictions, plat restrictions, or restrictions or other governmental interests imposed under the natural resources and environmental protection act”

Source: MCL - Section 124.759 - Michigan Legislature · MCL 124.759(11)(c), (11)(e) · verified 2026-11-02

Is the title insurable or does it need a quiet-title action? Statewide

A land bank authority may bring an expedited quiet title and foreclosure action on property it holds; the circuit court's judgment specifies that fee simple title is vested absolutely in the authority without further rights of redemption and that the authority has good and marketable fee simple title, with the exceptions the statute lists.

“An authority may initiate an expedited quiet title and foreclosure action under this section to quiet title to real property held by the authority or interests in tax reverted property held by the authority ... That fee simple title to property foreclosed by the judgment is vested absolutely in the authority, except as otherwise provided in subdivisions (c) and (e), without any further rights of redemption. ... That, except as otherwise provided in subdivisions (c) and (e), the authority has good and marketable fee simple title to the property.”

Source: MCL - Section 124.759 - Michigan Legislature · MCL 124.759(1), (11)(b), (11)(d) · verified 2026-11-02

Liens & Certificates

What is a tax lien or certificate here? Statewide

Michigan tax sales do not sell tax liens; property is transferred directly to the buyer by quitclaim deed.

“Do I get a tax lien when I buy property from Michigan tax sales? No, under Michigan law all buyers receive a quitclaim deed. While some other states sell tax liens at public auction, all properties sold in Michigan will be transferred directly to the bidder via quitclaim deed.”

Source: Frequently Asked Questions - Michigan Public Land Auction | Tax-Sale.info · verified 2026-11-02
