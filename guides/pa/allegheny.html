How to buy tax-sale property in Allegheny County, PA

What the Allegheny County offices publish about buying tax-sale property: who runs each sale or list, when and where, and what a buyer must do. Every answer below was read on an official page and links to it. Questions not yet confirmed are listed as such.

Available

Repository property: parcels unsold at the tax sales, offered by the Tax Claim Bureau by bid.

Which office handles it and how do I contact them?

The Real Estate Division of the City of Pittsburgh Department of Finance handles purchases of city-owned property; phone 412-255-2300.

“For additional information about the vacant lots, call the Real Estate Division at: 412-255-2300 The Real Estate Division of the Department of Finance accepts questions from the general public for purchase of properties.”

Source: Real Estate - Pittsburgh, PA · Real Estate Division, Department of Finance, City of Pittsburgh · verified 2026-11-02

What is this list and why is the property available? Statewide

Property that remains unsold after being exposed to a judicial sale is placed in a "repository for unsold properties". The county tax claim bureau keeps the list and makes it available to the public during normal office hours.

“If, after conducting and exposing a property to a judicial sale under the provisions of this act, whether prior to, at the time of or after the effective date of this amendatory act, any property remains unsold, it shall be placed in a category to be termed "repository for unsold properties." A list of properties in this category shall be maintained by the bureau and shall be available during normal office hours to the general public.”

Source: Real Estate Tax Sale Law, Act of July 7, 1947, P.L. 1368, No. 542 (Pennsylvania General Assembly) · Real Estate Tax Sale Law sec. 626 (72 P.S. 5860.626) · verified 2026-11-02

How do I buy a property from this list?

Buyers submit a Request to Purchase through the city's online portal with a $25 application fee per property; the city checks that the applicant is a qualified buyer (taxes, water and sewage paid, in compliance with city codes and policies) and tells the approved purchaser the price in its response letter.

“all Request to Purchase applications must be submitted through our online portal ... A $25 application fee per property will apply. ... The prospective purchaser submits a request to become a qualified buyer. A qualified buyer is one who: Has paid taxes, water, sewage, etc. Is in compliance with City codes and policies. The approved prospective purchaser is notified of the price in the response letter to the Request to Purchase form.”

Source: Real Estate - Pittsburgh, PA · Real Estate Division, Department of Finance, City of Pittsburgh · verified 2026-11-02

How is the price set?

Prices online show TBD; a property is priced after the applicant is approved as a buyer, and the approved purchaser is told the price in the response letter to the Request to Purchase form.

“The approved prospective purchaser is notified of the price in the response letter to the Request to Purchase form. ... Why do the prices online all say “TBD?” Pricing of all properties will be done after you are approved as a buyer.”

Source: Real Estate - Pittsburgh, PA · Real Estate Division, Department of Finance, City of Pittsburgh · verified 2026-11-02

What costs are added to the price?

A non-refundable $25.00 per parcel application fee; the purchaser pays for the insurable title report; the buyer pays all related costs, and the deed is recorded immediately upon closing.

“A non refundable $25.00 per parcel application fee will apply. ... The report must be paid for by the purchaser, and may or may not include title insurance. ... The buyer must pay all related costs and the deed must be recorded immediately upon closing.”

Source: Procedures for Purchasing Property Through Standard Court Auction Sales (City of Pittsburgh Department of Finance, rev. 9.30.25) · Real Estate Division, Department of Finance, City of Pittsburgh · verified 2026-11-02

What forms or documents are required?

A Request to Purchase application submitted through the city's online portal, one property per form; paper and e-mailed applications are not accepted.

“all Request to Purchase applications must be submitted through our online portal ... Paper applications and applications sent via email will no longer be accepted. ... There can only be one piece of property requested per form. If you are requesting multiple properties, each piece will require a new, separate, Request to Purchase form.”

Source: Real Estate - Pittsburgh, PA · Real Estate Division, Department of Finance, City of Pittsburgh · verified 2026-11-02

How and when do I pay?

After approval the buyer places a 10% non-refundable deposit (minimum $200.00) by cashier's check or money order payable to the Director of Finance; the remaining balance is paid at closing, by cashier's check or money order, within sixty (60) days of the notice that the deed is ready.

“The prospective buyer must place a 10% non- refundable deposit (minimum $200.00) with the Real Estate Division. The deposit must be in the form of a cashier’s check or money order made payable to the Director of Finance. ... The buyer will be required to close and pay the remaining balance within sixty (60) days of the notification. Final settlement of the sale must be paid by cashier’s check or money order.”

Source: Procedures for Purchasing Property Through Standard Court Auction Sales (City of Pittsburgh Department of Finance, rev. 9.30.25) · Real Estate Division, Department of Finance, City of Pittsburgh · verified 2026-11-02

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive?

A quit claim deed prepared by City Law and signed by the Mayor after a final Order of Court confirms the sale.

“A final Order of Court will confirm the sale to the buyer or successful bidder. City Law will prepare and execute a deed that will be signed by the Mayor. ... Title will be conveyed by quit claim deed.”

Source: Procedures for Purchasing Property Through Standard Court Auction Sales (City of Pittsburgh Department of Finance, rev. 9.30.25) · Real Estate Division, Department of Finance, City of Pittsburgh · verified 2026-11-02

How long does a property stay available?

The city's procedure says the sale process may take 12 – 18 months or longer and that ownership may not be guaranteed; it does not say how long a property stays listed.

“THESE PROCEDURES MAY TAKE 12 – 18 MONTHS OR LONGER, AND OWNERSHIP MAY NOT BE GUARANTEED”

Source: Procedures for Purchasing Property Through Standard Court Auction Sales (City of Pittsburgh Department of Finance, rev. 9.30.25) · Real Estate Division, Department of Finance, City of Pittsburgh · verified 2026-11-02

Which liens or interests stay with the property?

The city says the title and property are free and clear of all back taxes and liens when the buyer closes.

“Will I get the properties purchased from the City of Pittsburgh with a clear title? Yes. The title and property will be free and clear of all back taxes and liens when you close on the property.”

Source: Real Estate - Pittsburgh, PA · Real Estate Division, Department of Finance, City of Pittsburgh · verified 2026-11-02

Is the title insurable or does it need a quiet-title action?

After Council approval the buyer orders and pays for an insurable title report, which may or may not include title insurance; the Law Department uses it to petition the Court of Common Pleas to validate the title, and title is conveyed by quit claim deed.

“After Council approval is received, the City will notify the buyer in writing to order an insurable title report. The report must be paid for by the purchaser, and may or may not include title insurance. ... The Law Department will use the Title Report to prepare and submit a petition for a Rule to Show Cause to the Court of Common Pleas authorizing and validating the title. ... Title will be conveyed by quit claim deed.”

Source: Procedures for Purchasing Property Through Standard Court Auction Sales (City of Pittsburgh Department of Finance, rev. 9.30.25) · Real Estate Division, Department of Finance, City of Pittsburgh · verified 2026-11-02

Can I inspect the property before buying?

Not before approval; once approved and sent a correspondence letter, the buyer can set a date and time to view the property.

“Can I view the property/properties that I requested before I am approved? No. You will be able to set a date and time to view the property once you have been approved and received a correspondence letter from the City.”

Source: Real Estate - Pittsburgh, PA · Real Estate Division, Department of Finance, City of Pittsburgh · verified 2026-11-02

Are sales final or can a purchase be refunded?

The 10% deposit (minimum $200.00) is non-refundable and hand money is forfeited if the buyer does not comply with the procedures; hand money is refunded if City Council denies the sale.

“The prospective buyer must place a 10% non- refundable deposit (minimum $200.00) with the Real Estate Division. ... If denied by Council, hand money will be refunded. ... FAILURE TO COMPLY WITH THESE PROCEDURES WILL RESULT IN THE CANCELLATION OF THE SALE AND FORFEITURE OF HAND MONEY.”

Source: Procedures for Purchasing Property Through Standard Court Auction Sales (City of Pittsburgh Department of Finance, rev. 9.30.25) · Real Estate Division, Department of Finance, City of Pittsburgh · verified 2026-11-02

What does the law or the program tell buyers to check before buying?

The city warns that failure to comply with its procedures cancels the sale and forfeits the hand money, that the procedures may take 12 – 18 months or longer, and that ownership may not be guaranteed.

“FAILURE TO COMPLY WITH THESE PROCEDURES WILL RESULT IN THE CANCELLATION OF THE SALE AND FORFEITURE OF HAND MONEY. THESE PROCEDURES MAY TAKE 12 – 18 MONTHS OR LONGER, AND OWNERSHIP MAY NOT BE GUARANTEED”

Source: Procedures for Purchasing Property Through Standard Court Auction Sales (City of Pittsburgh Department of Finance, rev. 9.30.25) · Real Estate Division, Department of Finance, City of Pittsburgh · verified 2026-11-02
