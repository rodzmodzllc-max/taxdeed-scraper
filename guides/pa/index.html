Repository property: parcels unsold at the tax sales, offered by the Tax Claim Bureau by bid.

Step 1 · Find Find the sale or list

What is this list and why is the property available?

Property that remains unsold after being exposed to a judicial sale is placed in a "repository for unsold properties". The county tax claim bureau keeps the list and makes it available to the public during normal office hours.

Official wording
“If, after conducting and exposing a property to a judicial sale under the provisions of this act, whether prior to, at the time of or after the effective date of this amendatory act, any property remains unsold, it shall be placed in a category to be termed "repository for unsold properties." A list of properties in this category shall be maintained by the bureau and shall be available during normal office hours to the general public.”

Source: Real Estate Tax Sale Law, Act of July 7, 1947, P.L. 1368, No. 542 (Pennsylvania General Assembly) · Real Estate Tax Sale Law sec. 626 (72 P.S. 5860.626) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

How long does a property stay available?

Property that remains unsold after a judicial sale is placed in the repository for unsold properties, and the bureau keeps the list available to the public during normal office hours; the bureau may accept an offer at or above the minimum purchase price without court approval or published notice. The quoted sections set no time limit.

Official wording
“any property remains unsold, it shall be placed in a category to be termed "repository for unsold properties." A list of properties in this category shall be maintained by the bureau and shall be available during normal office hours to the general public. ... The bureau may, with the written consent of all the taxing districts where the property is located, establish a minimum purchase price and accept an offer of any price equal to or greater than the minimum purchase price for property placed in the "repository for unsold properties" without court approval and published notice of sale.”

Source: Real Estate Tax Sale Law, Act of July 7, 1947, P.L. 1368, No. 542 (Pennsylvania General Assembly) · 72 P.S. §§ 5860.626-5860.627 (Real Estate Tax Sale Law §§ 626-627) · county tax claim bureau · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 2 · Check Check before you commit

How is the price set?

For repository property, the bureau may set a minimum purchase price with the written consent of all taxing districts and accept an offer at or above it.

Official wording
“establish a minimum purchase price and accept an offer of any price equal to or greater than the minimum purchase price”

Source: Real Estate Tax Sale Law, Act of July 7, 1947, P.L. 1368, No. 542 (Pennsylvania General Assembly) · Real Estate Tax Sale Law sec. 627(a) (72 P.S. 5860.627) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 3 · Buy How to buy

How do I buy a property from this list?

With the written consent of all taxing districts, the bureau may set a minimum purchase price and accept an offer at or above it without court approval. A taxing district may require the buyer to appear in person before its governing body.

Official wording
“The bureau may, with the written consent of all the taxing districts where the property is located, establish a minimum purchase price and accept an offer of any price equal to or greater than the minimum purchase price for property placed in the "repository for unsold properties" without court approval and published notice of sale.”

Source: Real Estate Tax Sale Law, Act of July 7, 1947, P.L. 1368, No. 542 (Pennsylvania General Assembly) · Real Estate Tax Sale Law sec. 627(a) (72 P.S. 5860.627) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What costs are added to the price?

The bureau records the deed at the purchaser's expense. A county that has adopted the ordinance may also collect a fee of up to $250 from the buyer at the time of sale for its demolition and rehabilitation fund.

Official wording
“It shall be the responsibility of the bureau to have the deed recorded at the expense of the purchaser. ... The governing body of a county may, by ordinance, impose a fee not to exceed two hundred fifty dollars ($250) for the sale of any property being sold in accordance with this act”

Source: Real Estate Tax Sale Law, Act of July 7, 1947, P.L. 1368, No. 542 (Pennsylvania General Assembly) · Real Estate Tax Sale Law sec. 627(b), 631(b) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What forms or documents are required?

The purchaser must give an affidavit with the information in section 502-A, including a statement that the purchaser is not delinquent in real estate taxes to any taxing district in Pennsylvania.

Official wording
“The bureau shall require, as a condition of sale, that the purchaser provide an affidavit that includes the information specified under section 502-A. ... is not delinquent in paying real estate taxes to any taxing district in this Commonwealth”

Source: Real Estate Tax Sale Law, Act of July 7, 1947, P.L. 1368, No. 542 (Pennsylvania General Assembly) · Real Estate Tax Sale Law sec. 627(a), 502-A · Checked Nov 2, 2026

How and when do I pay?

The property is conveyed upon payment of the agreed price.

Official wording
“The property shall be conveyed to the purchaser upon payment of the agreed price”

Source: Real Estate Tax Sale Law, Act of July 7, 1947, P.L. 1368, No. 542 (Pennsylvania General Assembly) · Real Estate Tax Sale Law sec. 627(b) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 4 · After After you buy

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive?

Repository property is conveyed free and clear of all tax and municipal claims, mortgages, liens, charges and estates of any kind, except ground rents separately taxed.

Official wording
“The property shall be conveyed to the purchaser upon payment of the agreed price, free and clear of all tax and municipal claims, mortgages, liens, and charges and estates of whatsoever kind, except ground rents separately taxed.”

Source: Real Estate Tax Sale Law, Act of July 7, 1947, P.L. 1368, No. 542 (Pennsylvania General Assembly) · Real Estate Tax Sale Law sec. 627(b) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 5 · Risks Risks to weigh

Which liens or interests stay with the property?

Repository property is conveyed on payment of the agreed price free and clear of all tax and municipal claims, mortgages, liens, charges and estates, except ground rents separately taxed. The deed is recorded at the purchaser's expense.

Official wording
“The property shall be conveyed to the purchaser upon payment of the agreed price, free and clear of all tax and municipal claims, mortgages, liens, and charges and estates of whatsoever kind, except ground rents separately taxed. It shall be the responsibility of the bureau to have the deed recorded at the expense of the purchaser.”

Source: Real Estate Tax Sale Law, Act of July 7, 1947, P.L. 1368, No. 542 (Pennsylvania General Assembly) · 72 P.S. 5860.627(b) · Checked Nov 2, 2026
