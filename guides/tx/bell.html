How to buy tax-sale property in Bell County, TX

Property struck off to a taxing unit after no sufficient bid, which the unit may later resell under its own process.

Source: Bell - 014 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What is this list and why is the property available? Statewide

These are properties sold to a taxing unit at a tax sale. Such a taxing unit may sell the property at any time by public or private sale, subject to any right of redemption of the former owner; the redemption period begins when the deed to the taxing unit is filed for record.

Official wording
“If property is sold to a taxing unit that is a party to the judgment, the taxing unit may sell the property at any time by public or private sale. … The sale is subject to any right of redemption of the former owner. The redemption period begins on the date the deed to the taxing unit is filed for record.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.05(a); 34.01(j) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

How long does a property stay available? Statewide

The taxing unit may sell the property at any time. If it has not sold the property within six months after the owner's right of redemption ends, any taxing unit entitled to the proceeds may ask the sheriff or a constable in writing to sell it at a public sale.

Official wording
“If the purchasing taxing unit has not sold the property within six months after the date on which the owner's right of redemption terminates, any taxing unit that is entitled to receive proceeds of the sale by resolution of its governing body may request the sheriff or a constable in writing to sell the property at a public sale.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.05(a), (c) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 2 · Check Check before you commit

How is the price set? Statewide

A public resale requested by the taxing unit may be for any amount. A private sale under 34.05(h) may not be for less than the lesser of the market value in the judgment or the total judgments against the property.

Official wording
“Property sold pursuant to Subsections (c) and (d) of this section may be sold for any amount. … Property sold under this subsection may not be sold for an amount that is less than the lesser of: (1) the market value specified in the judgment of foreclosure; or (2) the total amount of the judgments against the property.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.05(b), (h), (i) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 3 · Buy How to buy

How do I buy a property from this list? Statewide

The taxing unit that bought the property may ask the sheriff or a constable to sell it at a public sale, or may instead sell it at a private sale.

Official wording
“The taxing unit purchasing the property by resolution of its governing body may request the sheriff or a constable to sell the property at a public sale. … In lieu of a sale pursuant to Subsections (c) and (d) of this section, the taxing unit that purchased the property may sell the property at a private sale.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.05(c), (d), (h) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What costs are added to the price? Statewide

For a private sale at or above market value under 34.05(j), the conveyance is subject to the purchaser's obligation to pay the prorated taxes for the current year.

Official wording
“The conveyance is subject to any remaining right of redemption at the time of the sale and to the purchaser's obligation to pay the prorated taxes for the current year as provided by Section 26.10 .”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.05(k) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 4 · After After you buy

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive? Statewide

The seller executes a deed conveying the right, title and interest held by each taxing unit that was a party to the judgment. The conveyance is subject to any remaining right of redemption.

Official wording
“shall execute a deed to the property conveying to the purchaser the right, title, and interest acquired or held by each taxing unit that was a party to the judgment foreclosing tax liens on the property. The conveyance shall be made subject to any remaining right of redemption at the time of the sale.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.05(e) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Which liens or interests stay with the property? Statewide

A resale by a taxing unit is subject to any remaining right of redemption of the former owner. A private sale at or above market value under Subsection (j) discharges and extinguishes all liens foreclosed by the judgment.

Official wording
“The sale is subject to any right of redemption of the former owner. ... The conveyance shall be made subject to any remaining right of redemption at the time of the sale. ... A sale under Subsection (j) discharges and extinguishes all liens foreclosed by the judgment”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.05(a), (e), (k) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Is the title insurable or does it need a quiet-title action? Statewide

An action attacking the validity of a resale of property by a taxing unit may not be instituted after the expiration of one year after the date of the resale.

Official wording
“An action attacking the validity of a resale of property pursuant to this section may not be instituted after the expiration of one year after the date of the resale.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.05(f) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

A sheriff's or constable's tax foreclosure sale of the property under a court judgment.

The Texas Comptroller's county directory lists the Bell County Tax Assessor-Collector at 254-933-5318 (website www.bellcountytx.com).

Official wording
“Tax Assessor-Collector: Shay Luedeke Phone: 254-933-5318 … Website: www.bellcountytx.com”

Source: Bell - 014 · Checked Nov 2, 2026

How does a tax sale work here? Statewide

Real property seized under a tax warrant or ordered sold after foreclosure of a tax lien is sold by the officer charged with selling the property. The sale is conducted the way similar property is sold under execution, except as the Tax Code provides otherwise.

Official wording
“Real property seized under a tax warrant issued under Subchapter E , Chapter 33 , or ordered sold pursuant to foreclosure of a tax lien shall be sold by the officer charged with selling the property, unless otherwise directed by the taxing unit that requested the warrant or order of sale or by an authorized agent or attorney for that unit. The sale shall be conducted in the manner similar property is sold under execution”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.01(a) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

When are sales held?

Tax sales begin at 10:00 a.m. on the first Tuesday of the month, but are not conducted every month in Bell County - there are usually 4 to 5 tax sales a year.

Official wording
“Tax sales begin at 10:00 a.m. on the first Tuesday of the month and are held at the Bell County Clerk’s Alcove outside to the east of the main entrance of the Bell County Justice Complex, 1201 Huey Drive, in Belton. ... Tax Sales are not conducted every month in Bell County. There are usually 4 to 5 tax sales a year.”

Source: Property Tax Sales - Tax Appraisal District of Bell County · Tax Appraisal District of Bell County · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Where do I bid - online or in person?

In person only: bidders must be present at the sale, held at the Bell County Clerk's Alcove outside to the east of the main entrance of the Bell County Justice Complex, 1201 Huey Drive, in Belton; bids are not accepted by mail, phone or internet.

Official wording
“Bidders must be present at the tax sale. Bids will not be accepted via mail, phone or internet. ... are held at the Bell County Clerk’s Alcove outside to the east of the main entrance of the Bell County Justice Complex, 1201 Huey Drive, in Belton.”

Source: Property Tax Sales - Tax Appraisal District of Bell County · Tax Appraisal District of Bell County · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 2 · Check Check before you commit

What makes up the opening or minimum bid? Statewide

The officer calculates the total amount due under the judgment: taxes, penalties and interest, any other amount awarded, court costs and the costs of the sale. If no bid sufficient to pay the lesser of that amount or the adjudged value is received, the taxing unit that requested the order of sale may end the sale.

Official wording
“The officer shall calculate the total amount due under the judgment, including all taxes, penalties, and interest, plus any other amount awarded by the judgment, court costs, and the costs of the sale. … If a bid sufficient to pay the lesser of the amount calculated under Subsection (b) or the adjudged value is not received, the taxing unit that requested the order of sale may terminate the sale.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.01(b), (j) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What if the property is occupied?

Neither the Tax Appraisal District nor the Bell County Sheriff's Department puts the purchaser in possession at the sale; purchasers have the right to a Writ of Possession on the 20th day after the deed is filed of record, and the page points to Section 33.51 of the Tax Code for the procedure for occupied property.

Official wording
“neither the Tax Appraisal District of Bell County nor the Bell County Sheriff’s Department will put you in possession of the property at the time of the tax sale. Purchasers have the right to a Writ of Possession on the 20 th day following the date on which the purchaser’s deed is filed of record with the County Clerk. See Section 33.51 of the Tax Code for the procedures for gaining possession of occupied property.”

Source: Property Tax Sales - Tax Appraisal District of Bell County · Tax Appraisal District of Bell County · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What does the law or the county tell buyers to check before buying?

The Appraisal District tells bidders to view the property from public streets or roadways before the sale and satisfy themselves about its location and condition; it cannot give permission to enter the property. All property is sold by legal description, "AS IS" with all faults, and the approximate address on the bid sheet may not be accurate.

Official wording
“Prior to the sale, bidders should view the property and satisfy themselves as to the location and condition of the property. After the sale is too late. ... cannot give you permission to go onto or access the property prior to the sale. Do not trespass. You should view the property from public streets or roadways. All property is sold by legal description. Property is sold “AS IS” with all faults. ... Any “approximate property address” reflected on the bid sheet is the address on the tax records and may not be accurate.”

Source: Property Tax Sales - Tax Appraisal District of Bell County · Tax Appraisal District of Bell County · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 3 · Buy How to buy

How do I register to bid?

All bidders register with the person conducting the sale before it begins; registration starts at approximately 9:45 a.m. on the day of the sale and requires a valid driver's license or identification card issued by a State Agency or the United States Government.

Official wording
“All bidders must register to bid prior to the sale with the person conducting the sale. If you do not register prior to the beginning of the sale, you may not bid. Registration will begin at approximately 9:45 a.m. the day of the sale ... Bidders must present a valid driver’s license or identification card issued by a State Agency or the United States Government at the time of registration.”

Source: Property Tax Sales - Tax Appraisal District of Bell County · Tax Appraisal District of Bell County · Checked Nov 2, 2026

When and how must the winning bidder pay?

The full purchase price is paid by cashier's check payable to the Bell County District Clerk, at the District Clerk's Office at the Justice Complex between 1:00 and 3:00 pm on the day of the sale; personal checks are not accepted.

Official wording
“Bidders must have the funds readily accessible with which to pay the purchase price in full. ... The cashier’s check should be made payable to the Bell County District Clerk. ... Personal checks will not be accepted ... The Bell County District Clerk’s Office will accept your payment between 1:00 and 3:00 pm on the day of the sale.”

Source: Property Tax Sales - Tax Appraisal District of Bell County · Bell County District Clerk · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What costs are added on top of the winning bid?

Purchasers pay all taxes that became or will become delinquent after the date of judgment, the deed recording fee at the conclusion of the sale (two pages is $14.00), and a $10.00 fee for the document showing no delinquent taxes are owed.

Official wording
“The minimum bid includes all taxes which were delinquent at the date of Judgment. Purchasers will be required to pay all taxes which became or will become delinquent subsequent to the date of Judgment. ... You will be required to pay for the deed recording fee at the conclusion of the tax sale. Most deeds are two pages and the fee for two pages is $14.00. ... There is a $10.00 fee for this document which will be collected at the conclusion of the sale.”

Source: Property Tax Sales - Tax Appraisal District of Bell County · Tax Appraisal District of Bell County · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 4 · After After you buy

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive? Statewide

The deed vests good and perfect title in the purchaser to the defendant's interest in the property, including the right to use and possess it, subject to the defendant's right of redemption (and the recorded covenants and easements listed in the statute). It may be impeached only for fraud.

Official wording
“The deed vests good and perfect title in the purchaser or the purchaser's assigns to the interest owned by the defendant in the property subject to the foreclosure, including the defendant's right to the use and possession of the property, subject only to the defendant's right of redemption … The deed may be impeached only for fraud.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.01(m), (n) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Can the former owner redeem after the sale?

Yes. The redemption period begins when the purchaser's deed is recorded: 2 years for homestead property and land designated for agricultural use, 180 days for all other property. The redemption price is the purchase amount, deed recording fee, taxes paid and costs, plus a premium of 25 percent in the first year or 50 percent in the second.

Official wording
“The redemption period begins on the date that the purchaser’s deed is recorded in the County Clerk’s office. The redemption period for homestead property and land designated for agricultural use is 2 years. ... The redemption period for all other property is 180 days. ... purchase amount, deed recording fee, taxes paid by the purchaser after the tax sale, and costs expended on the property, plus a redemption premium of 25 percent of the aggregate total during the first year or 50 percent of the aggregate total during the second year.”

Source: Property Tax Sales - Tax Appraisal District of Bell County · Tax Appraisal District of Bell County · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What happens to property that does not sell? Statewide

If no sufficient bid is received and the taxing unit does not end the sale, the officer bids the property off to the taxing unit that requested the order of sale, for the lesser of the judgment amount or the market value in the judgment. That taxing unit takes title for itself and the other taxing units in the suit.

Official wording
“the officer making the sale shall bid the property off to the taxing unit that requested the order of sale … for the aggregate amount of the judgment against the property or for the market value of the property as specified in the judgment, whichever is less. … The taxing unit to which the property is bid off takes title to the property for the use and benefit of itself and all other taxing units that established tax liens in the suit.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.01(j), (k); 34.05(a) · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What happens to money paid above what was owed (surplus)? Statewide

The officer pays any excess proceeds to the clerk of the court that issued the warrant or order of sale. The clerk keeps them for two years after the sale unless the court orders otherwise; a claim must be filed by petition in the court before the second anniversary of the sale, and the court orders the proceeds paid by a set order of priorities.

Official wording
“shall pay any excess proceeds after payment of all amounts due all participants in the sale as specified by Subsection (b) to the clerk of the court issuing the warrant or order of sale. ... keep the excess proceeds paid into court as provided by Section 34.02 (d) for a period of two years after the date of the sale unless otherwise ordered by the court ... The petition must be filed before the second anniversary of the date of the sale of the property. ... the court shall order that the proceeds be paid according to the following priorities”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.02(d), 34.03(a)(2), 34.04(a), 34.04(c) · Clerk of the court that ordered the sale · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Step 5 · Risks Risks to weigh

Which liens or interests survive the sale?

The sale should extinguish the ownership interests and recorded liens of everyone named as a defendant in the judgment, including IRS tax liens; the purchaser takes title subject to the interest or lien of any party not named as a defendant, and the property may be subject to City or Property Owners Association liens for demolition, mowing or maintenance fees.

Official wording
“The tax sale should extinguish the record ownership interest of all persons and the recorded liens of all persons who were named as defendants in the judgment on which the tax sale is based, including any tax liens filed by the Internal Revenue Service. If a party who has an ownership interest or holds a lien on the property was NOT named as a defendant in the judgment, the purchaser at the tax sale will take title to the property subject to the interest or lien of that party. ... Property purchased at the tax sale may be subject to liens for demolition, mowing, or maintenance fees”

Source: Property Tax Sales - Tax Appraisal District of Bell County · Tax Appraisal District of Bell County · Checked Nov 2, 2026

Is the title insurable or does it need a quiet-title action?

Purchasers receive an ordinary Sheriff's Deed without warranty, expressed or implied; title is not guaranteed and a policy of title insurance may be difficult to obtain.

Official wording
“Purchasers of property at the tax sale will receive an ordinary type of Sheriff’s Deed which is without warranty, either expressed or implied. Title to property sold at the tax sale is NOT guaranteed. A policy of title insurance on the property purchased at the tax sale may be difficult to obtain.”

Source: Property Tax Sales - Tax Appraisal District of Bell County · Tax Appraisal District of Bell County · Checked Nov 2, 2026

What is a tax lien or certificate here? Statewide

Texas does not sell tax lien certificates. The tax office sells no tax liens; the property itself is sold at the monthly tax foreclosure sale.

Official wording
“The tax office does not sell tax liens. Texas law allows the public to purchase properties from the county at a monthly tax foreclosure sale. The states sells the deed to the property. Note: The state does not sell tax lien certificates where a buyer becomes the lienholder for the back taxes.”

Source: Foreclosed properties frequently asked questions · Checked Oct 29, 2026
