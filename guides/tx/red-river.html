How to buy tax-sale property in Red River County, TX

What the Red River County offices publish about buying tax-sale property: who runs each sale or list, when and where, and what a buyer must do. Every answer below was read on an official page and links to it. Questions not yet confirmed are listed as such.

Auctions

A sheriff's or constable's tax foreclosure sale of the property under a court judgment.

Which office handles it and how do I contact them?

The Texas Comptroller's county directory lists the Red River County Tax Assessor-Collector, phone 903-427-3009, website www.co.red-river.tx.us.

“Tax Assessor/Collector ... Phone: 903-427-3009 ... Website: www.co.red-river.tx.us”

Source: Red River - 194 · verified 2026-11-02

How does a tax sale work here? Statewide

Real property seized under a tax warrant or ordered sold after foreclosure of a tax lien is sold by the officer charged with selling the property. The sale is conducted the way similar property is sold under execution, except as the Tax Code provides otherwise.

“Real property seized under a tax warrant issued under Subchapter E , Chapter 33 , or ordered sold pursuant to foreclosure of a tax lien shall be sold by the officer charged with selling the property, unless otherwise directed by the taxing unit that requested the warrant or order of sale or by an authorized agent or attorney for that unit. The sale shall be conducted in the manner similar property is sold under execution”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.01(a) · verified 2026-11-02

When are sales held?

The Red River County Tax Assessor-Collector says tax sale properties can be viewed on the LGBS tax sales site by searching Red River County, TX.

“To View Tax Sales Properties: lgbs.taxsales.com (seach Red River County, TX)”

Source: Red River County, Texas · verified 2026-11-02

Where do I bid - online or in person? Statewide

A tax sale must take place at the county courthouse, unless the commissioners court authorizes a public auction using online bidding and sale.

“The commissioners court of a county by official action may authorize the officer charged with selling property under this section to conduct a public auction using online bidding and sale. … Except as provided by Subsection (a-1) and this subsection, a sale of real property under this section must take place at the county courthouse in the county in which the land is located.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.01(a-1), (r) · verified 2026-11-02

How do I register to bid? Statewide

A commissioners court may require bidders to register with the county assessor-collector before the sale begins. The officer may not deliver a deed unless the purchaser shows an unexpired written statement issued by the county assessor-collector.

“A commissioners court may require that, to be eligible to bid at a sale of real property under this chapter, a person must be registered as a bidder with the county assessor-collector before the sale begins. … may not execute or deliver a deed to the purchaser of the property unless the purchaser exhibits to the officer an unexpired written statement issued under this section to the person by the county assessor-collector”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.011(b); 34.015(b) · verified 2026-11-02

What makes up the opening or minimum bid? Statewide

The officer calculates the total amount due under the judgment: taxes, penalties and interest, any other amount awarded, court costs and the costs of the sale. If no bid sufficient to pay the lesser of that amount or the adjudged value is received, the taxing unit that requested the order of sale may end the sale.

“The officer shall calculate the total amount due under the judgment, including all taxes, penalties, and interest, plus any other amount awarded by the judgment, court costs, and the costs of the sale. … If a bid sufficient to pay the lesser of the amount calculated under Subsection (b) or the adjudged value is not received, the taxing unit that requested the order of sale may terminate the sale.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.01(b), (j) · verified 2026-11-02

When and how must the winning bidder pay? Statewide

Under the Texas Rules of Civil Procedure for execution sales, a bidder who fails to comply with the terms of the sale is liable to pay the plaintiff twenty percent of the value of the property plus costs, and the officer sells the property again.

“If any person shall bid off property at any sale made by virtue of an execution, and shall fail to comply with the terms of the sale, he shall be liable to pay the plaintiff in execution twenty per cent on the value of the property thus bid off, besides costs … When the terms of the sale shall not be complied with by the bidder the levying officer shall proceed to sell the same property again on the same day, if there be sufficient time”

Source: Texas Rules of Civil Procedure · Tex. R. Civ. P. 652, 653 · verified 2026-11-02

What costs are added on top of the winning bid? Statewide

The Tax Code counts the costs of sale - advertising, an auctioneer's commission and fees, and anticipated deed recording fees - inside the total amount due that the officer calculates for the sale. Separately, the county assessor-collector may charge a fee of up to $10 for each written statement a purchaser requests to show that no delinquent taxes are owed.

“The costs of a sale include the costs of advertising, an auctioneer's commission and fees, and deed recording fees anticipated to be paid in connection with the sale of the property. ... To cover the costs associated with the issuance of statements under Subsection (c), a county assessor-collector may charge the person requesting a statement a fee not to exceed $10 for each statement requested.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.01(b); 34.015(j) · verified 2026-11-02

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive? Statewide

The deed vests good and perfect title in the purchaser to the defendant's interest in the property, including the right to use and possess it, subject to the defendant's right of redemption (and the recorded covenants and easements listed in the statute). It may be impeached only for fraud.

“The deed vests good and perfect title in the purchaser or the purchaser's assigns to the interest owned by the defendant in the property subject to the foreclosure, including the defendant's right to the use and possession of the property, subject only to the defendant's right of redemption … The deed may be impeached only for fraud.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.01(m), (n) · verified 2026-11-02

Can the former owner redeem after the sale? Statewide

Yes. For a residence homestead or land designated for agricultural use, the owner may redeem on or before the second anniversary of the date the purchaser's deed is filed, paying the amount bid and other statutory amounts plus a 25% premium in the first year or 50% in the second. For other property the right must be exercised within 180 days after the deed is filed.

“may redeem the property on or before the second anniversary of the date on which the purchaser's deed is filed for record by paying the purchaser the amount the purchaser bid for the property … plus a redemption premium of 25 percent of the aggregate total if the property is redeemed during the first year of the redemption period or 50 percent … may be exercised not later than the 180th day following the date on which the purchaser's or taxing unit's deed is filed for record”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.21(a), (e) · verified 2026-11-02

Which liens or interests survive the sale? Statewide

A sale to a purchaser other than a taxing unit extinguishes each lien securing the delinquent taxes, penalties and interest included in the judgment. Title remains subject to restrictive covenants recorded before January 1 of the year the tax lien arose, a recorded lien under such a covenant not extinguished in the judgment, and valid easements of record (34.01(n)).

“a recorded restrictive covenant running with the land that was recorded before January 1 of the year in which the tax lien on the property arose, a recorded lien that arose under that restrictive covenant that was not extinguished in the judgment foreclosing the tax lien, and each valid easement of record … extinguishes each lien securing payment of the delinquent taxes, penalties, and interest against that property and included in the judgment”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.01(n), (q) · verified 2026-11-02

Is the title insurable or does it need a quiet-title action? Statewide

The statute says the officer's deed may be impeached only for fraud. A person may not bring an action challenging the validity of the tax sale unless the person deposits into the court registry the delinquent taxes, penalties and interest specified in the judgment plus all costs of the sale, or files an affidavit of inability to pay under Rule 145.

“The deed may be impeached only for fraud. ... A person may not commence an action that challenges the validity of a tax sale under this chapter unless the person: (1) deposits into the registry of the court an amount equal to the amount of the delinquent taxes, penalties, and interest specified in the judgment of foreclosure obtained against the property plus all costs of the tax sale; or (2) files an affidavit of inability to pay under Rule 145, Texas Rules of Civil Procedure.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.01(n); 34.08(a) · verified 2026-11-02

What if the property is occupied? Statewide

The statute says the right of redemption does not give the former owner the right to use or possess the property, or to receive rents, income or other benefits from it, while the right of redemption exists.

“(h) The right of redemption does not grant or reserve in the former owner of the real property the right to the use or possession of the property, or to receive rents, income, or other benefits from the property while the right of redemption exists.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.21(h) · verified 2026-11-02

What happens to property that does not sell? Statewide

If no sufficient bid is received and the taxing unit does not end the sale, the officer bids the property off to the taxing unit that requested the order of sale, for the lesser of the judgment amount or the market value in the judgment. That taxing unit takes title for itself and the other taxing units in the suit.

“the officer making the sale shall bid the property off to the taxing unit that requested the order of sale … for the aggregate amount of the judgment against the property or for the market value of the property as specified in the judgment, whichever is less. … The taxing unit to which the property is bid off takes title to the property for the use and benefit of itself and all other taxing units that established tax liens in the suit.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.01(j), (k); 34.05(a) · verified 2026-11-02

What happens to money paid above what was owed (surplus)? Statewide

The officer pays any excess proceeds to the clerk of the court that issued the warrant or order of sale. The clerk keeps them for two years after the sale unless the court orders otherwise; a claim must be filed by petition in the court before the second anniversary of the sale, and the court orders the proceeds paid by a set order of priorities.

“shall pay any excess proceeds after payment of all amounts due all participants in the sale as specified by Subsection (b) to the clerk of the court issuing the warrant or order of sale. ... keep the excess proceeds paid into court as provided by Section 34.02 (d) for a period of two years after the date of the sale unless otherwise ordered by the court ... The petition must be filed before the second anniversary of the date of the sale of the property. ... the court shall order that the proceeds be paid according to the following priorities”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.02(d), 34.03(a)(2), 34.04(a), 34.04(c) · Clerk of the court that ordered the sale · verified 2026-11-02

Available

Property struck off to a taxing unit after no sufficient bid, which the unit may later resell under its own process.

Which office handles it and how do I contact them?

The Texas Comptroller's county directory lists the Red River County Tax Assessor-Collector, phone 903-427-3009, website www.co.red-river.tx.us.

“Tax Assessor/Collector ... Phone: 903-427-3009 ... Website: www.co.red-river.tx.us”

Source: Red River - 194 · verified 2026-11-02

What is this list and why is the property available? Statewide

These are properties sold to a taxing unit at a tax sale. Such a taxing unit may sell the property at any time by public or private sale, subject to any right of redemption of the former owner; the redemption period begins when the deed to the taxing unit is filed for record.

“If property is sold to a taxing unit that is a party to the judgment, the taxing unit may sell the property at any time by public or private sale. … The sale is subject to any right of redemption of the former owner. The redemption period begins on the date the deed to the taxing unit is filed for record.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.05(a); 34.01(j) · verified 2026-11-02

How do I buy a property from this list? Statewide

The taxing unit that bought the property may ask the sheriff or a constable to sell it at a public sale, or may instead sell it at a private sale.

“The taxing unit purchasing the property by resolution of its governing body may request the sheriff or a constable to sell the property at a public sale. … In lieu of a sale pursuant to Subsections (c) and (d) of this section, the taxing unit that purchased the property may sell the property at a private sale.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.05(c), (d), (h) · verified 2026-11-02

How is the price set? Statewide

A public resale requested by the taxing unit may be for any amount. A private sale under 34.05(h) may not be for less than the lesser of the market value in the judgment or the total judgments against the property.

“Property sold pursuant to Subsections (c) and (d) of this section may be sold for any amount. … Property sold under this subsection may not be sold for an amount that is less than the lesser of: (1) the market value specified in the judgment of foreclosure; or (2) the total amount of the judgments against the property.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.05(b), (h), (i) · verified 2026-11-02

What costs are added to the price? Statewide

For a private sale at or above market value under 34.05(j), the conveyance is subject to the purchaser's obligation to pay the prorated taxes for the current year.

“The conveyance is subject to any remaining right of redemption at the time of the sale and to the purchaser's obligation to pay the prorated taxes for the current year as provided by Section 26.10 .”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.05(k) · verified 2026-11-02

What kind of deed or title does the buyer receive? Statewide

The seller executes a deed conveying the right, title and interest held by each taxing unit that was a party to the judgment. The conveyance is subject to any remaining right of redemption.

“shall execute a deed to the property conveying to the purchaser the right, title, and interest acquired or held by each taxing unit that was a party to the judgment foreclosing tax liens on the property. The conveyance shall be made subject to any remaining right of redemption at the time of the sale.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.05(e) · verified 2026-11-02

How long does a property stay available? Statewide

The taxing unit may sell the property at any time. If it has not sold the property within six months after the owner's right of redemption ends, any taxing unit entitled to the proceeds may ask the sheriff or a constable in writing to sell it at a public sale.

“If the purchasing taxing unit has not sold the property within six months after the date on which the owner's right of redemption terminates, any taxing unit that is entitled to receive proceeds of the sale by resolution of its governing body may request the sheriff or a constable in writing to sell the property at a public sale.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.05(a), (c) · verified 2026-11-02

Which liens or interests stay with the property? Statewide

A resale by a taxing unit is subject to any remaining right of redemption of the former owner. A private sale at or above market value under Subsection (j) discharges and extinguishes all liens foreclosed by the judgment.

“The sale is subject to any right of redemption of the former owner. ... The conveyance shall be made subject to any remaining right of redemption at the time of the sale. ... A sale under Subsection (j) discharges and extinguishes all liens foreclosed by the judgment”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.05(a), (e), (k) · verified 2026-11-02

Is the title insurable or does it need a quiet-title action? Statewide

An action attacking the validity of a resale of property by a taxing unit may not be instituted after the expiration of one year after the date of the resale.

“An action attacking the validity of a resale of property pursuant to this section may not be instituted after the expiration of one year after the date of the resale.”

Source: TAX CODE CHAPTER 34. TAX SALES AND REDEMPTION · Tex. Tax Code 34.05(f) · verified 2026-11-02

Liens & Certificates

What is a tax lien or certificate here? Statewide

Texas does not sell tax lien certificates. The tax office sells no tax liens; the property itself is sold at the monthly tax foreclosure sale.

“The tax office does not sell tax liens. Texas law allows the public to purchase properties from the county at a monthly tax foreclosure sale. The states sells the deed to the property. Note: The state does not sell tax lien certificates where a buyer becomes the lienholder for the back taxes.”

Source: Foreclosed properties frequently asked questions · verified 2026-10-29
